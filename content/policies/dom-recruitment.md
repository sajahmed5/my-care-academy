## 1. Purpose

Our care workers go into clients' homes alone, often with a key safe code, and nobody is watching. Who we employ is the first safeguard we offer. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} recruits safely and fairly, the checks we make before anyone starts, and how we keep those checks up to date.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone we employ or engage directly, including care workers, office and on-call staff, managers, bank staff and self-employed contractors. Directors, the nominated individual and the registered manager are also checked under Fit and Proper Persons. Agency workers are checked by their agency (see Safe Staffing, Rotas and Agency Staff).

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 19: Fit and proper persons employed — everyone we employ is of good character, competent, and able after reasonable adjustments to do the work, and our recruitment procedures must make sure of it.
- Schedule 3 of the same Regulations — what we must hold about each person: proof of identity with a recent photograph; a criminal record check at the level the role needs, with barred list information where it applies; evidence of conduct in previous care work and of why it ended; relevant qualifications; a full employment history with a written explanation of any gaps; and information about any health condition relevant to the work. Section 6 follows it.
- Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006 — a barred person must not work in regulated activity with adults, and it is an offence for an employer knowingly to let them.
- Police Act 1997 and the Disclosure and Barring Service Code of Practice — checks only where the role is eligible, and the information used fairly, kept securely and not kept longer than needed.
- Rehabilitation of Offenders Act 1974 (Exceptions) Order 1975 — care work is excepted, so applicants must declare spent convictions and cautions, except protected ones that are filtered from DBS certificates.
- Equality Act 2010 — no discrimination, and reasonable adjustments for disabled applicants. Under section 60 we do not ask about health before offering the job, except for the narrow reasons the Act allows, such as adjustments for the interview.
- Immigration, Asylum and Nationality Act 2006 — the right to work check (see Right to Work and Sponsored Workers).
- CQC's Single Assessment Framework — the "Safe and effective staffing" quality statement, under the safe key question.

## 4. Our policy

- Nobody starts work, including shadowing and paid training, until every check below is complete, satisfactory and recorded.
- We recruit for values as well as experience: kindness, honesty and reliability.
- A criminal record does not automatically rule someone out. We consider each one fairly, against the role.

## 5. How we recruit

### 5.1 The job

Each post has a job description and a person specification setting out the duties and the values we look for. Adverts say that the post is subject to an enhanced DBS check, and, where the job needs it, a full driving licence and use of a vehicle.

### 5.2 Applications and shortlisting

Every applicant completes our application form, with a full employment history since leaving school, dated by month and year, and referees. A CV alone is not enough. Equality monitoring information is kept apart and not seen by those shortlisting. We shortlist against the person specification and ask each shortlisted applicant whether they need adjustments for the interview.

### 5.3 Interview

Interviews are held by at least two people, at least one trained in safer recruitment, in person or by video where needed. Every candidate is asked the same core questions, including:

- why they want to do care work, and questions about scenarios that test their values;
- how they would cope working alone, without a colleague to ask, in someone else's home;
- each gap in their employment history, until it is explained;
- why they left previous care work, if they have done it;
- whether there is any reason they should not work with adults at risk.

The interviewers check the candidate's photo ID and record their notes and scores. The candidate declares spent and unspent convictions, as the Exceptions Order allows, on a separate form seen only by the person making the decision.

### 5.4 Conditional offer

The successful candidate is offered the job in writing, conditional on every check listed in the letter being satisfactory. Only then do we ask about their health.

## 6. The checks

### 6.1 Identity and right to work

We check photo ID and proof of address, and check the right to work exactly as Right to Work and Sponsored Workers sets out. Since 1 October 2026 that includes self-employed contractors and other workers we engage. Care worker and senior care worker jobs can no longer be sponsored for people recruited from overseas; we sponsor someone for these jobs only as that policy allows.

### 6.2 Enhanced DBS check

Care workers and anyone else in regulated activity with adults have an enhanced DBS check with a check of the adults' barred list. Office staff and others whose roles are eligible have the level of check their role allows. Where someone has lived outside the UK as an adult, we also ask for a criminal record certificate or certificate of good conduct from that country, where one can be obtained.

If a certificate shows a conviction, caution or other information, the registered manager discusses it with the applicant before deciding, weighing its relevance and seriousness, how long ago it was, whether it was a one-off and what has changed since, and records the decision and reasons. Anyone on the adults' barred list is never employed in regulated activity.

### 6.3 DBS Update Service

An applicant on the DBS Update Service may let us check their existing certificate online instead of applying for a new one, if it is enhanced, for the adult workforce, and includes the adults' barred list. We ask all staff to join the Update Service, and recheck their status, with their consent, as often as Fit and Proper Persons sets.

### 6.4 References and conduct in care work

We ask for at least two references: one from the most recent employer, and one from the most recent work in health or social care or with children or vulnerable adults, if different. We ask each care employer about the person's conduct and why they left, and confirm by phone any reference that is unclear or comes from a personal email address. Open "to whom it may concern" letters are not accepted.

### 6.5 Employment history

Every gap in the history has a written explanation on file. An unexplained gap stops the process.

### 6.6 Health

After the offer, the applicant completes a health questionnaire, so that we can check they can do the essential parts of the job, such as moving and handling and working alone, and plan reasonable adjustments. With their consent, we ask occupational health where needed.

### 6.7 Qualifications

We see original certificates for qualifications the person relies on, such as a Diploma in Adult Care, and check any professional register directly.

### 6.8 Driving

Where the job needs driving, we check the licence with a DVLA check code, insurance that covers business use, and that the vehicle is taxed with a current MOT where it needs one, as Driving at Work sets out.

## 7. Starting work

When every check is complete, the registered manager signs off the file and confirms a start date. The new starter begins induction and probation, and does not work alone until signed off as ready (see Induction, Probation and the Care Certificate). Staff must tell us straight away if they are arrested, charged, cautioned or convicted, and confirm at each yearly appraisal that nothing has changed.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — is responsible for safe recruitment, decides on any DBS information, signs off each file before a start date, and makes sure every file meets Schedule 3.
- **Nominated individual** — checks a sample of staff files as part of our governance.
- **Interviewers** — follow this policy, interview fairly and consistently, and record their reasons.
- **Office staff who carry out checks** — complete and record each check, and raise anything unclear with the registered manager.

## 9. Training

At least one person on every interview panel has completed safer recruitment training and refreshes it every three years. Staff who carry out right to work and DBS checks are trained before they do so. Everyone involved in recruitment completes equality and diversity training.

## 10. Records

The application, interview notes, offer, Schedule 3 evidence and sign-off are kept on the person's file on the site, ready to show CQC. For a DBS certificate we record its number, date, level, the barred list checked and our decision, and do not keep a copy longer than the DBS Code of Practice allows. Files are kept for six years after employment ends. Papers about applicants we do not employ are kept for six months after the decision and then destroyed securely.
