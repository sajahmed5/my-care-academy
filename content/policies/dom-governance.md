## 1. Purpose

Good care depends on knowing, every week, whether care is being given safely and well, and acting quickly when it is not. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} checks the quality and safety of its service, who is responsible for what, how information reaches the people who must act on it, and how we keep improving.

## 2. Scope

It applies to the directors, the nominated individual, the registered manager, office and on-call staff, seniors who carry out checks, and every care worker. It covers every part of the service: care, staffing, records, the office and the business.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 17: Good governance — systems to assess, monitor and improve quality and safety; to assess, monitor and reduce risks; to keep accurate records for each client, for staff and for the running of the service; and to seek and act on feedback. CQC can ask for a written report on this within 28 days.
- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, Regulation 12: Statement of purpose — a written statement of our aims, the services we provide and who we provide them for, kept under review, with any revision sent to CQC within 28 days.
- Regulation 20A of the 2014 Regulations: Requirement as to display of performance assessments — our CQC rating displayed at our office and on our website, within 21 days of its publication.
- Regulations 5, 6, 7 and 19 of the 2014 Regulations — directors, the nominated individual, the registered manager and staff are fit and proper (see Fit and Proper Persons).
- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, Regulations 14, 16 and 18 — telling CQC of the registered manager's absence, of deaths, and of other incidents.
- CQC's Single Assessment Framework, the well-led key question — its quality statements look for a shared direction and open culture, capable leaders, staff free to speak up, sound governance, management and sustainability, partnership working, and continual learning and improvement.
- Data Security and Protection Toolkit — the yearly self-assessment care providers are expected to complete (see Data Security and Breaches).

## 4. Our policy

- Quality is checked in everyday work, not once a year before an inspection.
- Every check has a frequency, an owner and a record. What a check finds is acted on, and the action is checked.
- Information flows upwards: from care workers to the office, from the office to the registered manager, and from the registered manager to the nominated individual and the directors.
- We ask clients, families and staff what they think, and show them what we changed.
- We are open with CQC, commissioners and the people we support when things go wrong.

## 5. How we are organised

{{nominated_individual}} supervises the management of the regulated activity on behalf of the company, and reports to the directors. {{registered_manager}} manages the service day to day and is accountable to the nominated individual. Office staff, coordinators and seniors report to the registered manager. Each care worker has a named supervisor.

## 6. How we check quality

### 6.1 Visits, rotas and on-call

- Call monitoring — the office watches visits as they happen and acts on any that have not started within their window, as Missed and Late Visits sets out. Each week the registered manager or a coordinator reviews punctuality, visit length against planned, and visits not logged electronically.
- Missed and late visits — each one is recorded through the site's concerns process and reviewed every month for patterns.
- Rotas — reviewed every month for realistic travel time between visits, continuity of care workers, two-person visits covered by two trained workers, and hours that respect rest and breaks (see Safe Staffing, Rotas and Agency Staff).
- On-call — the office reads the on-call record on the next working day and follows up anything left open.

### 6.2 Care, medicines and records

- Medicines administration records — checked every month for gaps, errors and refusals not followed up.
- Daily notes and care records — a sample checked every month, and the care plan and risk assessments reviewed for each client at least every year and whenever needs change.
- Clients' money — shopping records checked as Handling Clients' Money sets out.
- Restrictive practice and capacity — care plans checked for restrictions, best interests decisions and any Court of Protection authorisation, as Deprivation of Liberty sets out.

### 6.3 Staff

The site holds each person's file, so the registered manager can see at any time who is due or overdue for training on the training matrix, supervision every three months, appraisal every year, spot checks every 12 weeks, competency assessments every year, and policies to accept. Driving licence, insurance and MOT checks are renewed every year for those who drive for work, and a sample of staff files is checked every three months for complete recruitment records.

### 6.4 Incidents, complaints and safeguarding

Accidents, incidents, near misses, complaints, safeguarding concerns and duty of candour cases go through the site's concerns process. The registered manager reviews them together every month, looking for patterns, and checks that every notification to CQC, the local authority and commissioners was made.

### 6.5 Feedback

- Clients and families — asked about their care at every review, at spot checks, and by a telephone quality check from the office at least every six months. A written survey is sent to every client at least once a year.
- Staff — asked at supervision and team meetings, and through a yearly staff survey. Staff can raise concerns at any time, including under Whistleblowing.
- Professionals and commissioners — their feedback, and the results of contract monitoring, are recorded and acted on.

## 7. The monthly report and the governance meeting

Each month {{registered_manager}} sends {{nominated_individual}} a short quality report: hours delivered against those planned, missed and late visits, staffing, sickness and agency use, training, supervision and spot checks overdue, incidents, complaints and compliments, safeguarding concerns, notifications made, and progress on the improvement plan.

The registered manager and the nominated individual hold a governance meeting at least every three months, and the directors attend or receive its notes. It reviews the monthly reports and trends, the risk register, the improvement plan, policies due for review, the statement of purpose, the Data Security and Protection Toolkit, and anything CQC or commissioners have raised. Actions are minuted, each with a named person and a date.

## 8. The risk register and the improvement plan

The business risk register is reviewed at the governance meeting, and so at least every three months, as Risk Management sets out, and sooner whenever a new risk arises.

Everything our checks, feedback, inspections and contract monitoring show needs to change goes onto one improvement plan, with a named person and date for each action. The registered manager closes an action only once it is done and has worked.

## 9. Our duties to CQC

- The statement of purpose is reviewed at least every year and whenever the service changes, and CQC is sent any revision within 28 days.
- Our current CQC rating is displayed at the office where people will see it, and on our website, within 21 days of its publication.
- Notifications, including of the registered manager's absence, are made as Reportable Incidents and Registered Manager Absence and Deputising set out.
- Directors, the nominated individual and the registered manager make a yearly fit and proper persons declaration.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Directors** — are accountable for the quality and safety of the service, provide the resources it needs, and act on what the governance meeting tells them.
- **Nominated individual** — supervises the management of the service, holds the registered manager to account, reads the monthly report, chairs the governance meeting, visits the office and talks to staff and clients, and makes sure that CQC is told what it must be.
- **Registered manager** — runs the checks in this policy, writes the monthly report, keeps the improvement plan, and acts on what is found.
- **Office staff and seniors** — carry out the checks given to them, record them, and report what they find.
- **Care workers** — keep accurate records, log every visit honestly, report incidents and concerns, and tell us how we could do better.

## 11. Training

The registered manager and nominated individual keep up to date with CQC's framework and the regulations. Staff who carry out audits and spot checks are trained to do so. All staff learn at induction why records, reporting and feedback matter.

## 12. Records

Audits, monthly reports, minutes, the risk register, the improvement plan, surveys and their results are kept by the registered manager for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention, and shown to CQC and commissioners on request.
