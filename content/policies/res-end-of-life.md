## 1. Purpose

For many residents, the home is where they will spend the last part of their lives, and many will want to die here rather than in hospital. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} plans end of life care with residents and their families, respects decisions made in advance, cares for residents who are dying, and what staff do when a resident dies, expectedly or not.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works in the home, including night staff, agency staff and the registered manager, and to the care of every resident approaching the end of life.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — advance decisions to refuse treatment, lasting powers of attorney for health and welfare, and best interests decisions.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 9: Person-centred care, Regulation 9A: Visiting and accompanying in care homes, Regulation 10: Dignity and respect, and Regulation 11: Need for consent.
- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, Regulation 16 — notifying CQC of the death of a resident.
- Coroners and Justice Act 2009 — deaths that must be referred to the coroner, and the medical examiner system that reviews other deaths.
- NICE guideline NG31: Care of dying adults in the last days of life, and NICE guideline NG142: End of life care for adults: service delivery.
- Resuscitation Council UK guidance on decisions about cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) and on the ReSPECT process.

## 4. Our policy

- Residents who wish to die in the home are supported to do so wherever their needs can be met here, with the GP and community nurses.
- We plan end of life care with the resident, and with their family if the resident wishes, well before it is needed.
- We follow valid advance decisions and respect residents' wishes about treatment and about hospital.
- Families can be with a resident who is dying at any time of day or night.
- Staff never decide on their own that a resident should not be resuscitated. That is a clinical decision made by a doctor or senior clinician.
- We look after residents' bodies, belongings and families with dignity after death, and support the other residents and our staff.

## 5. Planning ahead

Soon after admission, and again when a resident's health changes, the registered manager or a senior offers the resident a conversation about their wishes. With their consent, the family and GP are involved. The care plan records:

- where the resident wants to be cared for and to die, and whether they want to go to hospital if they become seriously ill;
- their wishes for comfort, faith, culture, music, and who they want with them;
- any lasting power of attorney for health and welfare, and who the attorney is;
- any advance decision to refuse treatment, and where it is kept;
- any DNACPR decision or ReSPECT form, and where the original is kept;
- who to call, and when, including at night.

Where a resident lacks capacity to plan, decisions are made in their best interests with their attorney, family and GP, following our Mental Capacity, Best Interests and Advocacy policy.

### 5.1 Advance decisions to refuse treatment

An advance decision lets a person aged 18 or over, while they have capacity, refuse specific treatment for a time when they lack capacity. If it refuses life-sustaining treatment it must be in writing, signed and witnessed, and say it applies even if their life is at risk. A valid advance decision that applies to the situation must be followed. A copy is kept on the care record and goes with the resident to hospital.

### 5.2 DNACPR and ReSPECT

A DNACPR (do not attempt cardiopulmonary resuscitation) decision is made by a doctor or senior clinician, with the resident or, if they lack capacity, with those close to them. A ReSPECT form (Recommended Summary Plan for Emergency Care and Treatment) records the resident's wishes and clinical recommendations for emergencies. The original is kept at the front of the resident's care file so that it can be found at once, and its existence is passed on at every handover. It applies only to CPR, and the resident receives all other care. The shift leader checks at the start of every shift which residents have one.

## 6. Caring for a resident who is dying

- The shift leader tells the GP or community nurse when a resident appears to be in the last days of life, so that they can review the resident and prescribe anticipatory ("just in case") medicines. These are given by the community nurse, as the home does not provide nursing.
- Keep the resident clean, comfortable, and as free from pain and distress as possible. Report pain, breathlessness, agitation or noisy breathing to the shift leader, who calls the community nurse.
- Offer drinks and mouth care often; do not force food or fluid.
- Reposition as planned and care for the resident's skin.
- Check on the resident often, including through the night. Where they wish it and staffing allows, someone stays with them.
- Welcome family at any time. Offer them drinks, a place to rest, and the option of staying overnight where it can be arranged.

## 7. When a resident dies

### 7.1 An expected death

- Note the time, and tell the shift leader.
- The shift leader contacts the GP or out-of-hours service, or the community nurse, to verify the death, following the local arrangements for verification of expected death. Care staff do not verify death unless they have been trained and authorised under a local protocol.
- If family are not present, the shift leader tells the next of kin, in the way they asked to be told, at any hour unless they asked to wait until morning.
- After verification, and following the resident's and family's wishes and any religious or cultural practice, staff wash and lay out the resident with care. Leave medical devices in place unless told otherwise.
- The family choose a funeral director. Give the family time with the resident before the funeral director comes.

### 7.2 An unexpected death

- Call 999 and follow the call handler's instructions, including about CPR. Start CPR unless there is a valid DNACPR decision or ReSPECT recommendation against it, or the call handler tells you otherwise.
- Do not move the resident or anything around them unless told to, and keep other residents away from the room.
- Tell the registered manager or on-call manager straight away.

An unexpected death is reported to the coroner, and the police usually attend on the coroner's behalf. This is routine. Co-operate fully.

### 7.3 After any death

- Write a full account on the resident's record: the time, who was present, who was contacted, and what was done.
- Keep the resident's medicines separate and do not return or destroy them until the period set out in our Medicines Management policy has passed, in case the coroner needs them.
- Lock the resident's room or keep their belongings safe, and list their property against the admission inventory. Belongings and any money we hold are returned to the executor or the person dealing with the estate, as our Residents' Money and Property policy sets out.
- Tell other residents sensitively.

## 8. What the registered manager does

- Notifies the Care Quality Commission of the death without delay, as Regulation 16 requires.
- Tells the local authority or NHS commissioner, and the Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards supervisory body if the resident had an authorisation.
- Raises a safeguarding concern, and records it through the site's concerns process, if there is any concern that neglect, abuse or our care contributed to the death.
- Keeps the care records secure and makes them available to the medical examiner or coroner.
- Contacts the family to offer condolences and bereavement support, and agrees with them when the room will be cleared.
- Offers a debrief and support to the staff involved.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure every resident has the chance to plan ahead, works with the GP and community nurses, notifies CQC and commissioners, and supports families and staff.
- **Shift leaders** — know which residents have advance decisions and DNACPR or ReSPECT forms, call health professionals when a resident deteriorates or dies, and lead the response.
- **Care staff** — give compassionate care, follow each resident's plan, and follow this policy when a resident dies.

## 10. Training

All care staff complete end of life care and basic life support training during induction, and refresh them every year. Shift leaders are trained in recognising the last days of life, and in what to do when a resident dies, including at night.

## 11. Records

Advance care plans, advance decisions, DNACPR and ReSPECT information, the record of a death, and the property inventory are kept on the resident's care record for as long as our record retention schedule requires (see Record Keeping and Retention). CQC notifications are kept through the concerns process.
