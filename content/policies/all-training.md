## 1. Purpose

The people we support are only as safe as the skills of the staff who care for them. This policy sets out what training {{organisation}} requires, how often it is refreshed, how we check that learning turns into good practice, and how we help staff grow in their careers.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us: care staff, office staff, managers and directors. It covers e-learning, face-to-face and practical training, competency assessment, qualifications and other development.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 18: Staffing — staff receive the training, learning and development they need to carry out their role, and are supported to gain further qualifications.
- Health and Care Act 2022 — registered providers must make sure their staff receive training on learning disability and autism, appropriate to their role. The Oliver McGowan Mandatory Training on Learning Disability and Autism is the government's preferred and recommended training for this.
- Skills for Care guidance on core and mandatory training in adult social care — the subjects care staff need and how often to refresh them.
- Skills for Care, the Care Certificate — the standards for those new to care (see Induction, Probation and the Care Certificate).
- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974, Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992 and Health and Safety (First-Aid) Regulations 1981 — information, instruction and training to work safely.
- National Minimum Wage Regulations 2015 — time spent on training we require is working time, and is paid.

## 4. Our policy

- Every role has a set of required courses, recorded on the training matrix on the site.
- Nobody does a task they have not been trained in and, where the task needs it, assessed as competent to do.
- Required training is done in paid time and at no cost to the employee.
- Training is refreshed before it expires.
- Training is checked in practice: a certificate shows someone has learned, a spot check or competency assessment shows they can do it.
- Everyone has the chance to develop, through qualifications and further learning agreed at appraisal.

## 5. What we require

### 5.1 Core training

For care staff, the core training follows Skills for Care's guidance and includes:

- safeguarding adults, and safeguarding children;
- the Mental Capacity Act and, in care homes, the Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards;
- moving and handling people, with a practical session;
- infection prevention and control;
- health and safety, fire safety and, in homecare, lone working;
- basic life support and first aid;
- food hygiene, and nutrition and hydration;
- medication, for anyone who gives or helps with medicines;
- equality and diversity, dignity and person-centred care;
- data protection and confidentiality;
- learning disability and autism, through the Oliver McGowan Mandatory Training at the tier right for the role.

Office staff and managers complete the courses the company sets for their roles, which always include safeguarding, data protection, health and safety, fire safety and learning disability and autism.

### 5.2 Specialist training

Staff who support people with particular needs receive training in those needs before they do so — for example dementia, end of life care, epilepsy and rescue medication, diabetes, catheter and stoma care, percutaneous feeding tubes, or positive behaviour support. Clinical tasks delegated by a health professional are taught and signed off by that professional, as our Delegated Healthcare Tasks policy sets out.

### 5.3 Refreshers

Each course on the matrix has a refresh period set by the company, in line with Skills for Care's guidance and the course provider's advice. Practical subjects such as moving and handling and basic life support are refreshed more often than knowledge subjects. When a certificate is near its expiry date, the site reminds the employee and their manager; an expired certificate shows as overdue on the matrix.

If required training expires, the manager agrees a date to complete it. Where the training is needed to do a task safely — moving and handling, medication — the employee does not do that task until it is renewed.

## 6. How we check learning

- Competency assessments are held for practical skills such as medication, hoisting, moving and handling, infection control and personal care, before someone does the task alone and then every year. They are recorded on the person's file.
- Spot checks of care staff at work look at whether training shows in practice.
- Supervisions ask what training has helped and what more is needed.
- When a spot check, competency assessment or incident shows a gap, the manager decides what training is needed and assigns it. Training is never a punishment; it is support.

## 7. Qualifications and development

We encourage care staff to work towards recognised qualifications, such as the Level 2 and Level 3 Diplomas in Adult Care, and senior staff and managers towards higher qualifications in leadership and management. Development goals are agreed at appraisal and reviewed at supervision. We look for funding available to care providers to support this.

> State any arrangement you have for repaying the cost of a qualification if someone leaves soon after, or remove this. Required training is never repayable.

## 8. Training providers and trainers

Training is delivered by competent trainers, using up-to-date content. Practical training is delivered face to face by a trainer qualified in the subject. We check external providers before using them, and record all training — e-learning, face to face and external — on the training matrix, so there is one complete record.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — decides what training each role requires and how often it is refreshed, keeps the matrix up to date, and acts on overdue training.
- **Managers and seniors** — check competence, identify training needs at supervision, spot check and appraisal, and release staff to attend.
- **Employees** — complete required training on time, put it into practice, and tell their manager when they need more.

## 10. Records

The training matrix holds each person's courses, certificates, expiry dates and competency assessments. A person's training record is kept for six years after they leave, and a copy of their record and certificates is given to them when they go.
