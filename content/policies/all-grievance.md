## 1. Purpose

Anyone who works for {{organisation}} should be able to raise a concern or complaint about their work, their colleagues or the way they are treated, and have it dealt with fairly and quickly. This policy sets out how to do that and what will happen next.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all employees. A concern about the safety or welfare of the people we support, or about wrongdoing in the company, is not a grievance — raise it under our Safeguarding or Whistleblowing policy, which protect you in other ways. A complaint about bullying, harassment or discrimination can be raised under this policy or under Bullying and Harassment.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Employment Relations Act 1999, section 10 — the right to be accompanied at a grievance meeting.
- Acas Code of Practice on Disciplinary and Grievance Procedures — the standard an employment tribunal judges us against.
- Equality Act 2010 — protection from discrimination, harassment and victimisation, including for raising a complaint.

## 4. Our policy

- Try to sort it out informally first. Many problems are solved by talking to your line manager or supervisor. You do not have to do this if it would not be appropriate, for example if your grievance is about them.
- A formal grievance is taken seriously, looked into, and answered in writing.
- You may bring a companion — a colleague or a trade union representative — to any grievance meeting, and to an appeal.
- Nobody will be treated less favourably for raising a grievance in good faith.
- A grievance about a manager is handled by a different manager, and the manager it is about does not see the case.
- Grievances are confidential and are shared only with those who need to know to deal with them.

## 5. The procedure

### 5.1 Raising a grievance

Write down what the problem is, what has happened, and what you would like done about it. Send it to your line manager, or to {{registered_manager}} if it is about your line manager, or raise it from your own login on the site. If you need help putting it in writing, ask.

### 5.2 Acknowledgement

We acknowledge your grievance in writing within five working days and tell you who will deal with it.

### 5.3 The grievance meeting

We invite you to a meeting, normally within ten working days of receiving your grievance, and tell you of your right to be accompanied. At the meeting you explain your grievance and how you think it should be settled. Notes are taken and shared with you. If your companion cannot attend, you may suggest another time within five working days of the original date.

### 5.4 Investigation

If the facts need to be checked, the manager looks into it — speaking to others involved and reviewing records — before deciding. They tell you if this will take longer than expected.

### 5.5 The outcome

The manager writes to you, normally within five working days of the meeting or the end of the investigation, saying whether your grievance is upheld, partly upheld or not upheld, the reasons, any action we will take, and how to appeal.

### 5.6 Appeal

If you are not satisfied, you may appeal in writing within five working days of the outcome letter, saying why. A different, preferably more senior, manager hears the appeal, and you may be accompanied. Their decision is confirmed in writing and is final.

## 6. Grievances during a disciplinary case

If you raise a grievance during a disciplinary process, the disciplinary may be paused briefly to deal with it. Where the two are related, they may be dealt with together.

## 7. Collective grievances

Where several employees share the same grievance, it may be dealt with together, with a representative speaking for the group.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure grievances are dealt with promptly and by someone independent of the issue, and hears or appoints appeal managers.
- **Managers** — try to settle problems early, hold meetings, investigate fairly and keep to the timescales.
- **Employees** — raise problems early, explain what they would like done, and attend meetings.

## 9. Records

Grievances, notes, letters and outcomes are kept confidentially in the HR pack. You can read the letters sent to you; investigation notes and witness statements are not shared beyond what you need to understand the decision. Records are kept for six years after employment ends.
