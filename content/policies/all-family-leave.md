## 1. Purpose

This policy explains the leave {{organisation}} gives employees when they have a baby, adopt or foster a child, lose a child, care for a dependant, or face a family emergency. It describes each kind of leave, how to ask for it, and how we support you before, during and after it.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all employees. Some kinds of leave and pay depend on how long you have worked for us, how much you earn, or the notice you give. Those qualifying conditions are set by law and change from time to time, so we apply the rules in force when you ask. Your manager or the office will check your entitlement with you.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Employment Rights Act 1996 and the regulations made under it — the rights to maternity, paternity, adoption, shared parental, parental bereavement, neonatal care and unpaid parental leave, to time off for dependants and for antenatal care, and not to be treated unfairly for using them.
- Neonatal Care (Leave and Pay) Act 2023 — leave for parents whose baby needs neonatal care.
- Carer's Leave Act 2023 and Carer's Leave Regulations 2024 — leave to care for a dependant with a long-term care need.
- Protection from Redundancy (Pregnancy and Family Leave) Act 2023 — extra protection in a redundancy situation during and after pregnancy and family leave.
- Social Security Contributions and Benefits Act 1992 — statutory maternity, paternity, adoption, shared parental, parental bereavement and neonatal care pay, at the rates set each year.
- Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — a risk assessment for new and expectant mothers.
- Equality Act 2010 — protection from pregnancy and maternity discrimination.

## 4. Our policy

- You will not be treated less favourably for being pregnant, for asking for or taking family leave, or for having caring responsibilities.
- During statutory family leave your terms and conditions continue, apart from pay, and you continue to build up annual leave.
- You have the right to return to your job, or in some circumstances a suitable alternative.
- Statutory pay is paid to those who qualify, at the rates in force at the time.

> State any enhanced pay you offer above statutory pay, and its conditions, or say that you pay statutory pay only.

## 5. The kinds of leave

### 5.1 Pregnancy and maternity

Tell your manager as soon as you are ready, so that we can assess any risks to you and your baby in your work — for example moving and handling, infections, or night work — and change your duties if needed. You must tell us formally, by the time the law sets before the baby is due, that you are pregnant, when the baby is due, and when you want your leave to start, and give us your maternity certificate.

You may take paid time off for antenatal appointments. Your partner may take unpaid time off to go with you to some of them.

Maternity leave can be up to 52 weeks. You must take at least the first two weeks after the birth. You may change your return date by giving the notice the law requires.

### 5.2 Adoption and surrogacy

Adoption leave works in a similar way to maternity leave, for the main adopter, and is also available to intended parents through surrogacy who apply for a parental order. You may take time off for appointments before the child is placed with you.

### 5.3 Paternity leave

The father, or the partner of the mother or main adopter, may take paternity leave of one or two weeks, together or as two separate weeks, within the first year after the birth or placement.

### 5.4 Shared parental leave

Parents may share leave and pay in the first year, by ending maternity or adoption leave early. Shared parental leave can be taken in blocks, by one parent or both. Ask the office for the forms; the rules on notice are detailed.

### 5.5 Neonatal care leave

If your baby is admitted to neonatal care soon after birth and stays for an extended time, you may take neonatal care leave of up to 12 weeks, on top of your other leave, as the law sets out.

### 5.6 Parental bereavement leave

If your child dies, or you have a stillbirth after 24 weeks of pregnancy, you may take two weeks' parental bereavement leave, together or as two separate weeks, within the period the law sets after the death. Tell us as soon as you can; you do not need to give notice for leave taken straight away. We will also give you compassionate support beyond what the law requires.

### 5.7 Unpaid parental leave

Parents may take unpaid parental leave to care for a child — for example to spend more time with them, or to settle them into a new school. It is normally taken in blocks of a week, up to the limits the law sets. We may postpone it for a short time if your absence would seriously disrupt the care of the people we support, and we will tell you why in writing.

### 5.8 Carer's leave

If someone who depends on you has a long-term care need, you may take up to one week of unpaid carer's leave in each 12 months, in half or whole days or a full week. Give us notice of twice the length of the leave, or three days if longer. We may postpone it only if the care of the people we support would be seriously disrupted, and will agree another date with you.

### 5.9 Time off for dependants

You may take a reasonable amount of unpaid time off to deal with an emergency involving a dependant — for example if they fall ill or are injured, care arrangements break down, or there is an incident at school. Tell us as soon as you can, why you are away and how long you expect to be. This is for dealing with the emergency and arranging care, not for longer-term care; for that, talk to us about other leave.

## 6. Keeping in touch and returning

During maternity, adoption or shared parental leave you may work a small number of paid keeping-in-touch days, if we both agree. You do not have to, and we cannot require it. We will keep you informed of job vacancies, training and changes at work.

When you return, we will talk about any support you need. If you are breastfeeding, we will provide somewhere suitable to rest and express milk. If you want to change your hours or pattern, you may ask under our Flexible Working policy.

## 7. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure employees get the leave and pay they are entitled to, that risk assessments for new and expectant mothers are done, and that returns are planned.
- **Office staff** — record leave in the HR pack, check entitlement, notify payroll and arrange cover.
- **Employees** — give the notice and evidence the law requires, and keep us informed.

## 8. Records

Family leave is recorded in the HR pack, with notices, certificates, risk assessments and pay records. Records are kept for six years.
