## 1. Purpose

{{organisation}} holds a great deal of personal information about the people we support, their families and our staff, much of it about their health. This policy sets out how we handle it lawfully, keep it safe, and respect people's rights over it — including their right to see what we hold.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us, and to all personal information we hold in any form: on the site and other systems, on paper, on phones, in emails and messages.

## 3. The law and guidance

- UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR), as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 — the principles for handling personal data, the lawful bases for using it, and people's rights.
- Data Protection Act 2018 — the UK's additional rules, including the conditions for health and other special category data.
- Common law duty of confidentiality — information given in confidence is not shared without consent or another lawful reason.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 17: Good governance — records are accurate, complete, secure and kept as long as needed.
- Information Commissioner's Office guidance; the Data Security and Protection Toolkit, which care providers are expected to complete each year.

## 4. The principles we follow

Personal information is:

- used lawfully, fairly and openly, and people are told how we use it in our privacy notices;
- collected for clear purposes and not used for anything unrelated;
- limited to what we need;
- accurate and kept up to date;
- kept no longer than necessary;
- kept secure;

and we can show that we follow these principles.

## 5. Why we may use personal information

We use information about the people we support to provide and plan their care — a lawful basis under UK GDPR — and their health information because it is needed for health and social care. We use staff information to employ them. We seek consent only where no other basis applies, for example to use a photograph in a newsletter. Using information lawfully is not the same as sharing it freely: see our Confidentiality policy for when information may be shared.

## 6. What to do

### 6.1 Every day

- Only look at information you need for your work.
- Keep your login details to yourself and lock your phone.
- Do not keep personal information on your own devices, in personal email or in personal messaging groups, except where the company has approved it.
- Do not talk about the people we support where you can be overheard.
- Keep paper records in the person's home or our office, as their care plan says, and never leave them where others can read them.

### 6.2 If something goes wrong

A data breach is any loss of, unauthorised access to, or disclosure of personal information — a lost phone, a message sent to the wrong person, a care plan left on a bus. Tell {{registered_manager}} straight away, even if you are not sure. They assess the risk, contain it, record it, and decide whether it must be reported to the Information Commissioner's Office within 72 hours of our becoming aware of it, and whether the people affected must be told. See Data Security and Breaches.

## 7. People's rights

Anyone we hold information about has rights, including to:

- be told how their information is used;
- see a copy of it (a subject access request);
- have mistakes corrected;
- have information deleted or its use restricted, in some circumstances;
- object to some uses of it.

### 7.1 Subject access requests

A request to see personal information can be made in any way — in writing, by email, by phone or in conversation — and does not have to mention the law. Pass any request to {{registered_manager}} on the day you receive it.

We check the identity of the person asking, and, where someone asks on another's behalf, their authority to do so: for example, a lasting power of attorney, or the person's written consent. We reply within one month of receiving the request, which may be extended by up to two further months for complex requests, in which case we tell the person within the first month. We provide the information free of charge, in a clear form. We may withhold information that would identify someone else, or where the law allows, for example where disclosure would cause serious harm; we explain any withholding. Requests about a person who has died are dealt with under the Access to Health Records Act 1990 where it applies.

### 7.2 Requests from others

Requests from the police, the local authority, solicitors or other organisations are passed to the registered manager, who checks the legal basis before sharing anything and records what was shared and why.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — is responsible for data protection day to day, handles requests and breaches, keeps our records of processing and privacy notices up to date, and completes the Data Security and Protection Toolkit.
- **Data Protection Officer** — where we are required to appoint one, or choose to, advises on our compliance.
- **All staff** — follow this policy, complete training, and report breaches and requests at once.

## 9. Training

All staff complete data protection and information governance training during induction and refresh it every year.

## 10. Records

We keep a record of what personal information we process and why, a log of subject access requests and breaches, and the information itself for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
