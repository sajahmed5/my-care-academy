## 1. Purpose

Care work needs a clear head. A care worker who is affected by alcohol or drugs can make mistakes with medicines, moving and handling or driving that harm the people we support, themselves or others. This policy sets out what {{organisation}} expects, and how we support staff who have a problem with alcohol or drugs.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all staff, including agency workers, whenever they are working, on call, driving for work, or wearing uniform. It covers alcohol, illegal drugs, psychoactive substances, and prescribed or over-the-counter medicines that may affect someone's ability to work safely.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 — our duty to protect employees and others from risks at work, and employees' duty to take care of themselves and others.
- Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — the duty to assess risks.
- Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 — the possession and supply of controlled drugs, and the offence of knowingly allowing it on premises we control.
- Road Traffic Act 1988 — the offences of driving while unfit through drink or drugs, over the drink-drive limit, or with certain drugs in the blood, including some prescribed medicines.
- Equality Act 2010 — addiction to alcohol or illegal drugs is not itself a disability, but conditions caused by it may be.
- Health and Safety Executive guidance on drug and alcohol misuse at work.

## 4. Our policy

- You must not report for work, or work, while affected by alcohol or drugs. This includes the effects of alcohol drunk the night before.
- You must not drink alcohol, or take illegal drugs or psychoactive substances, while working, on call, or in uniform.
- You must not bring illegal drugs to work, or keep, share or sell them at work.
- You must never take, or use for yourself, any medicine belonging to a person we support. This is theft, and is gross misconduct.
- If you think you have a problem with alcohol or drugs, we want you to tell us, and we will support you.

## 5. What to do

### 5.1 Medicines

Some prescribed and over-the-counter medicines cause drowsiness, slow reactions or affect driving. If you are taking one, ask your pharmacist or doctor whether it affects your work or driving, and tell your manager in confidence if it does. You do not need to tell us your diagnosis. Your manager may change your duties for a while — for example, no driving, or no moving and handling — to keep you and others safe.

### 5.2 If you are not fit to work

If you have been drinking, or have taken anything that may affect you, do not come to work. Phone the office or on-call and say you are unfit to work. We would much rather cover your work than have you working unsafely.

### 5.3 If you think a colleague is affected

If you think a colleague is affected by alcohol or drugs at work — they smell of alcohol, their speech is slurred, they are unsteady, or they are behaving out of character — tell the office, on-call or the person in charge straight away. Do not let them give care, give medicines or drive if you can safely prevent it. Other signs, such as illness, a diabetic emergency or a head injury, can look similar, so do not assume; report what you see.

### 5.4 What the manager does

The manager:

- speaks to the employee in private, with a witness where possible, and asks how they are;
- if there is a reasonable belief that the employee is not fit to work, removes them from duty and arranges cover;
- makes sure the employee gets home safely — they must not drive; the manager arranges a lift or a taxi, and contacts their emergency contact if needed;
- if the employee seems unwell, gets medical help;
- checks the people the employee has cared for that day, and any medicines they gave or records they wrote;
- records what was seen and done, and the times.

The manager then follows our Disciplinary policy, which includes an investigation before any decision. Working under the influence of alcohol or drugs is gross misconduct. Possession or supply of illegal drugs at work, or taking medicines belonging to a person we support, may also be reported to the police, and is a safeguarding matter.

### 5.5 Testing

We do not routinely test for alcohol or drugs. We test only where the employee's contract allows it, for a clear reason, with the employee's consent, and in private. Refusing a test without a good reason may be taken into account.

> If you do not have a testing clause in your contracts, replace the paragraph above with "We do not test for alcohol or drugs."

## 6. Getting help

Dependency on alcohol or drugs is a health problem, and we treat it as one. An employee who tells us they have a problem before it has caused an incident is supported to get help — from their GP, local alcohol and drug services, or any employee assistance service we provide. We may agree time off for treatment, changes to their duties, and a plan to return to their full role. We will treat what they tell us in confidence, sharing it only with those who need to know.

Asking for help does not protect someone from the consequences of misconduct that has already happened, and if the problem continues to affect their work it may be dealt with under the capability part of our Disciplinary policy. But we will always take into account that someone has sought help.

## 7. Work events

At any work social event, alcohol is drunk responsibly. Nobody drives to or from an event, or goes on to work or on call, after drinking.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure this policy is followed, decides on testing and action, and supports staff who seek help.
- **Office, on-call and shift leaders** — act straight away when someone may be unfit to work, keeping the people we support safe.
- **All staff** — come to work fit, tell us about medicines that may affect their work, and report concerns about colleagues.

## 9. Training

This policy is covered at induction. Managers are shown how to recognise and respond to signs that someone is affected by alcohol or drugs.

## 10. Records

Concerns, actions and any test results are recorded confidentially in the HR pack. Health information is kept as special category data and shared only with those who need it.
