## 1. Purpose

When a care worker arrives and a client does not answer the door, the client may have gone out, be asleep, or have forgotten we were coming — or they may be lying on the floor, ill or hurt. This policy sets out what {{organisation}} does every time, so that we find out quickly whether the client is safe and nobody is left without help.

## 2. Scope

It applies to every care worker, and to the office and on-call staff who support them, whenever a care worker cannot get an answer at a client's home or cannot get in.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, and Regulation 13: Safeguarding service users from abuse and improper treatment.
- Care Act 2014 — the local authority's duties when an adult may be at risk, including from self-neglect.
- Police and Criminal Evidence Act 1984, section 17 — the police may enter premises without a warrant to save life or limb.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — a client with capacity may choose not to let us in.

## 4. Our policy

- A no reply is treated as an emergency until we know the client is safe.
- Care workers never leave a no-reply visit without telling the office, and the office does not close it until the client has been found.
- Care workers do not force entry. If entry is needed to save a client from harm, the police or ambulance service are called.
- Clients are asked to tell us if they will be out, and we record who to contact if they do not answer.

## 5. Planning ahead

At assessment, and at every review, we agree with the client what we should do if they do not answer: who holds a key, who their next of kin and other contacts are, whether they go out regularly, whether they have a personal alarm, and whether they have any condition that makes a no reply more worrying (such as diabetes, epilepsy, a history of falls or confusion). This is recorded in the care plan. Clients and families are asked to tell the office if the client will be away or out at a visit time.

## 6. What to do

### 6.1 The care worker

- Knock loudly and ring the bell. Call through the letterbox, giving your name.
- Look through the windows, front and back if you can get to them safely, and listen for any sound.
- Phone the client's landline or mobile.
- If you have a key or key safe access, let yourself in, calling out as you go. If the door is chained or bolted from the inside, do not force it: the client is probably inside, so phone the office straight away.
- If you can see or hear the client and they appear to be collapsed, injured or unable to get up, phone 999 straight away, then the office.
- Otherwise, phone the office or on-call and tell them you cannot get an answer. Do not leave until they tell you to.

Check with a neighbour only if the care plan says the client has agreed to it, and do not share any detail about the client's care.

### 6.2 The office or on-call

The office or on-call works through these checks quickly, while the care worker waits:

- Check whether the visit was cancelled or the client told us they would be out, and whether the client was admitted to hospital or respite.
- Phone the client's contacts and key holder from the care plan.
- Check with any day centre or other service the client attends.
- Phone the local hospitals to ask whether the client has been admitted.

If the client is found to be safe somewhere else, the visit is recorded as a no reply with the reason, and the care worker can move on.

### 6.3 When the client cannot be found

If the client has not been found, and there is any reason to think they may be inside and unwell, or they are at risk because of their health, the on-call manager phones the police (999 if there is a risk to life), explains the concern, and asks them to check on the client. The police decide whether to force entry. The care worker waits for the police where it is safe and the on-call manager asks them to, and the office arranges cover for their next visits.

If a family member or key holder is coming, the care worker waits for them where it is safe, and goes in with them.

### 6.4 When the client is found

- If the client is hurt or unwell, give first aid and call 999 as needed. Follow our First Aid, Medical Emergencies and Deterioration policy.
- If the client is well, give the care that was due.
- If the client has died, follow our End of Life Care, Death and Advance Decisions policy.
- Tell the office what happened, and record everything in the daily notes.

If a lock or door was broken to get in, the office makes sure the home is made secure before anyone leaves, and tells the client and their family.

## 7. When a client chooses not to let us in

A client with capacity can choose not to let us in, or to cancel a visit. If they answer the door, or speak to you, and say they do not want care today, respect their choice but check they are safe, tell them you will let the office know, and phone the office. If you are worried that the refusal puts them at risk, or that someone else in the home is preventing us from seeing them, say so to the office. The registered manager decides whether to contact the family, the GP, the commissioner, or raise a safeguarding concern.

## 8. Repeated no replies

Where a client is regularly out or does not answer, the registered manager reviews the care plan and access arrangements with them, their family and the commissioner. A pattern may be a sign of self-neglect, a change in the client's health or memory, or that someone is preventing care, and may need a safeguarding concern.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure no-reply arrangements are agreed in each care plan, reviews every no reply, and raises safeguarding concerns where needed.
- **Office and on-call staff** — take charge of every no reply until the client is found, make the checks quickly, call the police when needed, and arrange cover for the care worker's round.
- **Care workers** — follow the steps in this policy every time, never leave without telling the office, and record what happened.

## 10. Training

All care workers and office staff learn this policy during induction, and refresh it every year. On-call staff are shown how to manage a no reply before they first cover on-call.

## 11. Records

Every no reply is recorded in the client's daily notes and on their record, with the times, checks made and outcome. A no reply that leads to the police being called, a client being found hurt, or a safeguarding concern is also recorded through the site's concerns process.
