## 1. Purpose

Good records are part of good care. They tell the next person what has happened, show that care was given as planned, and protect the people we support and the staff who care for them. If it is not written down, it is hard to show it was done. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} writes, keeps, stores and finally disposes of its records.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who writes or uses records, and to all records we keep — care records, medicine records, daily notes, risk assessments, incident and complaint records, staff records and business records — whether on the site, in other electronic systems or on paper.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 17: Good governance — an accurate, complete and contemporaneous record for each person we support, and the records needed to manage the service and employ staff, kept securely.
- UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 — personal information is accurate, kept no longer than necessary, and kept secure.
- Records Management Code of Practice: a guide to the management of health and care records, published by NHS England and applying to adult social care — how long each kind of record should be kept, and how to manage records through their life.
- National Institute for Health and Care Excellence guidance, Managing medicines in care homes, and Managing medicines for adults receiving social care in the community — keeping accurate medicines records.
- Other laws that set retention periods for particular records, such as employment, tax, health and safety and right to work records.

## 4. Our policy

- Records are written at the time, or as soon as possible after, by the person who gave the care.
- Records are factual, accurate, clear and respectful.
- Records are kept securely and seen only by those who need them.
- Each kind of record is kept for the period in our retention schedule and then securely destroyed.

## 5. Writing good care records

### 5.1 What to write

Record what you did, what you saw, and what the person said, in a way that the next person can act on. A good record:

- says what care was given, and any care that was not given and why;
- notes how the person was — their mood, health, skin, eating and drinking — and anything different from usual;
- records what the person said they wanted, in their own words where it matters;
- records any concern, who you told, and when;
- includes the date, the time, and your name.

### 5.2 How to write it

- Write at the time or straight after the visit or task — never in advance, and never for someone else.
- Stick to facts. Write "Mrs A ate half her lunch and said she was not hungry", not "Mrs A was difficult".
- Use plain language, and avoid abbreviations unless they are on our approved list.
- Be respectful: write as if the person will read it, because they may.
- Do not write opinions about the person or other people, or blame.

### 5.3 Correcting mistakes

On the site and other electronic systems, correct a record using the system's own way of amending it, so that the original and the change can both be seen. On paper, draw a single line through the mistake so it can still be read, write the correction, and sign, date and time it. Never use correction fluid, tear out pages or rewrite a record.

### 5.4 Medicines records

Medicines given, prompted, refused or not given are recorded on the medicines administration record at the time, as our Medicines Management policy sets out.

## 6. Where records are kept

In homecare, the care plan and the records the care worker needs are available in the client's home as well as at the office, and the client is told who can see them. In a care home, records are kept securely in the home, and paper records are never left where visitors or other residents can read them.

Records belong to the company, not to the member of staff. Do not take them home, copy them to your own devices, or keep your own private notes about the people we support.

## 7. How long we keep records

We keep a retention schedule, based on the Records Management Code of Practice and the laws that apply to each kind of record, that lists every kind of record we hold and how long we keep it. The registered manager keeps it up to date. As a guide:

- Care records are kept for the period the Code sets for adult social care records, counted from the end of the person's care or their death.
- Staff files, including recruitment, supervision, training and HR case records, are kept for six years after employment ends, unless a safeguarding concern or legal claim means keeping them longer.
- Right to work checks are kept for the length of employment and two years after it ends.
- Accident records are kept for at least as long as health and safety law requires, and longer where a claim is possible or a person was a child.
- Pay and tax records are kept for as long as HM Revenue and Customs requires.

Records that may be needed for a complaint, a safeguarding enquiry, an investigation, an inquest or a legal claim are kept until it is over, even if their retention period has passed. Records relating to someone's death are kept in full.

## 8. Disposal

At the end of its retention period, a record is reviewed and, if no longer needed, destroyed securely: paper is shredded or collected by a confidential waste service that gives a certificate of destruction, and electronic records are deleted so they cannot be recovered. We keep a log of what was destroyed and when.

When a homecare service ends, paper records in the client's home are collected and returned to the office.

## 9. Audits

Records are checked regularly. Seniors and managers look at daily notes and medicines records as part of spot checks and audits, and give feedback. Poor recording is raised at supervision and, where needed, through training.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure records are accurate, complete and secure, keeps the retention schedule, and arranges audits and disposal.
- **Seniors and managers** — check records, give feedback, and correct problems.
- **All staff** — write accurate, timely records, keep them secure, and never alter a record improperly.

## 11. Training

Record keeping is covered at induction and in the Care Certificate, and refreshed through feedback at spot checks and supervision.
