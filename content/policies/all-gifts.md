## 1. Purpose

The people we support and their families often want to say thank you, and the wish to give is natural and kind. But gifts can blur professional boundaries, leave staff open to accusations, and can sometimes be a sign that someone is being exploited. This policy sets out what staff of {{organisation}} may and may not accept, how gifts are recorded, and what happens when someone wants to leave money to staff or to the company in their will.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us, including office staff, managers, directors and volunteers, and to gifts, tips, loans, hospitality and legacies offered by the people we support, their families and friends, and by suppliers and others who do business with us.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 13: Safeguarding service users from abuse and improper treatment — including protecting people from financial abuse.
- Care Act 2014 and the Care and Support Statutory Guidance — financial and material abuse, including pressure in connection with wills, property or inheritance.
- Fraud Act 2006 — dishonestly abusing a position of trust to make a gain is a crime.
- Bribery Act 2010 — offering or accepting an advantage to influence how someone does their job is a crime, and a company can be liable if it fails to prevent bribery.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — a person giving a gift must have the capacity to make that gift.

## 4. Our policy

- Staff do not accept money, gift vouchers, cheques, loans, valuables or items of real worth from the people we support or their families.
- Small, token gifts may be accepted only as this policy allows, and every gift offered — accepted or declined — is recorded.
- Staff never ask for, hint at or encourage a gift.
- No member of staff may benefit from the will of a person we support.
- Every concern that a gift may be a sign of abuse or undue influence is acted on.

## 5. What you may accept

You may accept a small, perishable or token gift, such as a box of chocolates or biscuits to share with colleagues, a card, or flowers, where refusing would cause real offence and the value is no more than [the limit the company sets]. Tell the office or the person in charge the same day, so that it is recorded in the gifts register.

Even a small gift should be declined if:

- the person may not have capacity to decide to give it;
- it is offered repeatedly, or by someone who cannot afford it;
- it is offered in return for a favour, extra time, or different treatment;
- a family member is unaware of it or likely to object;
- you feel uncomfortable about it.

## 6. What you must not accept

- Money of any amount, including tips, "something for your petrol", or change from shopping.
- Gift vouchers, cheques, lottery tickets, or anything that can be exchanged for money.
- Jewellery, ornaments, furniture, electrical items or anything of value or sentimental worth, even if the person says it would otherwise be thrown away.
- Loans, or the use of a car, property or anything else belonging to the person.
- Hospitality, such as meals out or holidays, other than a drink or snack offered during a visit or shift.
- Any gift from a supplier, contractor or anyone seeking business with us, other than modest items such as a diary or pen, without the registered manager's agreement.

## 7. Declining kindly

Thank the person warmly and explain that our rules do not let you accept, and that their thanks means a lot. Suggest they write a card or a compliment to the company instead, or, if they wish, give to a charity of their choice. If they insist, or leave something for you, do not keep it: tell the office or person in charge, who will return it to the person or their family, or deal with it as they agree, and record what happened.

## 8. Wills and legacies

### 8.1 Staff

No member of staff may accept a legacy, or anything left to them in a will, from a person we support or have supported. Staff must never:

- discuss a person's will with them, other than to suggest they speak to a solicitor;
- help to write, witness or sign a will, or act as an executor;
- recommend a solicitor or adviser of their own.

If you find out that a person we support intends to leave, or has left, something to you or another member of staff, tell the registered manager straight away. The registered manager records it, advises the person to take independent legal advice, and, if a gift has been made, makes sure the member of staff declines it in writing to the executors. Failing to report a legacy, or accepting one, is gross misconduct.

### 8.2 The company

If a person wishes to leave a legacy or make a donation to {{organisation}}, the registered manager tells the nominated individual. It may be accepted only if the person had capacity, had the chance to take independent advice, and there is no reason to think they were under pressure. It is recorded, and used for the benefit of the people we support, never to reward individual staff.

## 9. When a gift may be a sign of abuse

Repeated or generous gifts, a person giving away belongings, or anyone — staff, family or visitors — showing unusual interest in a person's money or will can be signs of financial abuse or undue influence. Tell the registered manager. They decide whether to raise a safeguarding concern with the local authority, and record it through the site's concerns process.

## 10. The gifts register

The registered manager keeps a gifts register recording every gift offered to staff or the company: the date, who offered it, who it was offered to, what it was, its estimated value, whether it was accepted or declined, and what was done with it. The register is reviewed as part of our governance checks, and patterns are looked into.

## 11. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — keeps the gifts register, decides on gifts that fall outside the rules, deals with legacies, and acts on concerns.
- **Nominated individual** — decides with the registered manager whether the company may accept a legacy or donation.
- **All staff** — follow this policy, decline gifts kindly, report every gift and every legacy, and raise concerns.

## 12. Training

All staff learn about gifts, professional boundaries and financial abuse during induction, and refresh it every year with safeguarding training.

## 13. Records

The gifts register and records of legacies and returned gifts are kept for as long as our record retention schedule requires. Concerns are kept through the concerns process.
