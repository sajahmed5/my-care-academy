## 1. Purpose

A good day is more than being washed, dressed and fed. Residents need things to do that matter to them, people to talk to, and a continuing part in the life of their family and community. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} makes sure every resident has the chance to take part in meaningful activity and to stay connected, whatever their abilities.

## 2. Scope

It applies to every resident, including those who spend most of their time in their rooms or in bed, and to all staff. Activities are everyone's job, not only the activities coordinator's.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 9: Person-centred care — care must meet residents' needs and reflect their preferences.
- Regulation 10: Dignity and respect — including supporting residents' autonomy, independence and involvement in the community.
- Regulation 9A: Visiting and accompanying in care homes — residents must be able to receive visitors.
- NICE quality standard QS50: Mental wellbeing of older people in care homes — including that residents are offered opportunities during their day to take part in meaningful activity that promotes their health and mental wellbeing.
- Equality Act 2010 — activities must be open to residents whatever their disability, faith, culture, sexual orientation or other protected characteristic, with reasonable adjustments.

## 4. Our policy

- Every resident has a chance, every day, to do something they find meaningful, if they want to.
- Activities are planned around each resident's life history, interests, culture and abilities, not around a fixed timetable for everyone.
- Residents who cannot or do not want to join group activities have one-to-one time.
- Residents keep their links with family, friends, faith and community, and can go out.
- A resident's choice not to take part is respected.
- We support residents to take reasonable risks in doing the things they enjoy.

## 5. Getting to know each resident

When a resident moves in, staff find out, with the resident and their family, about their life: their work, family, places they have lived, hobbies, faith, music, food, routines and what makes a good day for them. This life history is kept in the care plan and added to over time, and the care plan says how the resident likes to spend their time, what they can do for themselves, and the support they need to take part. It is reviewed at every care plan review and whenever staff notice a change in what the resident enjoys or can do.

## 6. What meaningful activity looks like

Meaningful activity is anything the resident finds worthwhile. It includes:

- everyday tasks: laying tables, folding laundry, watering plants, helping with baking, looking after a pet;
- hobbies and interests: reading, music, crafts, gardening, puzzles, sport on the television, games;
- social contact: conversation, visitors, phone and video calls, clubs and groups;
- faith and culture: services in the home or in the community, festivals, and food that matters to the resident;
- exercise and movement: walks, seated exercise, dancing, as the resident's health allows;
- outings: shops, cafes, parks, places that mean something to the resident;
- sensory activity for residents living with advanced dementia: music, touch, smell, hand massage, being outdoors;
- quiet time, if that is what the resident wants.

## 7. How we do it

### 7.1 Planning

The activities coordinator, with residents, plans a varied programme each week of group activities, one-to-one time, outings and visits from community groups, and makes it known to residents and families in ways each resident can understand. Residents' meetings and individual conversations are used to ask what residents want.

### 7.2 Everyone's role

Care staff support activity throughout the day, not only at set times: chatting while helping someone dress, involving a resident in a task, putting on music they like, or taking a resident into the garden. The shift leader makes sure that, on each shift, residents who are in their rooms or in bed have someone spend time with them.

### 7.3 Residents in their rooms and in bed

Residents who stay in their rooms, by choice or because of their health, have planned one-to-one time with staff, based on their interests. Staff make sure they have what they need within reach — radio, books, call bell, photographs — and that they are not isolated.

### 7.4 Staying connected

- Residents are supported to keep in touch with family and friends, by visits, phone, letters and video calls. See our Visiting policy.
- Residents can take part in the community: shops, clubs, places of worship, day centres, volunteering.
- Residents can vote. We help residents who wish to register to vote at the home, and to vote in person, by post or by proxy.
- We invite local schools, faith groups, and community groups into the home, with suitable checks.

### 7.5 Outings and risk

Outings are planned with a risk assessment that covers transport, staffing, medicines, mobility, toilets, weather, and what to do if a resident becomes unwell. Risks are managed so that residents can still go out, not used as a reason to stop them. Vehicles used are insured, roadworthy and suitable, and drivers follow our Driving at Work policy. Residents with a Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards authorisation go out in line with its conditions.

## 8. Recording and checking

Staff record in each resident's daily notes what they did, how they took part, and how they seemed — not only that an activity was offered. The registered manager checks regularly that every resident, especially those who are in bed, quiet or living with dementia, is getting meaningful activity, and acts if anyone is being missed. Residents' and families' views on activities are part of our quality checks.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure there are enough staff and resources for activities, checks every resident is included, and acts on feedback.
- **Activities coordinator** — gets to know each resident's interests, plans the programme with residents, builds community links, and supports staff to provide activity.
- **Shift leaders** — make sure activity happens on every shift, including for residents in their rooms.
- **Care staff** — involve residents in everyday life, spend time with them, and record what they did and enjoyed.

## 10. Training

All care staff learn about person-centred care, life history and meaningful activity during induction, and dementia awareness as part of the Care Certificate. The activities coordinator receives further training in activities for people living with dementia and sensory impairments.

## 11. Records

Life histories and activity preferences are kept in the resident's care plan. Daily notes record what each resident did. Outing risk assessments and the activities programme are kept for as long as our record retention schedule requires.
