## 1. Purpose

When people move into the home they bring their belongings with them, and many ask us to look after some of their money. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} keeps residents' money and property safe, records every item and transaction, keeps residents in control of their own affairs, and protects residents from financial abuse and staff from suspicion.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works in the home, to all money the home holds for residents, and to every resident's belongings, from clothes and glasses to furniture and valuables.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Care Act 2014 and the Care and Support Statutory Guidance — financial and material abuse is a form of abuse the local authority must make enquiries about.
- Care and Support (Charging and Assessment of Resources) Regulations 2014 — residents whose care is arranged by the local authority keep a personal expenses allowance, set by the government each year, for their own use.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — lasting powers of attorney for property and financial affairs, deputies appointed by the Court of Protection, and best interests decisions.
- Social Security (Claims and Payments) Regulations 1987 — the Department for Work and Pensions can appoint an appointee to manage a person's benefits.
- Fraud Act 2006 and Theft Act 1968 — abusing a position of trust for gain, and taking another's property, are crimes.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 13: Safeguarding service users from abuse and improper treatment, and Regulation 17: Good governance.

## 4. Our policy

- Residents keep and manage their own money and belongings wherever they can and wish to.
- The home holds money for a resident only with their agreement, or that of their attorney, deputy or appointee, and only for safekeeping.
- Every resident's money is accounted for separately. It is never mixed with the home's money or used for anyone else, and never used to pay the home's fees unless the resident or their representative has agreed this in writing.
- The personal expenses allowance belongs to the resident, for the things they choose.
- Every resident's property is listed on admission, and the list is kept up to date.
- Staff never borrow from, lend to, buy from or sell to a resident, or take any financial advantage from them.

## 5. Residents' money

### 5.1 Agreeing what we hold

On admission the registered manager asks the resident how they want to manage their money and who helps them. If the resident wants the home to hold money for them, a written agreement records how much may be held, what it may be spent on (such as hairdressing, toiletries, outings and newspapers), and who may top it up. Where a resident has an attorney, deputy or appointee, we check their authority and agree arrangements with them.

Where a resident lacks capacity to manage their money and has nobody with authority to do it, the registered manager asks the local authority to arrange it. The home, its owners and staff do not act as a resident's attorney or deputy. The home acts as a resident's appointee only when the Department for Work and Pensions agrees and there is no one else suitable; benefits are then paid into an account in the resident's name or a client account kept separate from the home's own money, and are audited as below.

### 5.2 Keeping money safe

- Each resident's money is kept in a separate, named wallet or envelope in the home's safe, or in an account held for residents that is separate from the home's own money.
- Only the registered manager and staff they name have access to the safe.
- Every amount in or out is recorded on the resident's money record at the time: date, amount, reason, balance, and two signatures. A receipt is kept for every purchase.
- The resident, or their representative, signs for money they take, where they can.
- We do not hold more than the agreed amount. Larger sums are returned to the resident's bank or representative.

### 5.3 Spending for a resident

When staff buy something for a resident, they take the money signed out from the safe, keep it separate from their own, bring back a receipt and the change, and sign it back in with a second member of staff. Staff do not use their own loyalty cards on residents' purchases.

### 5.4 Checks

A member of staff not involved in day-to-day handling checks each resident's balance against the record every week. The registered manager audits all residents' money every month and signs the audit. Any discrepancy is reported and looked into the same day. Residents and their representatives can see their record at any time.

## 6. Residents' property

### 6.1 On admission

On the day of admission, or as soon as possible after, two members of staff list the resident's belongings with them or their family: clothing, glasses, hearing aids, dentures, mobility aids, furniture, electrical items, jewellery, and anything of value or of sentimental importance. Both staff and the resident, or their representative, sign the inventory. A copy is given to the resident or their representative.

Clothing and personal items are discreetly marked with the resident's name, with their agreement.

### 6.2 Keeping the inventory up to date

When a resident brings in, buys or is given something of value, or something is thrown away, broken or given away, the inventory is updated and signed. Electrical items brought in are safety tested before use.

### 6.3 Valuables

We advise residents and families not to keep large sums of cash or valuable jewellery in the home. If a resident wants to keep valuables, the home offers its safe or a lockable drawer or cupboard in their room. Items handed in for the safe are recorded, with a description, and signed for when taken in and when given back. The home's insurance cover for residents' property is explained in the contract.

> Describe what your insurance covers for residents' belongings, and any limit, so that residents and families know whether to insure valuables themselves.

### 6.4 Lost or damaged property

If a resident's property is lost or damaged, staff search for it, record it, and tell the shift leader. Missing items that cannot be explained, especially money or valuables, are reported to the registered manager the same day. Damage caused by staff is put right by the home.

## 7. After a resident leaves or dies

When a resident moves out, their money and property are returned to them or their representative against the inventory and money record, and signed for. When a resident dies, money and property are kept safely and released only to the executor or the person legally entitled to deal with the estate, against the inventory and with a signature. We do not hand belongings to any relative who simply asks for them.

## 8. Financial abuse

Financial abuse includes theft, fraud, scams, misuse of a power of attorney or of benefits, pressure about wills, and taking money or belongings without consent. It can be committed by family, visitors, other residents or staff. Signs include a resident's money running short, unpaid fees when the resident should have enough, belongings going missing, or a visitor showing unusual interest in a resident's finances.

If you suspect financial abuse, tell the shift leader or registered manager straight away. The registered manager raises a safeguarding concern with the local authority, informs the police where a crime may have been committed, notifies the Care Quality Commission as Regulation 18 of the Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009 requires, and records it through the site's concerns process. Concerns about an attorney or deputy may also be reported to the Office of the Public Guardian.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — agrees money arrangements, controls access to the safe, audits money every month, and acts on concerns.
- **Shift leaders** — make sure transactions are recorded and witnessed, and report discrepancies and missing property.
- **Care staff** — complete inventories, handle money only as this policy sets out, keep receipts, and report concerns.

## 10. Training

All staff learn about residents' money and property and about financial abuse during induction and safeguarding training, and refresh it every year. Staff who handle residents' money are shown how by the registered manager before they do so.

## 11. Records

Money agreements, money records, receipts, audits, inventories and safe records are kept for each resident for as long as our record retention schedule requires, including after they leave or die.
