## 1. Purpose

COSHH stands for the Control of Substances Hazardous to Health. Care work involves cleaning products, disinfectants, bodily fluids and other substances that can harm the skin, the lungs or the eyes, or cause infection. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} protects staff and the people we support from them.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us, and to every hazardous substance staff use or come into contact with at work — in people's homes, in a care home and in our office. It includes:

- cleaning products, bleach and disinfectants;
- bodily fluids such as blood, urine, faeces and vomit, and the germs they can carry;
- substances that can cause skin or breathing problems, such as some gloves, hand products and powders;
- other products used in care, such as thickening powders and some creams.

Medicines are covered by our Medicines Management policy, and oxygen and emollient creams carry fire risks covered in our risk assessments.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 (COSHH) — employers must assess the risks from hazardous substances, prevent or adequately control exposure, make sure controls are used and maintained, provide information, instruction and training, plan for accidents and emergencies, and provide health surveillance where needed.
- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999.
- Personal Protective Equipment at Work Regulations 1992 — suitable protective equipment, provided free of charge.
- Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 — some work-related diseases, such as occupational dermatitis and asthma, must be reported.
- Health and Safety Executive guidance on COSHH and on skin care at work.

## 4. Our policy

- We use the least hazardous product that will do the job.
- Every hazardous substance we provide or ask staff to use has been assessed, and staff know how to use it safely.
- We provide gloves, aprons, eye protection and other protective equipment free of charge, and staff use them.
- Products are kept in their original containers, never decanted into unlabelled bottles, and never mixed.

## 5. How we assess substances

For every hazardous product we provide, the registered manager obtains the supplier's safety data sheet and completes a COSHH assessment, which records:

- what the product is, what it is used for, and how it can harm — by breathing it in, through the skin or eyes, or by swallowing it;
- who could be harmed, including people we support who might swallow or misuse it;
- how exposure is controlled — the way it is used, ventilation, protective equipment, storage;
- what to do if it is spilled, splashed or swallowed;
- how to dispose of it.

The hazard pictograms on labels — such as a flame, an exclamation mark, or a corroded hand — show the kind of danger. Staff are shown what they mean.

## 6. In people's own homes

The cleaning products in a client's home belong to the client, and they choose what to buy. We do not control them, but we must still protect our care workers:

- Use a client's products only if you know what they are and how to use them. Read the label, and follow it.
- Never mix products. Bleach mixed with some toilet cleaners or other acids gives off a poisonous gas.
- Do not use a product from an unlabelled or wrongly labelled container.
- If a product makes you unwell, irritates your skin or seems unsafe, stop using it and tell the office. The office will talk to the client or their family about an alternative.
- Wear the gloves and apron we provide.
- Do not bring your own cleaning products into a client's home unless the office has agreed it.

The environmental risk assessment notes where products are kept, and whether the client could be at risk from them — for example, a person living with dementia who might mistake a product for a drink. Where they are, we agree with the client or their family how products will be stored safely.

## 7. In a care home

- The home keeps a list of every hazardous substance on the premises, with its safety data sheet and COSHH assessment, where staff can find them.
- Hazardous substances are stored in locked cupboards or rooms, away from residents, and returned there straight after use. Cleaning trolleys are never left unattended where residents could reach them.
- Thickening powders and other products that could be swallowed accidentally are kept out of residents' reach.
- Only products approved by the registered manager are used. New products are assessed before they are brought in.
- Spills are cleaned up straight away, following the COSHH assessment, and the area made safe.

## 8. Bodily fluids and infection

Bodily fluids can carry infection. Wear gloves and an apron when you may come into contact with them, clean spills promptly with the product and method set out in our Infection Prevention and Control policy, and dispose of waste as the person's care plan or the home's procedure says. If you are injured by a used needle or splashed in the eyes or mouth with blood or other bodily fluid, wash the area, make it bleed if it is a puncture, and get medical advice straight away — then report it.

## 9. Looking after your skin

Frequent hand washing, glove use and cleaning products can cause dermatitis. To protect your skin:

- dry your hands thoroughly after washing;
- use the moisturiser provided, especially at the end of a shift;
- use gloves only when they are needed, and change them between tasks;
- tell your manager straight away if your skin becomes red, sore, cracked or itchy.

Gloves we provide are latex-free. Tell us if you have, or think you may have, a reaction to any product. Where the assessment shows a risk of skin problems, managers check staff's hands from time to time as a simple form of health surveillance, and record the check on the staff member's file.

## 10. If something goes wrong

- Splash in the eyes — rinse with clean running water for several minutes and get medical advice.
- On the skin — wash off with plenty of water, and remove contaminated clothing.
- Swallowed — do not make the person sick. Call 111 or 999, and have the product container or label to hand.
- Breathed in — move to fresh air, and get medical advice if the person feels unwell.

Report every exposure, spill or reaction through the site's concerns process the same day.

## 11. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure COSHH assessments are done for the products we provide, that protective equipment is available, that staff are trained, and that exposures are investigated.
- **Office staff and shift leaders** — make sure staff have the protective equipment they need, and act on concerns about products.
- **All staff** — follow the assessments and labels, use protective equipment, store products safely, and report reactions and incidents.

## 12. Training

All staff complete COSHH training during induction and refresh it every year with their health and safety training. Staff in a care home who handle cleaning chemicals in bulk, and laundry and kitchen staff, receive training on the products they use. Training is recorded on the training matrix.

## 13. Records

COSHH assessments and safety data sheets are kept by the registered manager and reviewed every year, or when a product or how it is used changes. Exposures and reactions are kept through the concerns process. Health surveillance checks are kept on the staff member's file, for the long period COSHH requires, which runs for many years after they leave. Everything else is kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
