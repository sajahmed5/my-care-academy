## 1. Purpose

Clients and their families have the right to complain, and to be listened to when they do. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} makes it easy to complain, looks into every complaint fairly and promptly, puts things right, and uses what complaints tell us to improve care for everyone.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us, and to complaints about any part of our service from anyone: a client, their family, friends or neighbours, an advocate, a professional, a commissioner or a member of the public.

A complaint is any expression of dissatisfaction about our service, spoken or written, that calls for a response. Staff concerns about their own work go under Grievance, and concerns about wrongdoing under Whistleblowing. A complaint about how we use personal information also follows Data Protection and Access to Records.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 16: Receiving and acting on complaints — an accessible system for receiving, recording, handling and responding to complaints, every complaint investigated and acted on, and a summary sent to CQC within 28 days if it asks.
- Local Authority Social Services and National Health Service Complaints (England) Regulations 2009 — how local authorities and NHS bodies handle complaints. They do not apply to us directly, but a client whose care the local authority arranges can complain to it, and we work with it to resolve the complaint.
- Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman — takes complaints about adult social care, including from people who pay for their own care, once our own process is finished.
- Care Quality Commission — does not investigate individual complaints, but wants to hear about people's experience of care.
- Equality Act 2010 and Mental Capacity Act 2005 — no victimisation, reasonable adjustments so that everyone can complain, and who may complain for a client who lacks capacity.

## 4. Our policy

- Complaints are welcome. They tell us what we need to put right.
- Anyone can complain, in any way, and nobody is treated worse for doing so. A client's care is never reduced, delayed or ended because they or their family complained.
- Every complaint is recorded, however small.
- We investigate fairly, apologise where we got something wrong, and say what we will change.
- The timescales below are our own standards, not set by law. Where we cannot meet one, we say why and when we will.
- If someone's contact becomes abusive or excessive, the registered manager may agree with them in writing how we keep in touch. Their complaint is still answered, and the client's care is not affected.

## 5. Making a complaint

Clients are told how to complain when their care starts, in our guide to the service, and at reviews. They can complain to their care worker or anyone at the office or on-call; by phone on {{office_phone}}; by email to {{office_email}}; in writing to {{registered_manager}} at {{office_address}}; or through a family member, friend or advocate. Information is available in large print, easy read and other languages on request, with an interpreter where needed (see Accessible Information and Communication).

A complaint can be made on a client's behalf with their consent. Where the client lacks capacity to make or agree to it, we accept it from a person acting in their best interests, such as an attorney, a deputy or a close family member, and share only what that person needs. We look into anonymous complaints as far as we can.

## 6. What to do

### 6.1 If a client or family member complains to you

- Listen, stay calm, and do not argue or defend yourself.
- Say you are sorry they are unhappy and that you will pass it to the office today. Do not promise an outcome, or agree to keep it between you.
- If the complaint is about you, still pass it on. Being complained about is not the same as being at fault.
- Tell the office before the end of your shift, and record it through the site's concerns process.

Some clients will not complain to the care worker who visits them alone, for fear of how they will be treated. The office asks clients about their care at reviews, telephone checks and spot checks, so that they can raise things without their care worker there.

### 6.2 Resolving it quickly

Many concerns can be put right at once: a late visit explained, a rota changed, a task done the way the client prefers. If the person is satisfied by the end of the next working day, the office records the concern, what was done, and that it was resolved. If not, it becomes a formal complaint.

### 6.3 Acknowledging

The registered manager, or a senior they name, acknowledges a formal complaint within 3 working days, in writing or in the way the person prefers, saying who is looking into it, what we understand the complaint to be, and when to expect a full reply. Where a local authority or the NHS arranges the care, we tell the commissioner as the contract requires.

### 6.4 Investigating

Someone not involved in what is complained about investigates. The evidence usually includes call monitoring records, rotas and travel time, daily notes, medicines records and the care plan, and accounts from the care workers, client and family. Common home care complaints are about missed, late or rushed visits, too many different care workers, a new care worker arriving unannounced, a care worker's conduct in the home, key safes, and families who live elsewhere not being kept informed. Where a complaint is about a care worker, the registered manager decides whether someone else should visit while it is looked into.

### 6.5 Responding

The full written response is sent within 20 working days. If we need longer, we write before then to explain why and give a new date. It sets out what we found on each point, apologises where we got something wrong, says what we will do and by when, and explains how to ask for a review and go to the Ombudsman.

### 6.6 Review

If the person is not satisfied, they can ask for a review by {{nominated_individual}}. We acknowledge the request within 3 working days and send the outcome within 20 working days. That is our final response.

> Say who reviews complaints if the nominated individual is also the registered manager, or was involved in the complaint — for example another director.

## 7. Outside our process

- Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman — once our process is finished, or if we have taken too long, the person can ask the Ombudsman to look at the complaint, whoever pays for the care. The service is free.
- The local authority — where it arranges the care, the person can complain to it instead of, or as well as, to us.
- The NHS — where the NHS funds the care, for example through NHS Continuing Healthcare, the person can complain to the NHS body that funds it, and then to the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman.
- Care Quality Commission — anyone can tell CQC about their experience of our care.

## 8. Complaints that are safeguarding concerns

Some complaints show that a client may have been abused or neglected — repeated missed visits, rough handling, money going missing. Safeguarding comes first: the registered manager raises it with the local authority the same day, as our Safeguarding policy sets out, and agrees with the local authority how the complaint is handled alongside its enquiry. Where harm was caused, the duty of candour may also apply, and some events must be notified to CQC (see Reportable Incidents).

## 9. Learning from complaints

{{registered_manager}} reviews all complaints every month with incidents and safeguarding concerns, looking for patterns by area, time of day, care worker or type of visit, and reports trends and actions to {{nominated_individual}} through our governance checks. Lessons are shared with staff without naming anyone, and lead to changes in rotas, care plans, training or policies. Compliments are recorded too, and passed on.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure every complaint is acknowledged, investigated and answered on time, and leads the monthly review.
- **Nominated individual** — carries out reviews, and checks that learning leads to change.
- **Office and on-call staff** — record concerns, resolve what they can, and pass formal complaints to the registered manager the same day.
- **Care workers** — listen, pass every complaint on the same day, and take part honestly in any investigation.

## 11. Training

All staff learn how to receive and pass on a complaint at induction, and refresh it every year. The registered manager and anyone who investigates complaints are trained to do so.

## 12. Records

Each complaint, its investigation, the letters sent and the outcome are recorded through the site's concerns process, separately from the client's care record, and kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
