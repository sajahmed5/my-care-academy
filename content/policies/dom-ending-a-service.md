## 1. Purpose

Every package of care ends at some point: the client no longer needs it, chooses another provider, moves into a care home, or dies, and occasionally we can no longer provide it safely. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} ends a service fairly, with proper notice, without leaving anyone at risk, and with keys, records and equipment dealt with properly.

## 2. Scope

It applies to every client, whether their care is arranged by a local authority, the NHS, or paid for privately or through a direct payment, and to the registered manager and office staff who manage the ending.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Care Act 2014 — the local authority's duty to meet the eligible needs of people in its area, which continues when a provider ends a package, and its temporary duty when a provider fails.
- Consumer Rights Act 2015 — the terms of our contract with a privately paying client must be fair and transparent, including the terms about notice.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — if a client lacks capacity to decide about their care, decisions are made in their best interests, involving their attorney or deputy.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 9: Person-centred care, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment (including working with other providers when care is handed over), and Regulation 17: Good governance.
- Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR — what happens to the client's records after care ends.

## 4. Our policy

- A client can end their care whenever they choose, on the notice their contract sets out.
- We end a client's care only for a good reason, after trying to resolve the problem, with the notice the contract requires, and never in a way that leaves the client without care they need.
- We tell the client, their representative and any commissioner in writing, and explain why.
- We hand over safely to whoever will provide care next.
- Keys, records and equipment are returned or collected, and signed for.

## 5. When the client ends the service

A client, or someone with authority to act for them, can end the service by telling the office. The registered manager or a senior:

- confirms the date the last visit will be, in line with the notice period in the contract; we do not insist on notice where it would be unfair, for example when the client has been admitted to a care home or hospital at short notice;
- asks, without pressure, why they are leaving, and records any concern as feedback or, if it is a complaint, through the site's concerns process;
- tells the commissioner if the care is funded by the local authority or NHS;
- checks whether the client still needs care, and if they will be left without it, makes sure the local authority knows.

## 6. When we must end the service

We may give notice to end a client's care when, after trying to resolve the problem:

- the client's needs have changed beyond what we can safely meet, for example they now need nursing care or more support than we can staff;
- staff are not safe in the home because of violence, threats, harassment, or hazards the client or household will not remove;
- the client or household repeatedly prevents us from providing safe care, such as refusing equipment that is essential for safe moving and handling;
- a privately paying client has not paid, after reminders and a chance to agree a plan;
- the commissioner ends the package or the contract, or we can no longer staff the area.

Before giving notice, the registered manager reviews the care with the client and their family and, where the care is funded, the commissioner, and records what was tried. The decision to end a service is made by the registered manager, and checked with the nominated individual if it is because of the client's or household's behaviour.

### 6.1 Ending a service at once

In rare cases we may need to stop visits immediately — for example after a serious assault on a care worker, or where a hazard puts staff at immediate risk. The registered manager decides this, tells the client and their representative straight away, and tells the commissioner the same day so that care can be arranged. If the client is at risk without care, the registered manager raises a safeguarding concern or asks the local authority for an urgent assessment, and we continue whatever care can be given safely until others take over, where we can.

### 6.2 Notice

Notice is given in writing to the client, and to their attorney, deputy or representative, and to the commissioner if there is one, with the reasons and the date care will end. The period is that set in the contract, and longer if the client needs more time to arrange other care safely. We stay in touch with the commissioner and do not stop care on the date if the client would be left at serious risk without agreeing a plan with the local authority first.

## 7. Handing over safely

Where another provider, a care home or a family member is taking over, and with the client's agreement, we share the information they need: a summary of the care plan and risk assessments, current medicines and how they are given, any DNACPR decision or ReSPECT form, and anything that matters to the client. We agree a date and time for the handover so that no visit is missed or duplicated. Medicines administration records and current care plans are completed up to the last visit.

## 8. On the last visit

- Collect the care record folder and any medicine administration records, leaving the client a copy of anything they ask for.
- Return any keys we hold, or make sure the client or their representative knows the key safe code should be changed. Record the return and ask the client or representative to sign. See our Key Holding and Key Safes policy.
- Return or account for any money we hold, with the record sheets.
- Leave the client's home secure.

The office stops visit scheduling from the agreed date, tells every care worker who was due to visit, and arranges for any of our own equipment to be collected. Equipment on loan from the community equipment service is not ours to remove.

## 9. When a client dies

When a client dies, the service ends at once and no notice is needed. Visits are cancelled, keys and records are dealt with sensitively with the family, and we follow our End of Life Care, Death and Advance Decisions policy. Any money owed or refundable is dealt with through the executor or the person dealing with the estate.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — decides whether to end a service, gives notice, works with commissioners, and makes sure no client is left at risk.
- **Nominated individual** — reviews decisions to end a service because of a client's or household's behaviour.
- **Office staff** — record the ending, stop visits, arrange the handover and the return of keys, records and money.
- **Care workers** — carry out the last visit as planned, return keys and records, and report any concern about the client's safety after care ends.

## 11. Training

Office staff and seniors are shown how to end a service and hand over safely during their induction. The registered manager keeps up to date with the terms of our contracts with commissioners.

## 12. Records

The reason the service ended, the notice given, handover information, and the return of keys and money are recorded on the client's record. Care records are brought back to the office and kept for as long as our record retention schedule requires (see Record Keeping and Retention).
