## 1. Purpose

Falls are common in care homes, and a fall can cost a resident their confidence, their mobility or their life. But keeping people sitting down to stop them falling causes harm of its own. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} reduces each resident's risk of falling while supporting them to move about freely, and what staff do when a resident falls, by day or by night.

## 2. Scope

It applies to every resident and everyone who works in the home, including night staff, agency staff and the maintenance team. It covers falls that staff see, residents found on the floor, and falls residents tell us about.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment — risks are assessed and reduced, and we respond properly when someone is hurt; and Regulation 15: Premises and equipment — the home and its equipment are safe, suitable and well maintained.
- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992 — staff must not lift a fallen resident by hand.
- Lifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations 1998 — hoists and lifting cushions are suitable, inspected and used only by trained staff.
- NICE guidance on assessing and preventing falls in older people, and NICE guideline NG232, Head injury: assessment and early management.
- Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency guidance on the safe use of bed rails.
- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, Regulation 18 — notification of serious injuries.
- Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR) — injuries to residents arising from our work activity or the condition of the premises, where the resident is taken directly from the scene to hospital for treatment, must be reported to the Health and Safety Executive.

## 4. Our policy

- Every resident's risk of falling is assessed on admission and reviewed regularly, and they have a plan to reduce it that they have agreed.
- We support residents to stay active and walk as much as they can, and we do not use restraint or keep people seated to prevent falls.
- Staff never lift a fallen resident by hand. A resident who may be injured is not moved until assessed.
- Every fall is recorded, reviewed and learned from.

## 5. Reducing the risk

### 5.1 Assessment

On admission, after any fall, and at each care plan review, a trained senior assesses the resident's falls risk: falls in the past year, walking and balance, dizziness, eyesight, feet and footwear, continence and night-time toilet use, confusion and restlessness, and medicines that increase the risk. The plan records what will be done and is agreed with the resident.

### 5.2 For each resident

Depending on the assessment:

- ask the GP for a medicines review, and the falls service or physiotherapist for advice on exercise and strength and balance;
- make sure walking aids are the right height, in good repair and within reach;
- help the resident to the toilet at times that suit their pattern, including at night;
- consider a low bed, a crash mat or hip protectors, agreed with the resident or in their best interests;
- consider a sensor mat or alarm only where it is needed, with consent or a best interests decision, and remember that monitoring is a restriction (see Restraint and Restrictive Practice);
- use bed rails only after an individual assessment, with the right rails for the bed and mattress, checked for gaps and entrapment risks.

### 5.3 The building

- Lighting is good in corridors, bathrooms and bedrooms, with night lights where residents want them.
- Floors are kept dry, even and free of clutter; spills are cleaned at once and wet floors signed.
- Handrails, grab rails and call bells are in place and working, and call bells are within reach of every resident in their room.
- Faults that could cause falls — lifting carpet, broken lights, faulty call bells — are reported to the maintenance team and the shift leader the same day, and made safe until fixed.
- Equipment is checked and serviced on schedule (see Equipment and Maintenance).

### 5.4 Every shift

Answer call bells promptly. Know which residents are at high risk, especially at night and at handover. Make sure residents have their glasses, hearing aids, walking aids and well-fitting footwear. Encourage residents to stand slowly. Report any change: new unsteadiness, dizziness, confusion or a near miss.

## 6. When a resident falls

### 6.1 Stop and check

- Call for help, and stay with the resident. The shift leader takes charge.
- Check whether the resident responds and is breathing normally. If not, phone 999 and start CPR, unless a valid do not attempt cardiopulmonary resuscitation decision is in place.
- Do not move them until they have been checked.

### 6.2 Phone 999 if

- they are unresponsive, very drowsy or more confused than usual;
- they have hit their head and take blood-thinning medicine, or are vomiting, or have a head wound;
- they have pain in the neck, back, hip or leg, or a limb looks shortened, turned or deformed;
- they have chest pain, difficulty breathing, new weakness on one side or slurred speech;
- they are bleeding heavily, or have been on the floor for a long time;
- the shift leader is in any doubt.

Keep the resident warm and comfortable where they are, and do not give food or drink. If they need medical advice but not an ambulance, the shift leader contacts the GP, NHS 111 or the local urgent community response service.

### 6.3 Helping an uninjured resident up

When the shift leader has checked the resident and is satisfied they are not injured:

- if the resident can get up by themselves, talk them through it in stages, using a sturdy chair, without taking their weight;
- if they cannot, two trained staff use the home's lifting cushion or hoist, as they have been trained;
- never lift a resident by hand, under the arms or by their clothing.

### 6.4 After the fall

- Check the resident for injuries again, and look after any wounds.
- Carry out post-fall observations — pulse, breathing, level of alertness and pain — at the intervals our post-fall procedure sets, and for longer after a head injury. Night staff continue them through the night.
- Watch for pain, drowsiness, vomiting or confusion over the following hours and days. Call 999 or 111 if they appear.
- Tell the resident's family or representative, as the resident wishes, and the GP.
- Record the fall in the resident's notes and through the site's concerns process as an incident, before the end of the shift.
- Hand over the fall, the observations and any concerns at the next handover.

### 6.5 Unwitnessed falls

Treat a fall that nobody saw — including a resident found on the floor — as a possible head injury, and follow the post-fall observations for a head injury unless the resident can clearly tell you they did not hit their head.

## 7. After every fall

A senior reviews the resident's falls risk assessment and care plan within a few days, talks with the resident about what happened, and makes referrals. The registered manager reviews every fall: whether it must be notified to CQC as a serious injury, whether it is reportable under RIDDOR, whether the duty of candour applies, and whether it raises a safeguarding concern. Each month they look at all falls for patterns — times, places, staffing, residents — and act on what they find.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure risks are assessed, the building and equipment are safe, staff are trained, and every fall is reviewed and notified as required.
- **Shift leaders** — take charge when a resident falls, decide on medical help, make sure observations are done and families told.
- **Seniors** — assess falls risk, write and review plans, and make referrals.
- **Care staff** — reduce risks, answer call bells, respond to falls, and record and report.
- **Maintenance staff** — keep floors, lighting, rails, call bells and equipment safe.

## 9. Training

All staff learn falls prevention, post-fall management and moving and handling during induction, and refresh them every year. First aid training covers head injuries and resuscitation. Staff are trained and assessed on the home's hoists and lifting cushion before using them, and shift leaders on post-fall observations. Competence is recorded on each person's file.

## 10. Records

Falls risk assessments, care plans, post-fall observations, incident records, reviews, referrals and the monthly falls analysis are kept on the resident's record and through the concerns process, for as long as our retention schedule requires.
