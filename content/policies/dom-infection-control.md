## 1. Purpose

An infection that would be minor for most people can put a frail client in hospital, and care workers who visit several homes a day can carry infection between them without knowing it. This policy sets out the everyday precautions {{organisation}} takes, at every visit and between visits, to stop infection spreading.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us, at every visit, in our office and between visits. It covers the precautions we take with every client, whether or not they are known to have an infection. What we do in addition when an outbreak is suspected is in our Outbreak Management policy.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008: Code of Practice on the prevention and control of infections and related guidance — what registered providers must do to prevent and control infection, which CQC takes into account when it inspects.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, which includes assessing the risk of, and preventing, detecting and controlling the spread of, infection.
- UK Health Security Agency, Infection prevention and control: resource for adult social care — the standard precautions, and when staff who are unwell should stay away from work.
- Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 (COSHH) and the Personal Protective Equipment at Work Regulations 1992 — protecting staff from germs and harmful products, with protective equipment provided free.
- Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR) — some infections caught at work, and some sharps injuries, must be reported to the Health and Safety Executive.
- Environmental Protection Act 1990, section 34 — the duty of care for waste: any waste we produce or remove is stored safely and passed only to someone authorised to take it.

## 4. Our policy

- Every care worker uses the standard precautions — hand hygiene, protective equipment, safe handling of waste, laundry and spills — at every visit, for every client.
- We provide protective equipment and hand rub free of charge.
- {{registered_manager}} is our infection prevention and control lead, or names a trained senior to be the lead.
- Care workers who are unwell with an infection stay away from work, and are not penalised for doing so.
- We offer and encourage the vaccinations staff are eligible for.

## 5. At every visit

### 5.1 Hand hygiene

Clean your hands on arrival, before and after personal care, after taking off gloves, before preparing food or giving medicines, after handling waste, laundry or bodily fluids, and before you leave. Use liquid soap, water and paper towels when your hands are visibly dirty, after contact with diarrhoea or vomit, and when a client has Clostridioides difficile (C. diff), which hand rub does not kill. Otherwise, use alcohol hand rub. Keep your nails short, be bare below the elbows during care, and cover cuts with a waterproof dressing (see Code of Conduct, Professional Boundaries and Dress).

### 5.2 Protective equipment

- Wear disposable gloves and an apron for personal care, continence care, and any contact with blood, bodily fluids, broken skin or soiled laundry.
- Put on a fluid-resistant mask and eye protection when there is a risk of splashing, or when the care plan or current guidance says so for a client with an infection.
- Use a fresh set for each client and each task. Take off gloves first, then the apron, then the mask, and clean your hands.
- Never wear protective equipment from one home into the next.

### 5.3 Sharps

Sharps in a client's home, such as insulin pen needles, are usually the client's own. Handle them only as part of a delegated task you have been trained for (see Delegated Healthcare Tasks). Never re-sheathe a needle. Put it straight into the client's sharps bin, and close the bin when it reaches the fill line. The office helps the client arrange collection with their pharmacy, GP or council.

If you are injured by a used needle or splashed with blood in the eyes or mouth, wash the area with running water and soap, encourage a puncture to bleed, and get medical advice straight away from the nearest emergency department, as treatment may need to start quickly. Then tell the office and record it through the site's concerns process.

### 5.4 Spills of blood and bodily fluids

Put on gloves and an apron, soak up the spill with paper towels, clean with detergent and water, then disinfect with the product the care plan names, following its label (see COSHH). Bag the waste and put it in the client's household waste.

### 5.5 Laundry

Where laundry is part of the care plan, handle soiled items with gloves and an apron. Do not shake them. Carry them away from your uniform straight to the washing machine, removing any solid matter into the toilet first. Wash them separately, at the hottest temperature the fabric allows. Never take a client's laundry home.

### 5.6 Waste

Used gloves, aprons and continence pads from a client without a known infection are bagged and put in the client's household waste, as their council allows. Where a client has an infection, or a nurse produces clinical waste in the home, the office agrees with the council or the health professional how it will be collected. Care workers do not carry clinical waste in their cars.

### 5.7 Cleaning and equipment

Clean what you use: surfaces you have prepared food or care on, commodes, and the client's equipment, such as hoists and slings, after use and as the care plan says. Clean any equipment you carry between clients, such as a thermometer, before and after each use. Keep your bag off the floor and take in only what you need.

## 6. Between visits

- Collect supplies from the office, and tell the office before you run low. Keep them in a clean, closed box in your car, away from anything dirty.
- Wear a clean uniform for every shift, change out of it at the end of the day where you can, and wash it at the hottest temperature the fabric allows.
- Carry hand rub, and use it when you leave a home and before you eat.

## 7. Clients with an infection

When a client has, or may have, an infection — such as diarrhoea and vomiting, a respiratory infection, MRSA or C. diff — the care plan sets out any extra precautions. Where possible, the office sends a small number of the same care workers, and plans the visit at the end of a run. Information about the infection is shared only with those who need it, such as the district nurse.

Report new signs of infection — a high temperature, a new cough, diarrhoea or vomiting, a red or weeping wound, new confusion — to the office the same day, so the GP can be asked for advice.

## 8. If you suspect an outbreak

If two or more clients, or clients and care workers, on the same run have similar symptoms within a few days, tell the office or on-call the same day. The registered manager contacts the UK Health Security Agency health protection team, and our Outbreak Management policy is followed from then on.

## 9. Staff health

- Do not come to work with diarrhoea or vomiting until 48 hours after your last symptoms.
- If you have symptoms of a respiratory infection and a high temperature, or do not feel well enough to work, stay at home and follow current UK Health Security Agency guidance on when to return.
- Tell the office about any skin infection on your hands or arms before you work.
- Report absence as our Sickness Absence policy sets out. Absence caused by an infection we asked you to stay home with is not held against you.

We offer and encourage the vaccinations staff are eligible for under the national programmes, such as flu, and tell staff where and when to get them. Where the COSHH assessment shows a risk of contact with blood, we offer hepatitis B vaccination free of charge. Vaccination is a personal choice, and no one is treated less favourably for declining.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — leads infection prevention and control, or names the lead, and makes sure supplies, training and risk assessments are in place.
- **Office and on-call staff** — keep supplies stocked, plan visits to reduce the spread of infection, and pass on precautions before a visit.
- **Care workers** — follow the standard precautions at every visit, report signs of infection, and stay away from work when unwell.

## 11. Training

All staff complete infection prevention and control training at induction, as part of the Care Certificate, and refresh it every year. Hand hygiene and putting on and taking off protective equipment are observed in spot checks, recorded on the care worker's file. Training is recorded on the training matrix.

## 12. Records

The care plan records each client's infection risks and precautions. Sharps injuries, and infections staff may have caught at work, are recorded through the site's concerns process. All are kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
