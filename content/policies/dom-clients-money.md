## 1. Purpose

Some clients need help with shopping, paying bills or collecting pensions, and they trust our care workers with their money. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} handles clients' money so that every penny is accounted for, clients stay in control of their own finances, care workers are protected from suspicion, and financial abuse is spotted and stopped.

## 2. Scope

It applies to every care worker, senior and office worker, and to any money, bank card, cheque, voucher or valuable belonging to a client that we handle in any way.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Care Act 2014 and the Care and Support Statutory Guidance — financial and material abuse is a form of abuse the local authority must make enquiries about.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — lasting powers of attorney for property and financial affairs, deputies appointed by the Court of Protection, and decisions in a person's best interests.
- Social Security (Claims and Payments) Regulations 1987 — the Department for Work and Pensions can appoint an appointee to manage a person's benefits.
- Fraud Act 2006 and Theft Act 1968 — dishonestly using a position of trust to make a gain, or taking another person's property, are crimes.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 13: Safeguarding service users from abuse and improper treatment, and Regulation 17: Good governance.

## 4. Our policy

- Clients manage their own money wherever they can, with the help they choose.
- Care workers handle a client's money only when the care plan says so, with the agreement of the client or the person who manages their money for them.
- Every transaction is recorded and backed by a receipt.
- Care workers never use a client's bank card or PIN, except under an agreed written arrangement approved by the registered manager.
- Care workers never borrow from, lend to, buy from, sell to, or take any financial advantage from a client.
- We act on every concern about financial abuse, whoever it involves.

## 5. Agreeing what help is needed

At assessment, and at every review, the registered manager or a senior asks the client what help they want with money, and who else helps them. The care plan records exactly what care workers may do — for example, weekly shopping up to an agreed amount, or collecting a prescription and paying the charge — and who the client's attorney, deputy or appointee is, if they have one. Check the document or ask for proof; an attorney's authority can be checked on the Office of the Public Guardian register.

Where a client lacks capacity to manage their money and has nobody with authority to do it, tell the registered manager, who will raise it with the local authority. We do not become anyone's attorney, deputy or appointee.

## 6. What to do

### 6.1 Shopping

- Agree the shopping list with the client, and write it on the shopping record sheet.
- Count the money with the client, and both sign the record sheet for the amount you are given. If the client cannot sign, record why, and ask a family member or colleague to witness where you can.
- Keep the client's money separate from your own, in a purse or envelope.
- Use only the shops agreed, and do not use your own loyalty card or collect points or rewards on a client's shopping.
- Get a receipt for every purchase. If a shop cannot give one, write down what you bought and the price, and tell the office.
- When you return, put the shopping away with the client, count the change with them, attach the receipt to the record sheet, and both sign for the change.

### 6.2 Bank cards and PINs

Care workers must not use a client's bank card, credit card or PIN, use their online banking, or withdraw cash for them, except under an agreed written arrangement. The arrangement is made only where there is no reasonable alternative, is agreed with the client (or their attorney or deputy) and approved in writing by the registered manager, and says exactly what the card may be used for, up to what amount, and how often. Each use is recorded on the record sheet with the receipt or slip. The card is returned to the client straight away. The PIN is never written down by the care worker or kept with the card.

### 6.3 Bills, pensions and post

Care workers may pay a bill or collect a pension only where the care plan says so, following the same steps as for shopping. Do not open a client's post unless they ask you to, and never sign anything on a client's behalf.

### 6.4 What care workers must never do

- Borrow money or property from a client, or lend them your own.
- Buy anything from a client, sell them anything, or take part in a raffle, lottery or collection with them.
- Accept money, a gift or a loan, except as our Gifts, Gratuities and Legacies policy allows.
- Hold or keep a client's money, cards or valuables overnight or at your home.
- Act as a client's attorney, deputy, appointee or executor, or witness their will or a financial document.
- Discuss your own money troubles with a client.

## 7. Checking and auditing

Shopping record sheets are kept in the client's home with their care records. A senior or the registered manager checks a sample every month, and at spot checks, against the receipts, and the completed sheets are brought back to the office. Any discrepancy is looked into straight away. Missing money, an unexplained gap or a record that does not add up is never put right by a care worker from their own pocket; it is reported.

## 8. Financial abuse

Financial abuse includes theft, fraud, scams, pressure about wills or property, misuse of a power of attorney or benefits, and taking money or belongings without consent. It may be committed by family, friends, neighbours, strangers, doorstep traders, other professionals, or our own staff.

Signs include unpaid bills or lack of food when the client should have enough money, belongings going missing, a new "friend" taking an interest in the client's finances, unusual letters or phone calls, or a client who seems frightened about money.

If you suspect financial abuse, tell the office or the registered manager the same day and record what you have seen. Do not confront anyone. The registered manager raises a safeguarding concern with the local authority, informs the police where a crime may have been committed, and records it through the site's concerns process. Where the abuse involves an attorney or deputy, it may also be reported to the Office of the Public Guardian. Allegations against our staff are dealt with under our Safeguarding and Disciplinary policies.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — agrees and approves what help clients receive with money, approves any bank card arrangement in writing, audits records, and acts on concerns.
- **Seniors** — record what help is agreed in the care plan, check record sheets and receipts, and report discrepancies.
- **Care workers** — handle money only as the care plan says, record every transaction with a receipt, keep within this policy, and report concerns.

## 10. Training

All care workers learn about handling clients' money and recognising financial abuse during induction, as part of safeguarding training, and refresh it every year. Spot checks include how money is handled and recorded.

## 11. Records

Shopping and money record sheets with receipts, and any written bank card arrangement, are kept on the client's care record for as long as our record retention schedule requires. Audits and concerns are kept through the concerns process.
