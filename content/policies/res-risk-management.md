## 1. Purpose

Risk is part of everyday life, including life in a care home. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} identifies and manages risks to our residents, our staff, our building and our business, so that residents are kept safe without being stopped from living the lives they choose, and so that the home can keep running when things go wrong.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works in the home, on every shift. It covers four kinds of risk:

- risks to residents in their care and daily life;
- risks to staff in their work;
- risks from the building, its grounds and its equipment, to residents, staff and visitors;
- risks to the business — anything that could stop us providing safe, good care.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — the duty to assess risks to staff and everyone else affected by our work, including residents and visitors, and to record the significant findings where we have five or more employees.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment — assessing and doing all that is reasonably practicable to reduce risks to residents; Regulation 15: Premises and equipment; Regulation 17: Good governance — assessing, monitoring and reducing risks to the service.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — a resident with capacity can choose to take risks; for a resident who lacks capacity, decisions about risk are made in their best interests and in the least restrictive way.
- Care Act 2014 — promoting wellbeing, including the person's control over their own life.
- Health and Safety Executive guidance on risk assessment, and Health and safety in care homes.

## 4. Our policy

- Risk assessment is a way of helping residents do things safely, not a reason to stop them doing things.
- We assess risks before a resident moves in, review them regularly, and review them whenever anything changes or something goes wrong.
- We involve the resident, and the people who matter to them, in every assessment about their care.
- We record what we find, what we will do about it, and who is responsible.
- Risks we cannot manage on our own are raised with those who can help — the resident's family, health professionals, the local authority or our senior managers.

## 5. How we assess risk

Every risk assessment follows the same steps:

- identify the hazards — anything that could cause harm;
- decide who could be harmed, and how;
- weigh up how likely harm is and how serious it would be, and decide what needs to be done to reduce it;
- record the findings and the actions, and tell the people who need to know, including staff on every shift;
- review the assessment at the date set, and sooner if anything changes.

We describe each risk as low, medium or high, taking account of the controls already in place, and we act first on the highest.

## 6. Risks to residents

### 6.1 Before and after a resident moves in

The pre-admission assessment identifies the main risks before we offer a place. Before the resident moves in, a senior writes the key risk assessments from it, so that staff on every shift know how to keep them safe from the first hour (see Assessment and Starting a Service). Over the first weeks they are completed with the resident: moving and handling, falls, pressure areas, nutrition and choking, medicines, continence and skin, going missing, smoking, and anything else their assessment shows. Every resident also has a personal emergency evacuation plan (see Fire Safety). What care staff need to do is written into the care plan in plain words.

### 6.2 Risks particular to living in a care home

Residents share a building, and some cannot recognise danger. The care plan sets out how we manage risks such as: bed rails, used only after an individual assessment (see Equipment and Maintenance); hot water and surfaces (see Legionella and Water Safety); hazardous substances (see COSHH); a resident whose distress or behaviour may put others at risk (see Positive Behaviour Support); and a resident who may leave the home without staff knowing (see Missing Residents).

### 6.3 Positive risk-taking

Residents have the right to make their own choices, including choices we would not make. A resident with capacity who wants to go to the shops alone, keep their own medicines or eat food that has been advised against is supported to do so as safely as possible. We talk through the risks with them, agree what would help, and record their decision. If a resident may lack capacity to decide about a significant risk, their capacity is assessed and, if they lack it, a best interests decision is made. See Choice and Positive Risk-Taking.

### 6.4 Restrictions

Some ways of reducing a risk — a locked door, a sensor mat, a bed rail, constant supervision — also restrict a resident's freedom. They are used only when they are necessary, proportionate and the least restrictive option, and are recorded and reviewed. Where restrictions on a resident who lacks capacity may amount to a deprivation of liberty, the registered manager considers whether a Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards authorisation is needed. See Restraint and Restrictive Practice and Deprivation of Liberty.

### 6.5 Reviewing resident risk assessments

Risk assessments are checked with the care plan every month, fully reviewed at least every year, and reviewed straight away when:

- the resident's health, needs or behaviour change;
- staff report a new hazard;
- there is an accident, incident or near miss;
- the resident returns from hospital.

Care staff are often first to notice a change. Tell the shift leader the same day.

## 7. Risks to staff

The registered manager keeps general risk assessments for the work staff do — moving and handling, infection, hazardous substances, violence and aggression, night working, kitchen, laundry and maintenance work — and individual ones where needed, for example for a new or expectant mother, a worker under 18, or someone with a health condition. These are reviewed every year and after any incident.

## 8. Risks from the building

The registered manager keeps a written premises risk assessment, and the specialist assessments that go with it: fire, legionella, COSHH and, where the building may contain asbestos, an asbestos survey. Each has its own policy and review date, and the maintenance schedule makes sure the checks they require are done (see Health and Safety).

## 9. Risks to the business

### 9.1 The risk register

{{registered_manager}} and {{nominated_individual}} keep a register of the main risks to the business, such as:

- not having enough staff on every shift, relying heavily on agency staff, or losing key people;
- empty rooms, fee levels, or a resident mix that leaves us dependent on one funder;
- cash flow and rising costs;
- failure of the building or its services — heating, hot water, the lift, the nurse call system, the kitchen;
- failure of our phones or IT systems, or a cyber attack or data breach;
- an outbreak, severe weather or another emergency;
- not meeting regulatory requirements, or a poor inspection;
- damage to our reputation.

For each risk, the register records how likely and how serious it is, the controls in place, who owns it, and what more we will do.

### 9.2 Reviewing the register

The register is reviewed by the registered manager and the nominated individual at least every three months, and whenever something new arises. Risks that could stop us providing care are planned for in our Business Continuity and Emergency Planning policy.

## 10. Nights and weekends

When managers are not in the building, the shift leader deals with new risks as they arise: they make the resident or the area safe, record what they did, and phone the on-call manager for anything they cannot resolve. The risk assessment is then reviewed on the next working day.

## 11. Learning

Accidents, incidents, near misses, complaints, safeguarding concerns and audits all tell us about risks we may have missed. The registered manager looks at them every month, and updates risk assessments, the register and care plans as a result.

## 12. Roles and responsibilities

- **Nominated individual** — makes sure business risks are identified and managed, and that resources are available to control them.
- **Registered manager** — makes sure every risk assessment is done, recorded, communicated and reviewed.
- **Seniors and shift leaders** — carry out resident risk assessments and reviews when trained to, and manage new risks on their shift.
- **Care staff** — follow care plans and risk assessments, and report new hazards and changes straight away.

## 13. Training

All staff learn about risk and how to report hazards at induction, and refresh it every year with their health and safety training. Staff who carry out risk assessments are trained to do so, and their assessments are checked by the registered manager until they are competent; this is recorded on their file.

## 14. Records

Resident risk assessments are kept with the care plan. Staff and premises risk assessments and the business risk register are kept by the registered manager. Accidents and incidents are kept through the site's concerns process. All are kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
