## 1. Purpose

Phones and apps are part of care work: staff use them to log visits, read care plans, complete training and stay in touch. They also make it easy to share something that should never be shared. This policy sets out how staff at {{organisation}} use social media, phones and other technology so that the privacy and dignity of the people we support are protected, and so are staff.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all staff, whether using a company device or their own, at work or in their own time, on any social media, messaging app, website or artificial intelligence tool.

## 3. The law and guidance

- UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 — personal information, especially about health, is kept secure and used only for the purpose it was collected for.
- Common law duty of confidentiality.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 10: Dignity and respect.
- Human Rights Act 1998 — the right to respect for private and family life, for the people we support and for staff.
- Road Vehicles (Construction and Use) Regulations 1986 — it is an offence to hold and use a phone while driving.
- Code of Conduct for Healthcare Support Workers and Adult Social Care Workers in England.

## 4. Our policy

- Never post, share or send anything that identifies, or could identify, a person we support — no names, photographs, videos, addresses or details of their care — anywhere outside our own systems.
- Never take a photograph, video or audio recording of a person we support on your own phone.
- Do not connect with or message the people we support, or their families, on social media or personal messaging apps.
- What you say online in your own time can still affect your work. Do not post anything that brings the company into disrepute, or that is abusive, discriminatory or bullying about colleagues, the people we support or the company.
- Use the systems the company provides for work. Care information belongs in care records, not in personal apps.

## 5. What to do

### 5.1 Using your phone at work

Your phone is a work tool for logging visits or tasks, reading care plans, contacting the office and completing training. Keep it on silent during care. Do not make or take personal calls, or use social media, while you are supporting someone, except in an emergency. In homecare, make personal calls between visits, not in a client's home. In a care home, use your phone for personal things only on your break, away from residents.

Lock your phone with a passcode or biometric, keep its software up to date, and do not let others use it while you are logged in to work systems. If a phone you use for work is lost or stolen, tell the office straight away, so access can be removed (see Data Security and Breaches).

### 5.2 Messaging

Messages about the people we support go only through the systems the company has approved. Do not discuss them in personal messaging groups, even with colleagues. If the company uses a messaging group for rotas or general updates, it must not contain anything about a person's care beyond what is needed — for example "visit moved to 10am", not details of their health.

> If you use a messaging app for rotas or staff updates, name it and say what it may and may not be used for. Remove this if you do not.

### 5.3 Photographs and recordings

Photographs may be needed for care — for example of a wound or skin damage for a health professional, or for a person's own records. Take them only with the person's consent (or, if they lack capacity, in their best interests), only on a company device or system approved for the purpose, and store them in their care record. Delete any copy from the device.

Photographs for activities, newsletters or social media need the person's written consent for that purpose, and the company's agreement. Only the company's official accounts post them.

### 5.4 Social media in your own time

- Keep your privacy settings high, and think before you post.
- Do not say where you work if you post things you would not want the people we support to see.
- Do not post about work incidents, complaints, or difficult days with the people we support, even without names: details can identify someone.
- Do not speak for the company online unless you have been asked to.
- If a person we support, or a relative, sends you a friend request or message, do not accept or reply; tell your manager.

### 5.5 Artificial intelligence tools

Do not put any information about the people we support, or about colleagues, into a public artificial intelligence tool, chatbot or translation app. Only tools the company has approved may be used with personal information, and only in the way approved.

### 5.6 Driving

Never hold or use your phone while driving, even when stopped in traffic. Use a hands-free cradle for navigation, set before you start. Pull over safely to make or take calls. See our Driving at Work policy.

### 5.7 People we support who record their care

Some people we support, or their families, use cameras or recording devices in their own homes or rooms. They may have good reasons. If you know of or find one, carry on working professionally, and tell your manager. Do not switch off, move or cover it.

## 6. If something goes wrong

If you post or send something by mistake, delete it if you can and tell your manager straight away. If you see a post by anyone that identifies a person we support or is abusive about them, tell your manager. Breaches of this policy may be dealt with under our Disciplinary policy, and some — such as sharing images of a person we support — are gross misconduct and may be a safeguarding matter.

## 7. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — decides which devices, apps and tools are approved, manages the company's official social media, and deals with breaches.
- **Managers and seniors** — set an example and act on concerns.
- **All staff** — follow this policy, keep their devices secure, and report problems.

## 8. Training

This policy is covered at induction and in data protection training, refreshed every year.

## 9. Records

Consent for photographs is recorded in the person's care record. Approved apps and devices are listed by the registered manager. Breaches are recorded as described in Data Security and Breaches.
