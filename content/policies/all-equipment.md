## 1. Purpose

Hoists, slings, beds, mattresses, wheelchairs and other equipment make care safer and more comfortable — but only when they are the right equipment, properly maintained and used by people who know how. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} makes sure the equipment our staff use is safe, who is responsible for it, and what to do when something is wrong with it.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us, and to all equipment used in our work, whoever owns it:

- equipment for moving and handling — hoists, slings, stand aids, transfer boards, slide sheets;
- beds, bed rails, pressure-relieving mattresses and cushions;
- walking aids, wheelchairs, bath and shower equipment, and commodes;
- medical devices — blood glucose meters, thermometers, blood pressure monitors, nebulisers, oxygen equipment;
- in a care home, the building's lifts, nurse call, kitchen and laundry equipment;
- office equipment and computers.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998 (PUWER) — equipment used at work must be suitable, maintained in a safe condition, inspected where needed, and used only by people who have been trained.
- Lifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations 1998 (LOLER) — lifting equipment must be strong and stable enough, used safely, and thoroughly examined by a competent person. Equipment used to lift people, and its accessories such as slings, must be thoroughly examined at least every six months, or in line with a written examination scheme.
- Medical Devices Regulations 2002 — the safety of medical devices.
- Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) guidance, Managing medical devices, and its safety alerts and guidance on bed rails.
- Electricity at Work Regulations 1989 — electrical equipment kept safe.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, and Regulation 15: Premises and equipment — equipment must be clean, suitable, properly used, maintained and stored.

## 4. Our policy

- Equipment is only used when it has been assessed as right for the person, and when the person using it has been trained.
- Equipment is checked before every use. Faulty or doubtful equipment is taken out of use straight away.
- Every piece of lifting equipment has a current thorough examination, and every piece of equipment is serviced as the manufacturer says.
- We act on every relevant safety alert.

## 5. Who owns and looks after equipment

### 5.1 In a person's own home

Equipment in a client's home usually belongs to someone else — most often the community equipment service run by the local authority and NHS, or the client, who may have bought it themselves. The owner is responsible for servicing it and arranging its thorough examination, and the community equipment service will usually do this for equipment it has loaned.

We are responsible for making sure that our care workers use only equipment that is safe. So:

- before care starts, the assessor records each piece of equipment, who owns it, and when it was last serviced or examined;
- where a hoist or sling has no current thorough examination, or none can be found, we contact the owner or the equipment service and do not use it until it is confirmed safe;
- where a client has bought equipment privately, we explain that it needs regular servicing and thorough examination, and help them arrange it;
- the office keeps a list of the examination dates for lifting equipment in clients' homes, and reminds the owner before they fall due.

### 5.2 In a care home

The home owns, or leases, most of the equipment residents use. The registered manager keeps an equipment register recording each item, where it is, its service and examination dates, and who services it. Lifts, hoists and slings are thoroughly examined by a competent person, usually through an insurance or specialist engineer, at the required intervals; other equipment is serviced as the manufacturer recommends. Residents' own equipment, such as a wheelchair, is added to the register when they move in.

> Name your equipment servicing and lifting examination contractors, if you use them.

### 5.3 Our own equipment

Equipment we provide to staff — such as thermometers, blood glucose meters, phones and laptops — is kept on a register, maintained, and calibrated or replaced as the manufacturer says.

## 6. Using equipment safely

### 6.1 The right equipment for the person

Equipment for moving and handling, pressure care and bed safety is chosen following an assessment, usually by an occupational therapist or physiotherapist. The care plan says which equipment is used, how, and with which sling or setting. Slings are for one person only and are labelled with their name, unless a single-use sling is used once and thrown away.

### 6.2 Before each use

Check the equipment before you use it:

- Is it the equipment the care plan says?
- Is it clean, complete and undamaged — no fraying, tears, cracks or loose parts?
- Does it have a label or record showing it is in date for its service or examination?
- For a hoist, is the battery charged, do the brakes and emergency lowering work, and does the sling's label show the right size and safe working load?
- For a pressure-relieving mattress, is it set to the right setting and working?

If the answer to any of these is no, do not use it.

### 6.3 Bed rails

Bed rails can prevent falls but can also trap and injure people. They are used only after a risk assessment, which considers whether the person wants them and whether they are safe for that person. Rails must fit the bed and mattress, and leave no gaps where a person's head, neck or limbs could be trapped. A care home checks them regularly; in home care, the care worker checks them on every visit. Any concern is reported at once.

## 7. When equipment is faulty

- Stop using it. If a person is in a hoist, lower them safely using the emergency lowering if needed.
- Make the person safe and comfortable another way.
- Label the equipment as not to be used, and take it out of use where you can.
- Tell the office or on-call (in home care) or the shift leader (in a care home) straight away.
- Record it through the site's concerns process.

The office or the registered manager contacts the owner or the servicing company to arrange repair or replacement, and changes the person's care plan until it is resolved. A failure of a load-bearing part of a hoist or other lifting equipment may need to be reported to the Health and Safety Executive (see Reportable Incidents). A serious incident involving a medical device is also reported to the MHRA through the Yellow Card scheme.

## 8. Safety alerts

The registered manager signs up to receive MHRA safety alerts and national patient safety alerts relevant to social care, checks every alert for equipment we use or that is used in clients' homes, acts on it within the time it gives, tells staff, and records what was done.

## 9. Cleaning and storage

Equipment is cleaned between uses and between people, as our Infection Prevention and Control policy sets out, and stored safely so that it is not damaged and does not create a trip hazard.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — keeps equipment registers, makes sure equipment is serviced and examined, acts on safety alerts, and makes sure staff are trained.
- **Assessors and senior staff** — record equipment and its examination status at assessment and review, and arrange equipment assessments where needed.
- **Office staff and shift leaders** — act on reports of faulty equipment the same day.
- **Care workers and care staff** — use only equipment they are trained to use, check it before each use, and report faults.

## 11. Training

All care staff are trained in moving and handling, including the use of hoists and slings, during induction, and refresh it every year. Staff are trained on any specialist equipment before they use it, and their competence is checked and recorded on their file. Training is recorded on the training matrix.

## 12. Records

Equipment registers, service and thorough examination reports, safety alert actions and fault reports are kept by the registered manager, for at least as long as the law requires for examination reports and for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention. Equipment used by each person is recorded in their care plan.
