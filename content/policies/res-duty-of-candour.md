## 1. Purpose

When something goes wrong in a resident's care, they and their family have a right to know what happened, to hear us say sorry, and to know what we will do about it. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} is open and honest with residents and families at all times, and how we meet the legal duty of candour when a resident is seriously harmed.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works in the home, on every shift, including agency staff. It covers every incident while we are providing a resident's care — in the home, on an outing or at an appointment.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 20: Duty of candour — a general duty to act in an open and transparent way with residents and the people acting for them, and a specific duty to follow set steps when a notifiable safety incident happens. Failing to notify the person, or to do so in the way the regulation requires, is a criminal offence.
- Compensation Act 2006, section 2 — an apology, or an offer of treatment or other redress, is not in itself an admission of negligence or breach of statutory duty.
- Care Quality Commission, guidance on Regulation 20: Duty of candour — how CQC expects the duty to be met.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — who can act for a resident who lacks capacity. Registered professionals, such as nurses, also have a professional duty of candour.

## 4. Our policy

- We are open with residents and families about anything that goes wrong, whether or not it caused harm, and whether or not it meets the threshold below. This is the general duty of openness.
- When a notifiable safety incident happens, we follow every step of the duty of candour, as soon as reasonably practicable.
- Saying sorry is the right thing to do. It is not an admission of liability, and nobody will be criticised for apologising.
- We support staff who report honestly. Covering up is not tolerated.

## 5. What a notifiable safety incident is

For a provider like us, which is not a health service body, a notifiable safety incident is an unintended or unexpected incident that happened to a resident while we were providing care, and that, in the reasonable opinion of a health care professional, either:

- appears to have resulted in the resident's death, where the death relates directly to the incident rather than to the natural course of their illness or condition;
- appears to have resulted in an impairment of the resident's sensory, motor or intellectual functions which has lasted, or is likely to last, for a continuous period of at least 28 days;
- appears to have resulted in changes to the structure of the resident's body, such as a fracture;
- appears to have resulted in the resident experiencing prolonged pain or prolonged psychological harm, meaning pain or harm they have experienced, or are likely to experience, for a continuous period of at least 28 days;
- appears to have resulted in the shortening of the resident's life expectancy; or
- requires treatment by a health care professional to prevent the resident's death, or to prevent an injury that, if untreated, would lead to one of the outcomes above.

The incident does not have to be anyone's fault. In a care home it might be a fall from a hoist or at night, a fracture found after a transfer, a medicines error needing hospital treatment, a scald, choking, a pressure ulcer that developed in our care, harm from another resident, or a resident injured after leaving the home unnoticed.

Our care staff are not health care professionals. Unless the registered manager is a registered health professional, they ask the resident's GP, the district nurse, the out-of-hours service or the hospital for their view of the harm, and record whose opinion was given. If the opinion is not yet clear, we follow the duty: it is better to be open too early than too late.

## 6. What to do

### 6.1 Straight away

- Make the resident safe, and get medical help if they need it. Call 999 in an emergency.
- Tell the shift leader at once. At night and weekends, the shift leader tells the on-call manager of any incident that has or may have caused serious harm.
- Be honest with the resident, and say you are sorry it happened.
- The shift leader tells the resident's next of kin or representative the same day, as the resident's wishes allow — at night, at a reasonable hour unless the resident's condition means calling at once. This first call is not the formal duty of candour conversation, but it must be honest.
- Record the incident through the site's concerns process before the end of your shift, as Accidents, Incidents and Near Misses sets out.

### 6.2 Deciding whether the duty applies

{{registered_manager}} reviews every incident report within one working day, and decides whether it may be a notifiable safety incident. The decision, the reasons and the health care professional's opinion are recorded with the incident, even where the answer is no.

### 6.3 Telling the resident or family, in person

As soon as reasonably practicable, the registered manager, or the deputy, tells the relevant person in person that the incident has happened. The relevant person is the resident or, where the resident has died or lacks capacity to deal with the matter, a person lawfully acting on their behalf, such as an attorney, a deputy or the next of kin. Where a resident lacks capacity, we still explain what happened to them in a way they can understand.

Our standard is to do this within two working days of deciding that the duty applies, in a private room in the home or wherever suits the family. The conversation:

- gives a true account of all the facts we know about the incident at that point;
- explains what further enquiries we will make;
- includes an apology — a sincere expression of sorrow or regret for what happened;
- offers reasonable support, such as an advocate or interpreter, help to understand what the hospital has said, or contacts for other help.

It is recorded in writing and kept securely.

### 6.4 Writing to them

The in-person conversation is followed by a letter to the relevant person. Our standard is to send it within ten working days of the conversation. It sets out what was said: the facts as we know them, the enquiries we will make, the results of any enquiries already done, and an apology. When our enquiries are complete, we write again with the findings and what we are changing. We keep a copy of every letter.

### 6.5 If we cannot reach them

If the relevant person cannot be contacted, or does not wish to speak to us, we respect that, and record every attempt to contact or speak to them. They can get in touch with us at any time.

## 7. How the duty fits with other processes

The duty runs alongside other processes, and does not replace them:

- the incident is investigated under Accidents, Incidents and Near Misses;
- abuse or neglect, including harm from another resident, is referred to the local authority under Safeguarding;
- deaths, serious injuries and other events are notified to CQC under Regulations 16 and 18 of the Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, as Reportable Incidents sets out. A CQC notification does not meet the duty of candour, and the duty of candour does not replace a notification;
- where a death is reported to the coroner, we cooperate with the coroner's enquiries;
- a complaint is handled under Complaints.

Where the police, the coroner or a safeguarding enquiry are involved, the registered manager agrees with them what can be shared. We still tell the relevant person that the incident happened and apologise.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — decides whether the duty applies, holds or oversees each conversation, sends the letters, and makes sure records are complete.
- **Nominated individual** — makes sure the company meets the duty, and checks compliance through our governance checks.
- **Shift leaders and the on-call manager** — make residents safe, tell families honestly on the day, and tell the registered manager.
- **Care staff** — report every incident at once, and are honest with residents and families.

## 9. Training

All staff learn about openness and the duty of candour at induction, and refresh it every year with incident reporting. Shift leaders are trained in what to say to families on the day, and anyone who holds duty of candour conversations in the regulation and in difficult conversations.

## 10. Records

The decision on the duty, the health care professional's opinion, the record of the conversation, copies of every letter and records of attempts to make contact are kept with the incident through the site's concerns process, for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention, and reviewed every month with all incidents.
