## 1. Purpose

Risk is part of everyday life, and part of everyday care. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} identifies and manages risks to our clients, our staff and our business, so that people are kept safe without being stopped from living the lives they choose, and so that the company can keep providing care when things go wrong.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us. It covers three kinds of risk:

- risks to clients in their care and in their homes;
- risks to staff in their work;
- risks to the business — anything that could stop us providing safe, good care.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — the duty to assess risks to staff and others affected by our work, and to record the significant findings where we have five or more employees.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment — assessing and doing all that is reasonably practicable to reduce risks to clients; Regulation 17: Good governance — assessing, monitoring and reducing risks to the service.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — a person with capacity can choose to take risks; for a person who lacks capacity, decisions about risk are made in their best interests and in the least restrictive way.
- Care Act 2014 — promoting wellbeing, including the person's control over their own life.
- Health and Safety Executive guidance on risk assessment.

## 4. Our policy

- Risk assessment is a way of helping people do things safely, not a reason to stop them doing things.
- We assess risks before care starts, review them regularly, and review them whenever anything changes or something goes wrong.
- We involve the client, and the people who matter to them, in every assessment about their care.
- We record what we find, what we will do about it, and who is responsible.
- Risks we cannot manage are raised with the people who can help — the client, their family, the local authority, health professionals or our own senior managers.

## 5. How we assess risk

Every risk assessment follows the same steps:

- identify the hazards — anything that could cause harm;
- decide who could be harmed, and how;
- weigh up how likely harm is and how serious it would be, and decide what needs to be done to reduce it;
- record the findings and the actions, and tell the people who need to know;
- review the assessment at the date set, and sooner if anything changes.

We describe each risk as low, medium or high, taking account of the controls already in place, and we act first on the highest.

## 6. Risks to clients

### 6.1 Before care starts

Every new client has, before their first visit:

- an environmental risk assessment of their home and its surroundings — access, lighting, stairs and steps, floors, heating, appliances the care worker will use, smoke alarms, smoking, pets, the condition of the property and anyone else living there;
- individual risk assessments for the care they need — moving and handling, falls, pressure areas, nutrition and choking, medicines, skin and continence, and anything else their assessment shows;
- a note of the risks the client chooses to take, and how we will support them.

The registered manager or a trained senior carries out the assessments, ideally in the client's home with the client present. What care workers need to know and do is written into the care plan in plain words.

### 6.2 Fire risk in the home

We do not control a client's home, but some clients are at high risk from fire — people who smoke, use oxygen, use emollient creams that soak into clothing and bedding, live alone, or cannot get out quickly. We check whether there is a working smoke alarm, explain the risks to the client, and, with their agreement, ask the local fire and rescue service for a home fire safety visit.

### 6.3 Positive risk-taking

Clients have the right to make their own choices, including choices we would not make. A client with capacity who wants to keep using their stairs, go out alone or cook for themselves is supported to do so as safely as possible. We talk through the risks with them, agree what would help, and record their decision. If a client may lack capacity to decide about a significant risk, a capacity assessment and best interests decision are made. See Choice and Positive Risk-Taking.

### 6.4 When the risk is to our staff

Some risks in a client's home affect our staff more than the client — a dangerous dog, a smoker who smokes during visits, an unsafe hoist. We agree with the client how the risk will be controlled. If it cannot be made acceptable, we may change how or when visits happen, use two workers, or, as a last resort, stop part of the care. See Health and Safety and Lone Working.

### 6.5 Reviewing client risk assessments

Risk assessments are reviewed with the care plan at least every year, and sooner when:

- the client's health, needs or home change;
- a care worker reports a new hazard;
- there is an accident, incident or near miss;
- the client goes into or comes out of hospital.

Care workers are the eyes of the service. Tell the office about any change you see, the same day.

## 7. Risks to staff

The registered manager keeps general risk assessments for the work our staff do — lone working, driving, moving and handling, infection, violence and aggression, and the office — and makes individual risk assessments where they are needed, for example for a new or expectant mother or a member of staff with a health condition. These are reviewed every year and after any incident.

## 8. Risks to the business

### 8.1 The risk register

{{registered_manager}} and {{nominated_individual}} keep a register of the main risks to the business, such as:

- not having enough staff to cover visits, or losing key people;
- loss or reduction of a commissioning contract, or a client mix that leaves us dependent on one funder;
- cash flow and rising costs;
- failure of our phones, IT systems or the systems we use to plan visits, or a cyber attack or data breach;
- severe weather, an outbreak, or another emergency affecting many clients;
- not meeting regulatory requirements, or a poor inspection;
- damage to our reputation.

For each risk, the register records how likely it is, how serious it would be, what we do to control it, who owns it, and what more we will do.

### 8.2 Reviewing the register

The register is reviewed by the registered manager and the nominated individual at least every three months, and whenever something new arises. Risks that could stop us providing care are planned for in our Business Continuity and Emergency Planning policy.

## 9. Learning

Accidents, incidents, near misses, complaints, safeguarding concerns and audits all tell us about risks we may have missed. The registered manager looks at them every month, and updates risk assessments, the register and care plans as a result.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Nominated individual** — makes sure business risks are identified and managed, and that resources are available to control them.
- **Registered manager** — makes sure every client, staff and business risk assessment is done, recorded, communicated and reviewed.
- **Senior staff and assessors** — carry out client risk assessments and reviews when trained to.
- **Office and on-call staff** — make sure care workers know about risks before they visit, and pass on new risks reported to them.
- **Care workers** — follow the care plan and risk assessments, and report new hazards and changes straight away.

## 11. Training

All staff learn about risk and how to report hazards at induction, and refresh it every year with their health and safety training. Staff who carry out risk assessments are trained to do so, and their assessments are checked by the registered manager until they are competent; this is recorded on their file.

## 12. Records

Client risk assessments are kept on the client's record. Staff risk assessments and the business risk register are kept by the registered manager. Accidents and incidents are kept through the site's concerns process. All are kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
