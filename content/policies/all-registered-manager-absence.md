## 1. Purpose

{{organisation}} must be well led every day, including when the registered manager is on leave, ill, or leaves the company. This policy sets out who manages the service when {{registered_manager}} is away, what they may decide, how we tell CQC about longer absences, and what happens if the registered manager leaves.

## 2. Scope

It applies to the registered manager, the nominated individual, the directors, and anyone who deputises for the registered manager, for any absence — a day off, annual leave, sickness, training, family leave, suspension or leaving.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 — a registered manager is a condition of registration for the regulated activity we provide; being without one, other than for a short time while a new manager is appointed, may lead to CQC action.
- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, Regulation 14: Notice of absence — the provider must tell CQC when the registered manager is to be, or has been, absent for a continuous period of 28 days or more, and the arrangements made for running the service, and tell CQC again when they return.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 17: Good governance — the service is well led, with systems that continue whoever is on duty.
- Regulation 7: Requirements relating to registered managers, and Regulation 18: Staffing.

## 4. Our policy

- There is always a named person in charge of the service, and everyone knows who it is.
- The registered manager's duties are planned to continue in their absence, with a named deputy who has the skills, training and authority to cover.
- CQC is told of an absence of 28 days or more, within the time the regulation sets.
- If the registered manager leaves, we appoint a new manager, who applies to CQC to register, as quickly as we can.

## 5. The deputy

The registered manager's deputy is named, with their role:

The deputy:

- knows the service, the people we support and the staff;
- has the training and experience to manage day to day, including safeguarding, medicines oversight, rotas and handling complaints;
- has access to everything they need on the site and other systems, with the permissions to act;
- meets the registered manager regularly so that they are ready to step in.

## 6. Short absences

For absences of less than 28 days — leave, sickness, training — the registered manager, or the nominated individual if the absence is unplanned, makes sure:

- the deputy knows they are in charge, from when, and until when;
- staff, and where relevant commissioners, know who to contact;
- the deputy has a handover of anything urgent: safeguarding enquiries, complaints, incidents under investigation, people whose needs are changing, staffing gaps and deadlines;
- on-call cover is arranged.

The deputy has authority to make the day-to-day decisions the registered manager would make. Decisions outside that — for example dismissing someone, ending a person's service, or taking on a large new contract — are discussed with the nominated individual first, unless delay would put someone at risk.

## 7. Absences of 28 days or more

### 7.1 Planned absence

When the registered manager plans to be away for 28 days or more — for example for family leave or a long holiday — the nominated individual tells CQC in advance, within the time Regulation 14 sets, using CQC's notification form. The notice says how long the absence will be, why, and who will manage the service and how.

### 7.2 Unplanned absence

When the registered manager is away unexpectedly — for example through sudden illness — and the absence reaches, or is expected to reach, 28 days, the nominated individual tells CQC as soon as possible and within the time Regulation 14 sets.

### 7.3 Return

When the registered manager returns, the nominated individual tells CQC within the time the regulation sets.

### 7.4 During a long absence

The nominated individual increases their oversight of the service: they meet the deputy regularly, review key information such as incidents, safeguarding, complaints, staffing, training and audits, and make sure the deputy has the support they need. Where the deputy cannot cover everything, the nominated individual arranges extra support, such as an interim manager.

## 8. If the registered manager leaves

When the registered manager resigns, is dismissed or otherwise leaves:

- the nominated individual tells CQC that they have left, as CQC requires, and who is managing the service in the meantime;
- the registered manager applies to CQC to cancel their registration for the service;
- the deputy or an interim manager takes charge, with the support set out in section 7.4;
- we recruit a new manager, with the checks in our Fit and Proper Persons policy, and they apply to CQC to register as soon as they are appointed;
- the outgoing manager hands over open matters, records and access, and their access to systems is removed when they leave.

## 9. Statutory notifications during absence

Notifications to CQC — of deaths, serious injuries, abuse or allegations of abuse, police involvement and other incidents under Regulations 16 and 18 of the Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009 — must still be made, without delay, while the registered manager is away. The deputy makes them, or makes sure the nominated individual does. See Reportable Incidents.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — plans for their absences, keeps their deputy ready, and hands over before planned absence.
- **Nominated individual** — makes sure there is always someone in charge, tells CQC of absences and changes, and oversees the service during long absences.
- **Deputy** — manages the service in the registered manager's absence, within the authority given, and escalates what they should.

## 11. Training

The deputy is trained in the parts of the registered manager's role they will cover, including making CQC notifications, safeguarding referrals and handling complaints, and in this policy.

## 12. Records

Absences of 28 days or more, notices sent to CQC and their dates, handovers and the arrangements made are recorded and kept for inspection.
