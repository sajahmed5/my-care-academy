## 1. Purpose

Cameras can help keep a care home safe, but they also film people in their home, often at their most private. This policy sets out when {{organisation}} uses CCTV and other surveillance in the home, how we make sure it is lawful, necessary and respectful of residents' privacy, and what we do when a resident or family wants to use a camera of their own.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all surveillance in and around the home: fixed CCTV, video doorbells, cameras in communal areas, audio recording, acoustic monitoring and any camera in a resident's room, whether installed by us or by a resident or their family. Equipment used only for an individual's care, such as a pressure mat or door sensor, is covered in their care plan, but the same principles apply.

## 3. The law and guidance

- UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 — images of identifiable people are personal information. We must have a lawful basis, use surveillance only when necessary and proportionate, complete a data protection impact assessment, tell people, keep footage secure and for no longer than needed, and respond to requests for it.
- Information Commissioner's Office guidance on video surveillance, including CCTV.
- Human Rights Act 1998, Article 8 — the right to respect for private and family life, for residents, visitors and staff.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 10: Dignity and respect, Regulation 13: Safeguarding, and Regulation 17: Good governance.
- Care Quality Commission guidance for providers on using surveillance in care services, and its information for the public on using cameras to check someone's care.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — where a resident cannot consent to surveillance that affects them, any decision is made in their best interests.
- Surveillance Camera Code of Practice — written for public bodies; we follow its principles as good practice.

## 4. Our policy

- Surveillance is used only for a clear purpose — such as the security of the building, the safety of residents and staff, or the prevention and detection of crime — and only where less intrusive ways would not achieve it.
- Before any camera is installed or changed, we complete a data protection impact assessment and consult residents, families and staff.
- Cameras are never placed in bedrooms, bathrooms or toilets unless there is a lawful, documented decision for that particular resident — made with their consent, or in their best interests if they lack capacity — that it is necessary and proportionate.
- Audio recording is switched off unless the impact assessment shows a clear need for it.
- Surveillance never replaces staff, and is not used to monitor staff performance routinely.
- Residents, visitors and staff are told about surveillance by clear signs and in our information.

## 5. How we use CCTV

### 5.1 Where cameras are

> List where your cameras are (for example entrances, car park, corridors, lounge) and say whether any record sound. If you have no CCTV, write "We do not use CCTV" and this policy covers only residents' and families' own cameras.

Cameras in communal areas are positioned to cover only what is needed, and not to look into bedrooms, bathrooms or toilets. Signs at the entrances and in covered areas say that CCTV is in use, who operates it, why, and how to contact us.

### 5.2 Viewing and storing footage

- Footage is viewed only by people the registered manager has authorised, for the purposes in this policy, and every viewing for an incident is logged.
- Live monitors are placed where visitors and residents cannot see them.
- Footage is kept securely and deleted automatically after [number] days, unless it is needed for an incident, complaint, safeguarding enquiry or police investigation, when the relevant footage is saved and kept for as long as that requires.
- The system's date and time are checked regularly so that footage can be relied on.

### 5.3 Requests for footage

A person caught on camera may ask for a copy of footage of themselves, as with any other personal information (see Data Protection and Access to Records). We respond within one month, and blur or remove other people where needed. Requests from the police are handled by the registered manager, who checks the legal basis, and records what was given and why.

## 6. Cameras in a resident's room

A resident, or someone acting for them, may ask for a camera in their room — for example to reassure a family, or to monitor a health condition at night. The registered manager:

- discusses with the resident what they want and why, and whether something less intrusive would work;
- if the resident has capacity, follows their decision, and agrees how and when the camera will be used, who will see footage, and whether staff and visitors can ask for it to be switched off during personal care;
- if the resident lacks capacity to decide, makes a best interests decision with those who know them, including anyone with lasting power of attorney for health and welfare;
- considers anyone else affected, such as a resident who shares the room;
- records the decision, the reasons and the impact assessment, and reviews it regularly.

## 7. Hidden cameras placed by families

Some families put a hidden camera in a relative's room, often because they are worried about their care. CQC's guidance recognises that some people do this. If staff find or suspect a hidden camera:

- carry on working professionally — do not move, cover, switch off or damage it;
- tell the registered manager.

The registered manager talks to the family to understand their concerns, and to the resident where possible. Any concern about care is taken seriously and looked into, under our Safeguarding policy if it may be abuse or neglect. The manager also explains the impact on the resident's privacy and dignity, and on others, and works with the family to agree an open arrangement under section 6, or a better way to address their concern.

If footage from a family's camera shows possible abuse or neglect, it is dealt with as a safeguarding concern straight away.

## 8. Covert surveillance by the home

Covert surveillance by the home is used only in exceptional circumstances — for example where there is a specific suspicion of a crime or abuse and no less intrusive way to investigate — and only after a documented decision by the registered manager and the nominated individual, a specific impact assessment, and legal advice where needed. It is limited in time and place, never in bedrooms, bathrooms or toilets, and stopped as soon as the purpose is achieved. Where a crime is suspected, the police and the local authority safeguarding team are involved first.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — decides on surveillance, completes impact assessments, authorises who can view footage, handles requests, and reviews this policy's use.
- **Nominated individual** — agrees any covert surveillance and checks surveillance is used lawfully.
- **All staff** — know where cameras are, respect residents' privacy, and report concerns about cameras.

## 10. Training

Staff are told about the home's surveillance at induction. Staff authorised to view footage are trained in this policy and in data protection.

## 11. Records

We keep data protection impact assessments, decisions about cameras in residents' rooms, the viewing log, requests for footage and what was disclosed, and a record of the system's checks. Saved footage is kept only as long as the purpose for saving it requires.
