## 1. Purpose

Most of our care workers drive between visits, often in the dark, in bad weather and to tight schedules. Driving is one of the most dangerous things anyone does at work. This policy sets out what {{organisation}} expects of everyone who drives for work, the checks we make, and how we plan work so that no one has to drive unsafely to keep to time.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who drives for work in their own vehicle or in one we provide, including care workers, office staff and managers. Driving from home to the first visit and back from the last may also count as working time, depending on your contract; the safety rules in this policy apply whenever you are driving for us.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — our duty to manage the risks of driving for work, as with any other work activity.
- Road Traffic Act 1988 — every driver must hold a valid licence for the vehicle and be insured for the way it is used, and the vehicle must be roadworthy.
- Road Vehicles (Construction and Use) Regulations 1986 — it is an offence to use a hand-held phone or similar device while driving, including when stopped in traffic.
- Highway Code.
- Health and Safety Executive guidance, Driving at work: managing work-related road safety.
- National Minimum Wage Regulations 2015 — time spent travelling between visits is working time and is paid.

## 4. Our policy

- No visit is so urgent that you should drive unsafely to reach it. If you are running late, the office will deal with it.
- Everyone who drives for work holds a valid licence, has insurance that covers business use, and drives a roadworthy vehicle — and shows us the evidence.
- Rotas include realistic travel time between visits, and travel time is paid.
- You never use a hand-held phone while driving.
- Clients are carried in a vehicle only where it has been agreed and risk-assessed.

## 5. Before you drive for us

Before you first drive for work, and every year after that, you provide:

- your driving licence, and a check code that lets us view your licence record on the DVLA's online service;
- your insurance certificate, showing that it covers business use — travelling between clients is business use, which ordinary social, domestic and commuting cover does not include;
- confirmation that the vehicle has a current MOT where it needs one, and is taxed.

You must tell the registered manager straight away if:

- you receive penalty points, a driving ban, or are charged with a driving offence;
- your insurance is changed, cancelled or does not cover business use;
- you develop a medical condition that affects your driving and must be reported to the DVLA, or start medicine that may make you drowsy.

We keep a record of these checks. You cannot drive for work until they are complete.

## 6. Your vehicle

You are responsible for keeping your own vehicle safe and legal. Check it regularly: tyres (tread and pressure), lights, wipers and screen wash, oil and coolant, and that nothing is damaged. Keep it serviced, and get faults put right before you drive. We recommend breakdown cover. In winter, carry a scraper, a torch, warm clothing and a charged phone.

> State your mileage allowance and how staff claim it, or refer to where your contract sets this out.

If we provide a vehicle, the registered manager makes sure it is insured, taxed, serviced and has a current MOT, and you check it before each use and report faults.

## 7. On the road

### 7.1 Phones

- Never hold or use a phone, satnav or other device while driving, including when stopped at lights or in traffic.
- Set your satnav and route before you set off. A device in a fixed holder, used hands-free, is allowed by law, but anything that distracts you is unsafe.
- Do not answer calls or read messages until you have parked safely with the engine off. The office will not expect you to answer while you are driving.

### 7.2 Fatigue

Early starts, late finishes and split shifts can leave you tired. Do not drive if you feel too tired to do so safely. Stop somewhere safe and rest if you become sleepy. Tell the office if your rota does not give you enough time to travel or to rest, and they will change it. See Working Time and Rest Breaks.

### 7.3 Alcohol, drugs and medicines

Never drive after drinking alcohol or taking illegal drugs, or while affected by them from the night before. Check with your pharmacist or GP whether medicines you take affect your driving, and tell us if they do. See Alcohol and Drugs.

### 7.4 Weather and conditions

In snow, ice, flood or fog, drive only if it is safe to do so, allow extra time, and tell the office if you cannot reach a visit. Never drive through floodwater. The office follows our Business Continuity and Emergency Planning arrangements to make sure the clients who most need their visits receive them.

### 7.5 Parking

Park safely and legally, ideally in a well-lit place facing the way out (see Lone Working). Do not block drives or dropped kerbs. Parking fines and other penalties are your own responsibility.

## 8. Carrying clients

Care workers carry clients only where it has been agreed in the client's care plan and a risk assessment has been done. Before it is agreed, the registered manager checks that:

- your insurance covers carrying clients in connection with your work — many policies do not, even with business use;
- the client can get in and out of the vehicle safely, and the vehicle is suitable for them and any wheelchair or walking aid;
- the client's behaviour or health will not make the journey unsafe for them or for you.

The client wears a seatbelt, unless they have a medical exemption. Never carry more passengers than the vehicle has seats with belts, and never carry a client's family or friends, or pets, unless that is also agreed. Do not drive a client's own car unless the registered manager has agreed it and you are insured to do so.

## 9. Accidents and breakdowns

- If you are involved in a collision, stop, make sure everyone is safe, and call 999 if anyone is injured or the road is blocked dangerously.
- Exchange names, addresses and vehicle and insurance details with the other people involved. If anyone is injured and you do not give your insurance details at the scene, or you cannot exchange details, report it to the police as soon as you reasonably can, and in any case within the time the law sets.
- Tell the office straight away, so that your visits can be covered.
- Report the collision through the site's concerns process the same day, and tell your insurer.

If you break down, get yourself to a safe place away from traffic, call your breakdown service, and tell the office.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — checks licences, insurance and MOTs, plans rotas with realistic travel time, agrees and risk-assesses any carrying of clients, and acts on incidents.
- **Office and on-call staff** — build rotas with enough travel time, do not call drivers who are driving, and deal with lateness without pressure.
- **Drivers** — drive safely and legally, keep their vehicle roadworthy, keep their documents up to date, and tell us of any change.

## 11. Training

Driving at work is covered at induction. Staff who carry clients are shown how to help them in and out of a vehicle safely and how to secure any equipment. Further training is offered to drivers who have had a collision or whose driving gives cause for concern.

## 12. Records

Licence, insurance and MOT checks are kept on the driver's file and renewed every year. Collisions and near misses are kept through the concerns process. Agreements to carry clients are kept on the client's record. All are kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
