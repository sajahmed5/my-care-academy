## 1. Purpose

The home is where our residents live, and they and their families have the right to say when something is not right. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} makes it easy to complain, looks into complaints fairly and promptly, puts things right, and learns from them.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all staff, on every shift, and to complaints about any part of our service from anyone — residents, families, friends, advocates, visitors, professionals, commissioners or the public.

A complaint is any expression of dissatisfaction about our service, spoken or written, that calls for a response. Staff concerns about their own work go under Grievance, and concerns about wrongdoing under Whistleblowing. A complaint about how we use personal information also follows Data Protection and Access to Records.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 16: Receiving and acting on complaints — an accessible system for receiving, recording, handling and responding to complaints, every complaint investigated and acted on, and a summary sent to CQC within 28 days if it asks.
- Regulation 9A: Visiting and accompanying in care homes — residents' right to visitors, never restricted because someone complained.
- Local Authority Social Services and National Health Service Complaints (England) Regulations 2009 — how local authorities and NHS bodies handle complaints. They do not bind us directly, but a resident whose placement the local authority arranges can complain to it, and we work with it to resolve the complaint.
- Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman — adult social care complaints, including from residents who pay for themselves, once our process is finished.
- Care Quality Commission — does not investigate individual complaints, but wants to hear about people's experience of care.
- Consumer law, and the Competition and Markets Authority's advice to care homes for older people — self-funding residents are consumers, owed a fair and accessible complaints process.
- Equality Act 2010 and Mental Capacity Act 2005 — reasonable adjustments, and complaints for residents who lack capacity.

## 4. Our policy

- Complaints are welcome. They tell us what we need to put right.
- Nobody is treated worse for complaining. A resident is never asked to leave, and a visitor never kept away, because they complained.
- Every complaint is recorded, however small.
- We investigate fairly, apologise where we got something wrong, and say what we will change.
- The timescales below are our own standards, not set by law. Where we cannot meet one, we say why and when we will.
- If someone's contact becomes abusive or excessive, the registered manager may agree with them in writing how we keep in touch. Their complaint is still answered, and any limit on visiting is decided only under our Visiting policy.

## 5. Making a complaint

The procedure is displayed in the home, in our guide to the service and contract, and explained on admission. Anyone can complain to any member of staff at any time, at a residents' or relatives' meeting, by phone on {{office_phone}}, by email to {{office_email}}, in writing to {{registered_manager}}, or through an advocate or family member. Information is available in large print, easy read, pictures and other languages, with an interpreter where needed (see Accessible Information and Communication).

### 5.1 Residents who cannot easily say

Many residents live with dementia or find words hard. Staff watch for signs of unhappiness — refusing food or care, distress at a particular time or with a particular person — which the shift leader records and raises with the registered manager as a possible complaint. A resident with no one to speak for them is helped to get an independent advocate.

### 5.2 Complaints made for a resident

A complaint can be made for a resident with their consent or, where they lack capacity to make or agree to it, by someone acting in their best interests: an attorney, a deputy, their relevant person's representative under the Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards, or close family. We share only what that person needs, and look into anonymous complaints, including notes in the comments box, as far as we can.

## 6. What to do

### 6.1 If someone complains to you

- Listen, stay calm, and do not argue or defend yourself, even if the complaint is about you.
- Say you are sorry they are unhappy and that you will tell the shift leader now. Do not promise an outcome, or agree to keep it between you.
- The shift leader records it through the site's concerns process before the shift ends. At night or weekends they deal with anything urgent, call the on-call manager if a resident is at risk, and otherwise pass it to the registered manager on their next working day.

### 6.2 Resolving it quickly

Many concerns can be put right at once: a meal replaced, lost laundry found. If the person is satisfied by the end of the next working day, the shift leader records the concern and what was done. If not, it becomes a formal complaint.

### 6.3 Acknowledging

The registered manager or deputy acknowledges a formal complaint within 3 working days, in the way the person prefers, saying who is looking into it, what we understand the complaint to be, and when to expect a reply. Where a local authority or the NHS funds the placement, we tell the commissioner as the contract requires.

### 6.4 Investigating

Someone not involved in what is complained about investigates. The evidence usually includes daily notes, care plans, medicines records, rotas, call bell records, CCTV in communal areas where we use it (see CCTV), and accounts from staff, the resident and others who saw what happened. Common complaints are about meals, laundry and lost belongings, rooms, call bells at night, too little to do, other residents, visiting and fees. If it concerns a member of staff, the registered manager decides whether they work away from that resident meanwhile.

### 6.5 Responding

The full written response is sent within 20 working days. If we need longer, we write before then to explain why and give a new date. It sets out what we found on each point, apologises where we got something wrong, says what we will do and by when, and explains how to ask for a review and go to the Ombudsman.

### 6.6 Review

If the person is not satisfied, they can ask for a review by {{nominated_individual}}. We acknowledge the request within 3 working days and send the outcome within 20 working days. That is our final response.

> Say who reviews complaints if the nominated individual is also the registered manager, or was involved in the complaint — for example another director.

## 7. Outside our process

- Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman — once our process is finished, or if we have taken too long, the person can ask the Ombudsman to look at it, whoever pays for the care. The service is free.
- The local authority — where it arranges the placement, the person can complain to it instead of, or as well as, to us.
- The NHS — where it funds the care, for example through NHS Continuing Healthcare, the person can complain to the NHS body that funds it, then to the Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman.
- Care Quality Commission — anyone can tell CQC about our care.

## 8. Complaints that are safeguarding concerns

Some complaints suggest abuse or neglect — rough handling, unexplained bruising, harm from another resident. Safeguarding comes first: the shift leader makes the resident safe, and the registered manager refers it to the local authority the same day, as our Safeguarding policy sets out, and agrees with it how the complaint is handled alongside its enquiry. Where harm was caused, the duty of candour may also apply, and some events must be notified to CQC (see Reportable Incidents).

## 9. Learning from complaints

{{registered_manager}} reviews complaints every month with incidents and safeguarding concerns, looking for patterns by shift, unit or time of day, and reports trends to {{nominated_individual}} through our governance checks. What we changed is shared at residents' and relatives' meetings and with staff, without naming anyone. Compliments are recorded too, and passed on.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure complaints are acknowledged, investigated and answered on time, and leads the monthly review.
- **Nominated individual** — carries out reviews, and checks that learning leads to change.
- **Shift leaders** — record concerns, resolve what they can, and deal with urgent matters out of hours.
- **Care staff** — listen, pass every complaint on straight away, and take part honestly in any investigation.

## 11. Training

All staff learn at induction how to receive and pass on a complaint, including recognising distress in residents who cannot easily say, and refresh it every year. Those who investigate complaints are trained to do so.

## 12. Records

Each complaint, its investigation, letters and outcome are recorded through the site's concerns process, separately from the care record, and kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
