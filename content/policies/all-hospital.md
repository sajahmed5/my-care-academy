## 1. Purpose

A move into or out of hospital is one of the riskiest times for the people we support: information gets lost, medicines change, and people come home weaker than they left. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} makes sure the right information goes with a person to hospital, keeps in touch while they are there, and only restarts care when we know we can meet their needs safely.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us and to every planned or emergency hospital admission, and every discharge back to our care, of a person we support.

## 3. The law and guidance

- NICE guideline NG27: Transition between inpatient hospital settings and community or care home settings for adults with social care needs — how information is shared, discharge is planned, and care is restarted safely; and the related NICE quality standard QS136.
- Care Act 2014 and the Department of Health and Social Care's hospital discharge and community support guidance — how hospitals, local authorities and providers work together on discharge.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — decisions about treatment and about where a person lives after hospital are made with them, or in their best interests if they lack capacity.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 9: Person-centred care, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment (including working with other providers and the safe management of medicines), and Regulation 17: Good governance.
- Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR — sharing the information the hospital needs to care for the person.

## 4. Our policy

- Every person we support has an up-to-date hospital passport: a short summary of who they are, how they communicate, their health, medicines, allergies, and what matters to them.
- When a person goes into hospital, the information the hospital needs goes with them or follows the same day.
- We stay in touch with the ward, with the person's consent, and take part in planning their discharge.
- We do not restart care, or accept a person back, until we have checked that their needs have not changed beyond what we can safely meet.
- When a discharge is unsafe, we say so, and we raise it as a concern.

## 5. The hospital passport

The hospital passport is completed with the person, and their family where they wish, when care starts, and checked at every care plan review. It sets out their name and preferred name, next of kin and any attorney, GP, diagnoses, allergies, current medicines, how they communicate, sight, hearing and mobility, eating and drinking needs, continence, how they show pain or distress, mental capacity, any advance decision, DNACPR decision or ReSPECT form, and the things that help them feel safe. It is written so that a hospital nurse can read it in a few minutes.

## 6. Going into hospital

### 6.1 In an emergency

- Call 999 if the person is seriously ill or injured. Follow our First Aid, Medical Emergencies and Deterioration policy.
- Give the ambulance crew the hospital passport, the current medicines list or a copy of the medicine administration record, and any DNACPR decision or ReSPECT form and advance decision.
- Send the person's medicines in their original packaging if the crew ask, together with glasses, hearing aids, dentures, walking aids and phone, if they can be taken.
- Tell the office or the person in charge, who tells the next of kin.
- Record what happened, the time, what was sent, and which hospital the person went to.

In a person's own home, the care worker stays with them until the ambulance arrives unless it is unsafe to do so, makes sure the home is secure and pets are cared for, and phones the office before leaving. In a care home, the shift leader decides whether a member of staff should go with the resident, and if the resident has dementia, a learning disability or is very distressed, someone familiar should go with them where staffing allows.

### 6.2 Planned admissions

Before a planned admission, the registered manager or a senior checks the hospital passport is current, agrees with the person what they will take, and tells the ward about any needs the hospital should prepare for.

### 6.3 While the person is in hospital

- In domiciliary care, the office suspends visits from the time of admission, tells the commissioner as the contract requires, and makes sure keys and the person's home are secure.
- In a care home, the resident's room is kept for them on the terms of their contract.
- With the person's consent, or in their best interests, a senior or the registered manager keeps in touch with the ward and the discharge team, gives information about how the person was before admission, and asks to be told when discharge is being planned.
- The person's wishes about their future, including where they want to live, are made known to the hospital.

## 7. Coming out of hospital

### 7.1 Before we agree to a discharge

The registered manager, or a senior they name, finds out from the ward or discharge team, before the person leaves hospital:

- how the person's needs have changed, including mobility, eating and drinking, skin, continence and mental state;
- what medicines they are on now, and what has started, stopped or changed;
- what equipment, nursing input or therapy they will need, and whether it is in place;
- any new DNACPR decision, ReSPECT form or advance decision;
- the planned date and time of discharge, and who will bring them.

Where needs have changed significantly, we reassess the person, in hospital if we can, before agreeing to restart care. We will not accept a discharge, or restart a package, if we cannot safely meet the person's needs; the registered manager tells the discharge team and the commissioner in writing, and explains why.

### 7.2 On the day

- Ask for the discharge letter, medicines and any changes in writing. Check that the medicines that come home match the discharge letter. If anything does not match or is missing, contact the ward or the GP the same day before giving any new or changed medicine.
- Check equipment has arrived and is working before the person needs it.
- Update the care plan, risk assessments and medicine administration record the same day, and tell staff what has changed.
- In domiciliary care, restart visits at the times agreed. A first visit is arranged for the time the person gets home if they need help straight away.
- In a care home, welcome the resident back, check their skin, and record their weight and how they are.

We ask hospitals not to discharge people late at night unless it has been planned with us. If a person arrives home without notice, or in a state that worries us, we make them safe first, then raise it with the ward the same day.

### 7.3 Unsafe discharges

If a person is discharged without the information, medicines, equipment or support they need, or comes back with an injury or pressure damage that was not explained, record it, tell the registered manager, and raise it with the hospital. If the person has been harmed or put at risk of harm, the registered manager raises a safeguarding concern with the local authority. All unsafe discharges are recorded through the site's concerns process so that patterns can be taken up with the hospital and commissioner.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure every person has a current hospital passport, decides whether we can safely accept a discharge, works with hospitals and commissioners, and raises unsafe discharges.
- **Office, on-call staff and shift leaders** — make sure the right information goes with the person, keep in touch with the ward, suspend and restart care, and update records.
- **Care workers and care staff** — call for help in an emergency, hand over the hospital passport and documents, and report changes after discharge.

## 9. Training

All staff learn about hospital admissions, the hospital passport and what to hand over during induction. Seniors and shift leaders are trained in discharge planning and medicines reconciliation, and refresh it every year.

## 10. Records

Hospital passports, admission and discharge records, discharge letters and the record of any changes are kept on the person's care record for as long as our record retention schedule requires. Unsafe discharges and any safeguarding concern are kept through the concerns process.
