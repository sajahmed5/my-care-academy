## 1. Purpose

Contact with family, friends and the wider community is essential to residents' wellbeing, and the home is their home. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} welcomes visitors, supports residents to go out with them, and limits visiting only where it is truly necessary — in line with Regulation 9A, which makes visiting and accompanying a legal requirement in care homes.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all residents, everyone who visits them, and all staff. Visitors include family, friends, partners, faith leaders, volunteers, advocates and anyone the resident wishes to see. Professionals visiting in their work role, such as the GP or community nurse, sign in but are not limited by this policy.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 9A: Visiting and accompanying in care homes — residents must be able to receive visitors and must not be discouraged from doing so, and any restriction must be justified; the same regulation requires hospitals and hospices to let people be accompanied when they attend outpatient and emergency appointments.
- Care Quality Commission guidance on Regulation 9A.
- Human Rights Act 1998 — Article 8, the right to respect for private and family life.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — decisions about contact for a resident who lacks capacity are made in their best interests, and restrictions on contact may form part of a deprivation of liberty.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 10: Dignity and respect, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment (including preventing and controlling infection), and Regulation 13: Safeguarding service users from abuse and improper treatment.
- UK Health Security Agency guidance on infection prevention and control and outbreaks in care homes.

## 4. Our policy

- Residents decide who visits them. Visitors are welcome at any reasonable time, and there are no fixed visiting hours.
- Residents can see visitors in their own room, in shared areas, or outside, in private if they wish.
- Residents can go out with their visitors, and can have someone with them when they go to hospital and other appointments.
- We restrict a visit only when it is necessary and proportionate for the resident's safety or wellbeing or that of others, for as short a time as possible, and we explain why.
- During an outbreak, we keep visiting going as far as we safely can, following the health protection team's advice, and visits to a resident at the end of life always continue.

## 5. Welcoming visitors

- We find out on admission who the resident would like to visit, and who they do not want to see, and record it in the care plan.
- Visitors sign in and out, for fire safety and so we know who is in the building.
- Staff greet visitors and tell them anything they need to know, such as how the resident is today, with the resident's consent.
- Visitors may bring food, help at mealtimes, join in activities, and help with care if the resident wishes and it has been agreed. Visitors who help with care follow the resident's care plan, and are shown how.
- Children and well-behaved pets are welcome, with consideration for other residents.
- We ask visitors to avoid the main mealtimes and the night unless the resident wishes otherwise or is unwell, so that residents can eat and sleep undisturbed. This is a request, not a rule.

## 6. Going out and being accompanied

Residents can go out with family and friends whenever they wish, and we help with the arrangements: medicines to take, mobility equipment, and what to do if they become unwell. A resident going to a hospital appointment or the emergency department can be accompanied by a family member, friend or member of staff. Where a resident has a Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards authorisation, any conditions about going out are followed, and the shift leader checks the care plan before the resident leaves.

## 7. When visits may be restricted

A restriction is any limit on who can visit, when, where or for how long. It is a serious step and is used only as a last resort.

### 7.1 Infection and outbreaks

If a resident or the home has an infection, we follow the advice of the UK Health Security Agency health protection team and our Outbreak Management policy. Visitors may be asked to wear protective equipment, wash their hands, or visit in a particular room. We do not stop visiting across the home unless the health protection team advises it for a specific reason, and even then each resident who wishes it can continue to receive at least one visitor, and residents at the end of life can receive their visitors. Visitors who are unwell are asked not to visit until they are better.

### 7.2 A risk from a visitor

If a visitor's behaviour puts a resident, other residents or staff at risk — for example, they are abusive, aggressive, under the influence of alcohol or drugs, or there is a safeguarding concern — the shift leader may ask them to leave and tells the registered manager. Any longer-term restriction is decided by the registered manager after considering the resident's wishes, their rights and any less restrictive option, and is reviewed regularly. Where a resident lacks capacity, the decision is made in their best interests, involving their attorney or deputy, and where there is a safeguarding concern, with the local authority. Where a court has made an order about contact, it is followed.

### 7.3 The resident's choice

A resident with capacity may decide not to see a particular person, and we respect that. Staff explain the resident's decision to the visitor kindly, without giving details the resident would not want shared.

### 7.4 Explaining a restriction

Any restriction is explained to the resident and the visitor, in writing if it lasts more than a short time, with the reasons, how long it is expected to last, when it will be reviewed, and how to complain. It is recorded in the resident's care plan.

## 8. Concerns and complaints

Visitors and residents who are unhappy with visiting arrangements can raise it with the shift leader or the registered manager, or make a complaint, which is recorded through the site's concerns process. They may also contact the Care Quality Commission.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure this policy is followed, decides on and reviews any restriction, and makes sure residents and families know the arrangements.
- **Shift leaders** — welcome visitors, deal with problems on the shift, and record any visitor asked to leave.
- **Care staff** — welcome and support visitors, respect residents' privacy, and report concerns.

## 10. Training

All staff learn about visiting, residents' rights and Regulation 9A during induction. Shift leaders learn how to manage a difficult visit and when a restriction may be justified.

## 11. Records

The visitors' book, the resident's wishes about visits, and any restriction with its reasons and reviews are kept for as long as our record retention schedule requires. Incidents involving visitors are kept through the concerns process.
