## 1. Purpose

Tired staff make mistakes, and in care those mistakes can harm people. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} keeps working hours within the law, makes sure staff get the rest and breaks they are entitled to, and pays for all the time that counts as work.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all workers, including those on zero-hours and part-time contracts, and anyone who works for us in more than one role. Agency workers' hours are the agency's responsibility, but we do not book them in a way we know would break these rules.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Working Time Regulations 1998 — limits on average weekly working hours, the right to daily and weekly rest and to rest breaks, limits and health assessments for night workers, extra protection for young workers, and the duty to keep records.
- National Minimum Wage Act 1998 and National Minimum Wage Regulations 2015 — every hour of working time is paid at least the National Minimum Wage or National Living Wage, at the rate in force at the time.
- HM Revenue and Customs guidance on calculating the minimum wage, including travel time and sleep-in shifts.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 18: Staffing — enough suitably skilled staff, deployed safely.

## 4. Our policy

- We plan rotas so that staff can rest, and do not ask anyone to work in a way that breaks the rules below.
- All working time is paid, including travel between visits.
- Nobody is pressured to opt out of the 48-hour limit, or treated less favourably for refusing to.
- Staff tell us about any other job they have, so that we can make sure their total hours are safe.

## 5. The rules

### 5.1 Weekly working hours

Workers must not work more than an average of 48 hours a week, normally averaged over 17 weeks. A worker aged 18 or over may choose to opt out of this limit by signing a written agreement. The opt-out is voluntary. A worker may cancel it by giving notice in writing — seven days, unless the opt-out agreement sets a longer period, which cannot be more than three months. Even with an opt-out, we keep an eye on the hours people work, and do not allow hours that would make someone unsafe.

If you have another job, your hours in both count towards the limit. Tell us, so that we can plan with you.

### 5.2 Daily and weekly rest

- At least 11 hours' rest in a row between working days.
- At least 24 hours' rest without work each week, or 48 hours each fortnight.

### 5.3 Rest breaks

Workers who work more than six hours in a day are entitled to a break of at least 20 minutes, away from their work, taken during the working day and not at the start or end. In homecare, the rota builds this break into a long run of visits, at a time when the care worker is free of care. In a care home, the shift leader makes sure each member of staff takes their break, and that the floor is covered while they do.

### 5.4 Where continuity of care needs it

The regulations allow some of these rest rights to be varied in work where care must continue round the clock, such as residential care, or in an emergency. Where a worker cannot take a rest period or break they are entitled to, they must be given an equivalent period of compensatory rest as soon as possible. This is the exception, not a way of planning rotas.

### 5.5 Night workers

A night worker is someone who normally works at least three hours during the night period. Night workers must not work more than an average of eight hours in each 24, and are offered a free health assessment before they start night work and at regular intervals after. If a doctor advises that night work is affecting someone's health, we will move them to day work where we can.

### 5.6 Young workers

Workers under 18 must not work more than eight hours a day or 40 hours a week, and cannot opt out. They are entitled to 12 hours' rest between working days, two days off each week, and a 30-minute break when they work more than four and a half hours. They are not normally employed at night, and their work is risk assessed with their age in mind.

## 6. What counts as working time

Working time includes:

- time spent providing care;
- in homecare, time spent travelling between visits, and waiting between visits when the worker is not free to use the time as their own;
- time spent travelling between a client's home and the office, or to training, during the working day;
- required training, meetings, supervisions and inductions;
- time on call when the worker must be at a place we decide.

Travel from home to the first visit, and from the last visit home, is treated differently by the two sets of rules. For the minimum wage it is not counted. For the 48-hour limit and rest periods, the courts have held that it counts as working time for workers who have no fixed place of work, so we include it when we check hours and rest.

### 6.1 Pay for travel time

Travel between visits is working time for National Minimum Wage purposes. Pay for a pay period must be enough that the total pay, divided by all the hours of working time, including travel and waiting, is at least the National Minimum Wage or National Living Wage. Payroll checks this every pay period. If travel time is paid at a different rate, the total must still meet the minimum.

### 6.2 Sleep-in shifts

Where a worker sleeps in, with somewhere suitable to sleep, the time they are permitted to sleep does not count as working time for the minimum wage, but any time they are awake to work does. Workers record any time they are woken to work, and it is paid. A waking night is working time throughout.

## 7. What to do

- Tell the office if your rota does not give you the breaks or rest set out here.
- Tell the office about any other job you have, and any change to it.
- If you are too tired to work safely, tell the office or the person in charge.
- Record your hours, visits and any sleep-in disturbances accurately.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure rotas meet these rules, that night workers are offered health assessments, and that all working time is paid at least the minimum wage.
- **Rota planners and shift leaders** — plan travel time and breaks into rotas, cover breaks, and flag anyone working excessive hours.
- **Payroll** — checks minimum wage compliance, including travel time, every pay period.
- **Workers** — take their breaks and rest, record their time, and tell us about other work.

## 9. Records

We keep opt-out agreements, records of hours worked showing we meet the limits, night worker health assessments, visit and travel times, and pay records. Records of working time are kept for at least two years, and pay records for at least six years.
