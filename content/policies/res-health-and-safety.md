## 1. Purpose

This is the health and safety policy of {{organisation}}. The home is a workplace for our staff and, at the same time, the home of our residents, many of whom are frail, have dementia, or cannot recognise danger for themselves. This policy sets out our commitment to keeping residents, staff, visitors and contractors safe, and how we organise ourselves to do it — day and night.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works in the home, on every shift, and to everyone who comes into it: residents, visitors, volunteers, contractors and visiting professionals. It covers the building, its grounds and its equipment, and everything we do there.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 — our duty to protect the health, safety and welfare of our employees and of everyone else affected by our work, including residents and visitors. Employers with five or more employees must have a written health and safety policy.
- Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — the duty to assess risks, put controls in place, appoint competent help, plan for emergencies, and give staff information and training; and to assess the risks to new and expectant mothers and young workers.
- Workplace (Health, Safety and Welfare) Regulations 1992 — a safe, clean and well-maintained building.
- Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992; Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002; Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998; Lifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations 1998; Electricity at Work Regulations 1989; Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998; Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012; Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013.
- Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 — see our Fire Safety policy.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment; Regulation 15: Premises and equipment.
- Health and Safety Executive guidance, Health and safety in care homes.

CQC takes the lead on the safety of residents in the care we give them; the Health and Safety Executive takes the lead on the safety of staff, visitors and contractors. Both can inspect us.

## 4. Our statement of intent

We will, so far as is reasonably practicable:

- assess the risks in the home and in our work, and put in place and keep under review the controls needed to manage them;
- keep the building, its services and its equipment safe and properly maintained;
- give staff the information, training, equipment and supervision they need to work safely;
- consult staff about health and safety, and act on what they tell us;
- protect residents from hazards they cannot recognise, while respecting their right to make choices;
- report and investigate accidents and incidents, and learn from them.

This statement is signed by {{nominated_individual}} on behalf of the company, and is brought to every member of staff's attention.

## 5. Organisation

- **Nominated individual** — has overall responsibility for health and safety, makes sure the money and resources are available, and reviews our performance every year.
- **Registered manager** — is responsible day to day: risk assessments, maintenance and servicing, training, contractors, and investigating incidents.
- **Shift leaders** — are responsible for health and safety on their shift, including at night and at weekends: they check the building is safe, deal with hazards and emergencies, and make sure incidents are reported.
- **Maintenance staff** — carry out the routine checks and repairs set out in the maintenance schedule, and arrange contractors.
- **All staff** — take reasonable care of themselves and others, follow their training and residents' care plans, use the equipment and protective clothing provided, and report hazards, defects, accidents and near misses.

## 6. The building

### 6.1 Risk assessment

The registered manager keeps a written risk assessment for the premises, covering every area residents, staff and visitors use. It is reviewed every year, and when anything changes — building work, a new piece of equipment, a resident whose needs bring new risks.

### 6.2 Hazards particular to a care home

Because residents may not recognise danger, we control risks that would not need controlling in an ordinary workplace:

- **Falls from windows** — windows that a resident could fall from are fitted with restrictors that cannot easily be overridden, and checked regularly.
- **Hot water and hot surfaces** — baths, showers and basins residents use are fitted with thermostatic mixing valves, and radiators and pipes that could burn someone are covered or kept at a safe temperature (see Legionella and Water Safety).
- **Bed rails** — used only after an individual risk assessment, fitted to the right bed and mattress, and checked for gaps that could trap someone (see Equipment and Maintenance).
- **Hazardous substances** — cleaning products, thickening powders and other substances are locked away (see COSHH).
- **Kitchens, laundry and sluices** — kept locked or supervised when residents could wander in.
- **Electrical items** — residents' own electrical items are checked before use when they move in, and at intervals after that.
- **Trip hazards** — corridors kept clear, flooring repaired promptly, and wet floors signed.

### 6.3 Services and checks

The maintenance schedule sets out every check and service, who does it and how often, and is kept up to date. It includes gas appliances, checked every year by a Gas Safe registered engineer; the fixed electrical wiring and portable appliances; lifts and hoists, thoroughly examined as the lifting regulations require; the nurse call system; water safety; and the fire alarm, emergency lighting and firefighting equipment. Where the building may contain asbestos, we keep an asbestos survey and manage it as the regulations require, and tell every contractor before they start work.

### 6.4 Visitors and contractors

Everyone who comes into the home signs in and out, so that we know who is in the building in an emergency. Contractors are told about the risks in the home and the fire procedure, show their insurance and competence, and agree a safe way of working before they start — keeping tools, materials and work areas away from residents.

## 7. Our main risks and how we manage them

Each of these has its own policy:

- moving and handling residents (Moving and Handling);
- fire (Fire Safety);
- falls (Falls Prevention and Management);
- infection (Infection Prevention and Control);
- water safety and scalding (Legionella and Water Safety);
- equipment and hazardous substances (Equipment and Maintenance; COSHH);
- violence and aggression (Violence and Aggression Towards Staff);
- the security of the building (Safety and Security of Premises).

## 8. Night and weekend working

Fewer staff are on duty at night and at weekends, and managers may not be in the building. The shift leader on duty knows how to deal with an emergency, has the on-call manager's number, and knows where the utility shut-off points, emergency contacts and residents' evacuation plans are kept. Night staff complete a walk-round check of the building at the start of their shift.

## 9. New and expectant mothers, and young workers

When a member of staff tells us in writing that she is pregnant, has given birth in the last six months or is breastfeeding, the registered manager carries out an individual risk assessment and adjusts her work — moving and handling, infection risks, night work, residents whose behaviour may put her at risk — to remove any risk. A risk assessment is also done for any worker under 18 before they start.

## 10. Consulting staff

We consult staff on health and safety at team meetings and handovers, in supervision, and whenever we change how we work. Anyone can raise a concern with the shift leader or the registered manager at any time. No one is treated unfairly for raising a health and safety concern. The health and safety law poster is displayed in the staff area, and our employers' liability insurance certificate is available to staff.

## 11. Training

All staff complete health and safety training during induction, including moving and handling, fire safety, infection prevention, first aid and COSHH, and refresh it every year. Maintenance staff and shift leaders receive the extra training their role needs. Training is recorded on the training matrix.

## 12. Monitoring

The registered manager carries out a health and safety walk-round of the whole home every month, records what they find, and makes sure faults are put right. Accidents and incidents are reviewed every month. The nominated individual reviews health and safety performance every year.

## 13. Records

Risk assessments, the maintenance schedule, service and examination reports, certificates, and contractors' details are kept by the registered manager. Accidents and incidents are kept through the site's concerns process. Training is kept on the training matrix. All are kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
