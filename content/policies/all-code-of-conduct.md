## 1. Purpose

The people we support let us into their lives and, often, their homes. They trust us to behave well, to keep to the edges of a professional relationship, and to look and act like people they can rely on. This policy sets out the standards of conduct, boundaries and dress {{organisation}} expects of everyone who works for us.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all staff, at work and wherever their conduct outside work could affect the people we support, their colleagues or the company. Agency staff working for us are expected to meet the same standards.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Skills for Care and Skills for Health, Code of Conduct for Healthcare Support Workers and Adult Social Care Workers in England — the standards every care worker is expected to meet.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 10: Dignity and respect, and Regulation 13: Safeguarding service users from abuse and improper treatment.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008: Code of Practice on the prevention and control of infections — the basis for our dress rules.
- Equality Act 2010 — dress rules are applied fairly, with adjustments for religion, belief, disability and pregnancy.

## 4. The Code of Conduct

We follow the national Code of Conduct. As a care worker you must:

- be accountable — able to explain what you did and why, and work within what you are trained and allowed to do;
- promote and uphold the privacy, dignity, rights, health and wellbeing of the people you support;
- work in collaboration with your colleagues;
- communicate in an open and effective way;
- respect a person's right to confidentiality;
- keep improving the quality of care through learning and development;
- uphold and promote equality, diversity and inclusion.

In practice this means being on time; following the care plan and the person's wishes; speaking to people respectfully and by the name they prefer; asking before you do something; recording honestly what you did; and speaking up when something is wrong.

## 5. Professional boundaries

A professional relationship exists for the person's care. It can be warm and friendly, but it is not a friendship, and it is your job, not theirs, to keep it professional. People we support may be lonely, unwell or dependent on us, and some may be at risk of being taken advantage of. Boundaries protect them, and protect you from allegations.

### 5.1 What you must not do

- Begin a personal, romantic or sexual relationship with a person you support, or with a member of their family where it could affect their care.
- Give a person you support your personal phone number, address or social media details, or contact them outside work.
- Connect with or message the people we support, or their families, on social media.
- Visit, or arrange to see, a person you support outside your rota, or take your children, friends or pets to work.
- Lend or borrow money or belongings, buy from or sell to them, or take part in any financial arrangement with them.
- Accept gifts, money, loans or legacies except as our Gifts, Gratuities and Legacies policy allows.
- Act as a witness to, executor of or beneficiary of a will, or take on a power of attorney, for a person you support.
- Use the person's phone, car, food, or belongings for yourself.
- Share your personal problems, religious or political views in a way that burdens or influences them.
- Smoke or vape in or near the person's home or room, or while in uniform in view of them.

### 5.2 Grey areas

Some situations are not clear: you already knew the person before they came to us, a family member asks you to do private work for them, or a person asks you to keep a secret. Tell your manager straight away. Being open protects you. A manager may move you to other work if a conflict of interest arises.

### 5.3 Private work

You must not work privately for, or take paid work from, a person we support or their family while you work for us, unless the registered manager has agreed it in writing.

## 6. Dress and appearance

Our dress rules exist mainly to prevent infection and to keep you and the people you support safe.

- Wear the uniform you are given, clean for every shift, and change out of it at the end of your working day where you can. Wash it at the hottest temperature the fabric allows.
- Wear your ID badge where it can be seen, at all times when working. In homecare, show it to the client every time.
- Bare below the elbows when giving personal care: short sleeves or sleeves rolled up, no wrist watch, no bracelets.
- Keep nails short and clean. No nail varnish, false nails, gels or nail art.
- Tie long hair back and off the collar.
- Jewellery is limited to a plain wedding band and small stud earrings. Remove other piercings or cover them where they could cause injury.
- Wear flat, enclosed shoes with non-slip soles.
- Cover cuts and grazes with a waterproof dressing.
- Personal protective equipment is worn as our Infection Prevention and Control policy sets out.

Religious dress, such as a headscarf, turban or kara, is welcome. Where it could affect infection control or safety, we agree a practical way to meet both needs with you — for example, a kara pushed up the arm and secured, or disposable over-sleeves.

## 7. Conduct at work

- Do not work under the influence of alcohol or drugs (see Alcohol and Drugs).
- Use your phone at work only as Social Media, Phones and Technology allows.
- Treat colleagues with respect; bullying or harassment of anyone is never acceptable.
- Do not use a client's home, or the care home, for personal business, rest or visitors of your own.
- Tell your manager at once if you are arrested, charged, cautioned or convicted, or if anything changes that might affect your DBS check or your right to work.

## 8. If the code is broken

Concerns about conduct are dealt with under our Disciplinary policy. A breach of boundaries that harms or puts at risk a person we support is also a safeguarding concern. If you see a colleague breaking this code, tell your manager, or use our Whistleblowing policy.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — sets the standard, makes sure staff know the code, and deals with breaches fairly.
- **Seniors and managers** — lead by example, check conduct and dress at spot checks, and address problems early.
- **All staff** — follow this code, ask when unsure, and raise concerns.

## 10. Training

The code, boundaries and dress rules are covered at induction, as part of the Care Certificate for those new to care, and discussed at supervision.

## 11. Records

Acceptance of this policy is recorded on the site. Conflicts of interest, agreements about private work and adjustments to dress are recorded on the person's file.
