## 1. Purpose

Policies only protect people if they are right, up to date, and known by the people who must follow them. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} adopts, completes, publishes, reviews and retires its policies, how we make sure staff have read and accepted the ones that apply to them, and how we follow up when they have not.

## 2. Scope

It applies to every policy and procedure the company uses: those adopted from the library on the site, and the company's own policies uploaded to it. It applies to everyone who prepares, approves or must follow a policy.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 17: Good governance — systems and processes to assess, monitor and improve the quality and safety of the service, and records to show it.
- Care Quality Commission, Single Assessment Framework — the well-led key question, including the quality statement on governance, management and sustainability, and how staff understand their roles and the policies that support them.
- The laws and guidance named in each policy, which each must keep up with.

## 4. Our policy

- Every policy has an owner, a date it was last reviewed, and a date by which it must be reviewed again.
- A policy is published only by a manager, because it is the company's official policy.
- Every member of staff reads and accepts the policies set for them, within the time allowed.
- A policy is reviewed every year by default, and sooner when the law, guidance or something that happens here shows it needs to change.
- Old versions, reviews and acceptances are kept, so we can show what was in force at any date and who had read it.

## 5. How a policy is made

### 5.1 Adopting

The registered manager chooses the policies the service needs from the library, for its type of service. A company may also upload its own policy, as a Word or PDF document, and manage it in the same way.

### 5.2 Completing

An adopted policy is checked and completed before it is published. The company's details — its name, registered manager, office address and the others set under the company's policy settings — are filled in, and every fill-in and guidance note is written over with what is true for this service. The site does not allow a policy to be published while any fill-in or guidance note is left. Office staff may prepare a policy, but the manager checks it against how the service really works: a policy that says one thing while staff do another is worse than none.

### 5.3 Who must read it

When a policy is published, the manager sets who must read it — care staff, office staff, managers, office staff and managers, or everyone — and how many days they have to accept it. The site uses the company's default number of days unless the policy says otherwise.

### 5.4 Publishing

Only a manager publishes a policy. Publishing makes it the version in force. The people it is set for are told it is waiting for them, and can read it on their own login, on a phone or computer.

## 6. Reading and accepting

### 6.1 What staff do

Read each policy set for you, and accept it on your own login by the date shown. Accepting means you have read it, understood it and will follow it. If you do not understand something, ask your manager before accepting — that is what supervision is for, too.

New starters are given the policies for their role on the day they start, with the full time to read them, counted from their start date.

### 6.2 Reminders and late acceptances

The site reminds staff as the date nears and when it has passed. Late acceptances show to managers with the other things that are due, and in the weekly email. A manager follows up each one:

- first, by speaking to the person, finding out why, and agreeing a date — some people need more time, help with reading, or a translation;
- where it continues, at supervision;
- where someone repeatedly refuses or fails to accept a policy they need to do their job safely, through our Disciplinary policy.

A person may be kept from a task until they have read the policy that governs it — for example, medicines, or moving and handling.

### 6.3 Supporting people to read

Policies are written in plain English. Where someone finds reading difficult or English is not their first language, the manager reads the policy with them, explains it, or provides it in another format, and records that they did so.

## 7. Reviewing

### 7.1 The yearly review

Each policy has a review date, a year after it was published or last reviewed unless the company sets a different period for it. Policies due for review show to managers with the other things that are due, and in the weekly email. At the review, the manager checks the policy against:

- the law and guidance in force;
- how the service actually works now;
- what incidents, complaints, safeguarding concerns, audits, inspections and staff feedback have shown.

### 7.2 Outcomes

- No change needed — the manager signs off the review with a note, and the review date moves on a year. Staff do not need to accept it again.
- Change needed — the manager edits the policy and publishes it as a new version. That counts as the review. Everyone it is set for must accept the new version, with the full time to do so, and is told what has changed.

Each review is kept with who did it, when, and their note.

### 7.3 Reviews out of turn

A policy is reviewed before its date when:

- the law or national guidance changes;
- we are told that the library template it came from has been updated;
- an incident, complaint, safeguarding enquiry, audit or inspection shows it is wrong or not followed;
- the service changes — for example, a new type of care, a new system, or new premises.

## 8. Retiring a policy

A policy that is no longer needed — because the service no longer does that work, or it has been replaced — is retired by a manager. It is no longer shown to staff, but it is kept, with its versions, reviews and acceptances, for the period set in Record Keeping and Retention.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — owns the policy set: adopts, completes, publishes and reviews policies, decides who must read each one, and follows up late acceptances.
- **Nominated individual** — makes sure the policy set is complete, current and followed, as part of the company's governance.
- **Office staff** — may prepare and update policies for a manager to check and publish, and help staff to read them.
- **All staff** — read and accept the policies set for them on time, follow them, and say when a policy does not match how the work is done.

## 10. Training

Managers are shown how to adopt, complete, publish and review policies on the site. New staff are shown how to find and accept their policies at induction.

## 11. Records

The site keeps each published version of every policy, the date it was in force, its reviews with who did them and their notes, and each person's acceptance of each version. Acceptances are shown on each person's file and in their evidence pack. These records are kept for the period set in Record Keeping and Retention.
