## 1. Purpose

Many clients cannot come to the door, so we need a way into their home. A key or a key safe code is the key to someone's safety, property and privacy. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} agrees access arrangements with clients, keeps keys and key safe codes secure, and what happens if one is lost or a code is shared.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us and to every client whose home we enter with a key, a key safe or any other access arrangement.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 10: Dignity and respect (including privacy), Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, Regulation 13: Safeguarding service users from abuse and improper treatment, and Regulation 17: Good governance.
- Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR — a client's address together with how to get into their home is personal data that must be kept secure.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — access arrangements need the client's consent or, if they lack capacity to decide, a best interests decision.

## 4. Our policy

- We enter a client's home only by the arrangement agreed in writing with the client or their representative.
- Key safe codes and keys are given only to staff who need them for a visit, and are never written down or labelled with the client's name or address.
- Care workers always knock or ring and announce themselves before going in, even when they have a key.
- A lost key, or a code that may have been seen by someone else, is reported straight away and put right.
- We do not have keys copied, and do not keep a client's key overnight unless agreed in writing.

## 5. Agreeing how we get in

At assessment, a senior agrees with the client, or their attorney or family, how care workers will get in: the client answering the door, a family member, a key safe, a key held by us, or another arrangement. The agreement is recorded in the care plan and signed. It says who else has access, such as family or other services.

We recommend that a client who needs a key safe has one that meets a recognised security standard, fitted out of sight from the street where possible, by someone competent. The key safe belongs to the client, who chooses the code. We advise them to change the code from time to time, and they may ask us to help with this.

Where a client lacks capacity to agree, the access arrangement is decided in their best interests, and recorded.

## 6. Key safe codes

- The office records key safe codes securely, separately from the client's name and address where it can, and gives codes to care workers only through our secure systems or by phone.
- Care workers learn the code for the visits they are doing. Do not write it down, store it in your phone notes, share it with anyone, or say it aloud where you can be overheard.
- When you open the key safe, shield the keypad. When you lock it, check the key is inside, close it firmly and scramble the code.
- Never leave the key in the door, in the key safe with the door open, or anywhere outside the home.

## 7. Keys held by us

Where the client has agreed that we hold a key, for example because they have no key safe:

- the key is tagged with a code, never the client's name or address, and the code is kept separately;
- the key is signed out and back in on the key log every time it leaves the office;
- care workers keep the key on their person during a round, and return it as agreed; it is not taken home overnight unless the written agreement says so;
- no copy is made.

## 8. Entering and leaving a client's home

- Knock or ring, call out your name and that you are from {{organisation}}, and wait a moment before you go in, even with a key.
- When you leave, check that windows and doors are closed and locked as the client wishes, the key is back in the key safe or with the client, and the key safe is locked and scrambled.
- If the client asks you to leave the door unlocked, or a window open, and you think this is unsafe, explain the risk, record their decision and tell the office.
- If you find the key safe open, damaged, empty or the code changed without notice, phone the office straight away.

If there is no answer when you arrive, follow our No Reply and Unable to Gain Entry policy.

## 9. Lost keys and shared codes

If you lose a client's key, or think a code may have been seen, written down or shared:

- Tell the office or on-call immediately — do not wait until the end of your shift.
- The office tells the client or their representative the same day, records it through the site's concerns process, and agrees what is needed: changing the key safe code, or the locks if a key is lost and could be linked to the address.
- Where the loss was ours, the cost of changing locks is met by us.
- The registered manager considers whether the client's safety is at risk and, if so, raises it as a safeguarding concern.

## 10. When staff leave and when care ends

When a care worker leaves us, the office checks that they have returned any keys and ID, and reminds clients whose codes they knew that they may wish to change them. When a client's care ends, every key we hold is returned and signed for, and the client is reminded to change the key safe code. Codes for clients we no longer support are deleted from our records.

## 11. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure access arrangements are agreed and recorded, codes and keys are kept secure, and losses are put right.
- **Office and on-call staff** — keep codes and the key log secure, give codes only to staff who need them, and act on lost keys and shared codes the same day.
- **Care workers** — keep codes secret, lock and scramble key safes, keep keys safe, and report any problem straight away.

## 12. Training

Care workers learn this policy during induction, and it is part of every spot check. Office staff are shown how codes and keys are kept and logged before they do it.

## 13. Records

Access agreements are kept on the client's care record. The key log and records of lost keys or codes are kept for as long as our record retention schedule requires. Codes are kept only while we support the client.
