## 1. Purpose

The home must keep out people who should not be there, protect residents who cannot recognise danger, and keep belongings safe — while still feeling like a home that residents come and go from and visitors are welcome in. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} keeps the building and grounds secure, and what staff do when security is threatened.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works in the home, on every shift, and covers the building, grounds and car park. Fire is covered by our Fire Safety policy, cameras by our CCTV policy, and residents who go missing by our Missing Residents policy.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 15: Premises and equipment — the premises must be clean, secure, suitable for their purpose, properly used and properly maintained.
- Regulation 9A: Visiting and accompanying in care homes, hospitals and hospices — residents must be supported to receive visitors, so security must never be used to discourage visits.
- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 — our duty to protect staff, residents, visitors and contractors from risks arising from our premises and work.
- Occupiers' Liability Acts 1957 and 1984 — as occupier, we take reasonable care that visitors are reasonably safe, and owe a more limited duty to people who are not visitors, such as trespassers, for dangers we know about.
- Health and Safety Executive guidance on preventing falls from windows and balconies in health and social care.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 and the Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards — a locked door can restrict a resident's liberty, and must be justified.
- Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 — security measures must never block the means of escape.

## 4. Our policy

- The home is secured against intruders in ways that respect residents' freedom and do not put off visitors.
- Residents with capacity come and go as they choose.
- No lock, keypad or restrictor is allowed to stop anyone escaping in a fire.
- Every resident can keep their belongings locked away.
- Security equipment is checked on a schedule.

## 5. How we keep the home secure

### 5.1 Doors and keypads

Staff check who is at the door before letting anyone in. Kitchens, sluices, laundries, plant rooms, the medicines room and stores are kept locked when not in use.

A keypad or locked door that a resident cannot open is a restriction on their freedom. Residents with capacity are given the code or a fob, or are let out whenever they ask. For a resident who lacks capacity and cannot leave freely, the restriction must be in their best interests and the least restrictive option, and is recorded in their care plan. The registered manager considers whether a Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards authorisation is needed. See Restraint and Restrictive Practice and Deprivation of Liberty.

Doors on escape routes that are locked electronically release automatically when the fire alarm sounds, as the fire risk assessment requires. Codes are changed when a code may be known to someone who should not have it, and at least every six months.

### 5.2 Visitors

Visitors sign in and out, for fire safety and so we know who is in the building. Signing in is not a barrier: there are no fixed visiting hours, and staff simply greet visitors they do not recognise and ask who they have come to see. Any limit on a particular visitor is decided only as our Visiting policy sets out.

### 5.3 Contractors and visiting professionals

Contractors' work is arranged in advance. Contractors sign in, show identification, and are told about the risks in the home, the fire procedure and any asbestos before they start (see Health and Safety). They keep tools and materials out of residents' reach, agree work in a resident's room with the resident first, and are never left alone with residents. Visiting professionals sign in and show their identification badge.

### 5.4 Keys

- The registered manager keeps a key register recording who holds each key and fob.
- The shift leader holds the keys to the medicines room, offices and records, and hands them over in person. Spare keys are kept in a locked key cabinet.
- Staff return keys and fobs when they leave. A lost key or fob is reported the same day, and the lock is changed or the fob cancelled if needed.
- Residents may lock their own room and have a key where it is safe. Staff hold an override key for emergencies, and otherwise knock and wait for an answer.

### 5.5 Lockable storage for residents

Every resident is offered a lockable drawer or cupboard in their room, and the home's safe for valuables, as our Residents' Money and Property policy sets out. Residents who look after their own medicines keep them in a lockable cupboard (see Medicines Management). Staff do not open a resident's locked storage without their permission, except in an emergency.

### 5.6 Windows and balconies

Following HSE guidance, every window a resident could fall from is fitted with a restrictor that limits the opening so that a person cannot fall or climb through. Restrictors are robust, fixed with tamper-resistant fittings, and released only with a key or tool that staff hold. Balconies, roof areas and low windows on stairs and landings are assessed in the premises risk assessment. Where a window is also an escape route, the arrangement is agreed with the fire risk assessor.

### 5.7 Nights

At night, external doors are secured so that they cannot be opened from outside but still open from inside for escape. The night shift's walk-round at the start of the shift checks external doors, gates, outbuildings and windows in empty rooms, and that outside lighting works. Staff check who is at the door before opening it at night. If someone unexpected is outside, or you hear something that worries you, do not go out to investigate: phone the on-call manager, or 999 if there may be an intruder.

### 5.8 Grounds

Gates, fences, paths and outside lighting are kept in good repair, gardens are secure enough for residents at risk to enjoy safely, outbuildings are locked, and bins are kept away from the building.

## 6. If security is threatened

### 6.1 Someone you do not recognise

Ask politely whether you can help and who they have come to see. Take them to the shift leader. Do not leave them alone with residents until you know who they are.

### 6.2 An intruder or a threat

If someone refuses to explain why they are there, behaves aggressively, or you think they mean harm:

- do not confront them or try to remove them yourself;
- move residents away and close doors between them and the intruder, if you can do so safely;
- call 999, then tell the shift leader;
- note what the person looks like and which way they went.

The shift leader then checks that every resident is accounted for and safe, secures the building, and tells the on-call manager.

### 6.3 Afterwards

Record the incident through the site's concerns process the same day. The registered manager checks for missing or damaged property, raises a safeguarding concern if a resident was harmed or put at risk, notifies CQC under Regulation 18 of the Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009 where the police were involved (see Reportable Incidents), and reviews how to prevent it happening again.

## 7. CCTV

If the home uses CCTV, it is used only as our CCTV policy sets out. Cameras are never placed in bedrooms, bathrooms or toilets unless there is a lawful, documented decision for that particular resident — made with their consent, or in their best interests if they lack capacity — that it is necessary and proportionate. See CCTV for the detail.

## 8. Checks

- Every night: the walk-round in section 5.7.
- Every week: electronic door releases checked when the fire alarm is tested.
- Every month: window restrictors, locks, door entry systems and outside lighting checked by maintenance staff, and the key register checked by the registered manager.

Faults are reported at once and put right promptly, with the area made safe in the meantime. The registered manager's monthly health and safety walk-round includes security (see Health and Safety).

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — keeps the premises risk assessment, key register and check records, and reviews every security incident.
- **Maintenance staff** — carry out the checks in section 8, and repair faults.
- **Shift leaders** — are responsible for security on their shift, including at night, and take charge if security is threatened.
- **All staff** — follow this policy, greet and check visitors, and report faults and concerns.

## 10. Training

All staff learn this policy at induction — doors, visitors, keys and intruders — and refresh it every year with their health and safety training. Night staff and shift leaders are shown the night procedures. Training is recorded on the training matrix.

## 11. Records

The visitors' book, contractors' records, key register, check records and premises risk assessment are kept by the registered manager. Security incidents are kept through the site's concerns process. All are kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
