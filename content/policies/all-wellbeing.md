## 1. Purpose

Care work is rewarding and it is hard. Staff deal with illness, death, difficult behaviour, long days and working alone. People who are well supported give better care and stay longer. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} looks after the wellbeing of its staff, prevents work-related stress where it can, and helps people who are struggling.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all staff, and covers both physical and mental wellbeing, whether the cause is at work or outside it.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 — our duty to protect the health, safety and welfare of employees, which includes mental health.
- Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — the duty to assess risks to health, including work-related stress, and act on them.
- Health and Safety Executive, Management Standards for work-related stress — the six areas of work that, if not managed, are linked to stress.
- Equality Act 2010 — reasonable adjustments for employees whose mental health condition is a disability.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 18: Staffing — staff receive the support they need to do their job.

## 4. Our policy

- We treat mental health as seriously as physical health.
- We assess the risk of work-related stress and act on what we find.
- Managers notice, ask and listen. Asking for help is a sign of good sense, not weakness.
- What staff tell us about their health is kept confidential and shared only with their agreement or where someone's safety requires it.

## 5. Preventing stress

We use the HSE Management Standards to look at the six areas of work that most affect stress:

- Demands — workload, rota patterns, travel time and the emotional demands of the work. We plan realistic rotas with travel time and breaks, and avoid long runs of shifts.
- Control — how much say people have in how they work. We involve staff in rota planning and in decisions that affect them.
- Support — from managers and colleagues. Everyone has regular supervision, and on-call support is always available.
- Relationships — working together without conflict. We do not tolerate bullying or harassment and deal with conflict early.
- Role — understanding what is expected. Job descriptions, induction and care plans make it clear.
- Change — how change is managed. We explain changes early, consult those affected and support them through it.

We review these areas using what supervisions, staff surveys, exit interviews, sickness absence and incidents tell us, and record our stress risk assessment and the actions we take.

## 6. What to do

### 6.1 If you are struggling

Tell your manager, supervisor or anyone you trust at work. You do not have to wait for supervision. You can say as much or as little as you want. Together you can agree what would help — for example a change to your rota, fewer visits for a while, time off, or someone to talk to.

If it is about your manager, speak to {{registered_manager}} or another manager.

If you are in crisis or thinking of harming yourself, contact your GP, call NHS 111 and choose the mental health option, or call 999 in an emergency. Samaritans can be called free on 116 123, at any time.

### 6.2 If you notice a colleague struggling

Signs can include being withdrawn, irritable, tearful, often late or absent, or making uncharacteristic mistakes. Ask them, privately, how they are, and listen. Encourage them to talk to their manager. If you are worried about their safety, tell a manager straight away.

### 6.3 What the manager does

When someone tells a manager they are struggling, the manager:

- listens without judging, and thanks them for saying;
- asks what is causing it, and what would help;
- completes an individual stress risk assessment with them if work is a cause, using the six Management Standards;
- agrees changes, with a date to review how things are going;
- signposts to support, including any employee assistance service and their GP;
- with their consent, considers a referral to occupational health.

## 7. Support we offer

> List the wellbeing support you offer, such as an employee assistance programme and its phone number, mental health first aiders, or access to counselling. If you offer none, say how staff can find free support, and remove this.

All staff can also use free NHS talking therapies, which they can refer themselves to through the NHS website.

## 8. After a difficult event

The death of a person we support, a serious incident, an assault, an allegation, or finding someone after a fall can affect anyone, sometimes days later. After such events the manager contacts the staff involved the same day or as soon as possible, offers a chance to talk, and checks in again over the following weeks. Time off or a change of duties is offered where needed. Debriefs focus on learning and support, not blame.

## 9. Looking after yourself

- Take your breaks and your annual leave.
- Keep to your rota, and say if the rota is not workable.
- Use supervision to talk about how you are, not only about the work.
- Tell us about anything outside work that is affecting you, if you want us to help.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure stress risk is assessed and acted on, that support is available, and that this policy is followed.
- **Managers and supervisors** — notice, ask, listen, complete individual stress risk assessments, and agree support.
- **All staff** — look after their own wellbeing as far as they can, look out for colleagues, and tell us when they need help.

## 11. Training

Managers are trained to recognise signs of stress and to have supportive conversations. All staff learn about this policy and the support available at induction.

## 12. Records

The company stress risk assessment is kept and reviewed every year. Individual stress risk assessments and agreed adjustments are kept confidentially on the employee's file in the HR pack.
