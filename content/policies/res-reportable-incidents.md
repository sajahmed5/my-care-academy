## 1. Purpose

Some incidents in the home must, by law or by contract, be reported outside {{organisation}}: to the Care Quality Commission, the Health and Safety Executive, the local authority, the commissioners who fund residents' care, and sometimes the police. This policy sets out what must be reported, by whom, to whom and how quickly, so that nothing is missed and nothing is delayed.

## 2. Scope

It applies to the registered manager, the nominated individual, the deputy manager, on-call managers, and the shift leaders in charge of the home at nights and weekends. Care staff report every incident to the shift leader as set out in our Accidents, Incidents and Near Misses policy; this policy covers what happens next.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009 — Regulation 16 (notification of death of a service user) and Regulation 18 (notification of other incidents). Regulation 14 covers the registered manager's absence; see Registered Manager Absence and Deputising.
- Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR) — reporting certain work-related injuries, diseases and dangerous occurrences to the Health and Safety Executive, including some injuries to residents and visitors.
- Care Act 2014, section 42 — the local authority's duty to make enquiries when an adult may be at risk of abuse or neglect.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005, Schedule A1 — the Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 20: Duty of candour; Regulation 17: Good governance.
- Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006 — the duty to refer to the Disclosure and Barring Service.
- UK GDPR — reporting personal data breaches that put people at risk to the Information Commission.
- Our contracts with the local authorities and NHS bodies that place residents with us.

## 4. Our policy

- Notifications are made without delay. When in doubt, we notify.
- The registered manager is responsible for notifications. When they are away, the deputy makes them; a notification is never held back until the manager returns.
- We make residents safe, and refer to safeguarding, before anything else.
- We keep a copy of every notification and the reply, linked to the incident.

## 5. Notifying CQC

Notifications are sent using CQC's notification forms, by the registered manager or the person deputising for them. Each one gives the facts, the action taken and the action planned, and identifies people by initials or reference rather than by name where CQC's forms ask for that.

### 5.1 Deaths (Regulation 16)

We notify CQC without delay of the death of any resident who dies in the home, whether the death was expected or not. We also notify the death of a resident who dies elsewhere, for example in hospital after being taken there from the home, where the death was or may have been a result of the care we provided. If we are unsure, we notify.

When a resident dies unexpectedly, staff call 999, follow the directions of the ambulance service and police, and move nothing in the room until they say so. The doctor or the police decide whether the death is reported to the coroner.

### 5.2 Other incidents (Regulation 18)

We notify CQC without delay of:

- a serious injury to a resident, as the regulation defines it, for example a fracture, a head injury needing hospital treatment, a serious burn or scald, or an injury likely to cause lasting harm, including from a fall. We advise notifying any category 3, 4 or unstageable pressure ulcer; the registered manager makes the final decision (see Pressure Ulcer Prevention and Skin Care);
- abuse, or an allegation of abuse, involving a resident, whoever is alleged to be responsible: a member of staff, a visitor, a relative or another resident;
- any incident reported to, or investigated by, the police that involves a resident or the home, including a resident who goes missing and is reported to the police (see Missing Residents);
- the outcome of every request to a supervisory body for a Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards authorisation, whether it is granted or refused, or the request is withdrawn (see Deprivation of Liberty);
- any event that stops, or may stop, us running the home safely or properly: for example too few suitably skilled staff; a loss of electricity, gas, water or sewerage; a fire, flood or damage to the building; or fire alarms or other safety systems not working.

### 5.3 At night and at weekends

The shift leader makes residents safe, calls 999 where needed, and phones the on-call manager straight away. The on-call manager decides what must happen now, including any call to the police or the out-of-hours safeguarding team, and makes sure CQC is notified without delay.

## 6. Reporting to the Health and Safety Executive (RIDDOR)

As the employer, and as the people in control of the home, we report to the Health and Safety Executive the RIDDOR events that arise from our work, within the time limits the regulations set. In a care home these are most likely to be:

- the death of, or a specified injury to, a member of staff (for example most fractures) caused by an accident at work, which includes physical violence against them at work;
- an injury to a member of staff that keeps them off work, or unable to do their normal duties, for more than seven days;
- an injury to a resident or a visitor that arises out of or in connection with our work and results in them being taken directly from the home to hospital for treatment, or in their death. Examples are a scald from hot water, a fall from a window without a restrictor, entrapment in bed rails, or a fall caused by a wet floor or a failed sling. A fall that comes only from the resident's own condition, with nothing about the building, equipment or our care contributing, is not reportable under RIDDOR, though it may still be notifiable to CQC;
- a reportable occupational disease diagnosed in a member of staff, such as occupational dermatitis, or an infection caught through work;
- a dangerous occurrence, such as the failure of a load-bearing part of a hoist or passenger lift, or an electrical short circuit causing a fire.

Deaths and specified injuries are reported without delay. Injuries that keep staff off work for more than seven days are reported within 15 days of the accident. Injuries that keep staff off work for more than three days are recorded, though not reported. The registered manager checks HSE's current RIDDOR guidance for health and social care before deciding, and records the reason when they decide an event is not reportable.

## 7. Reporting to the local authority and commissioners

### 7.1 Safeguarding

Any concern that a resident is experiencing, or is at risk of, abuse or neglect, including harm by another resident, is referred to the local authority's adult safeguarding team on the same day, as set out in our Safeguarding policy. A safeguarding referral and a CQC notification are separate: most abuse concerns need both.

### 7.2 Commissioners

Our contracts with the local authorities and NHS bodies that fund residents' care require us to tell them about certain events, often within a set time. These usually include serious incidents, safeguarding concerns, deaths, hospital admissions, complaints, outbreaks, and anything that affects our ability to care for the residents they fund.

## 8. Other bodies

- **Police** — any crime, or suspected crime, against a resident, a member of staff or the home; and immediately where anyone is in danger.
- **Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards supervisory body** — the death of a resident with an authorisation, or a change that affects it (see Deprivation of Liberty).
- **Disclosure and Barring Service** — anyone we dismiss, or who leaves, because they harmed a child or adult or put them at risk of harm, as the law requires.
- **Information Commission** — a personal data breach, unless it is unlikely to put people's rights and freedoms at risk, within 72 hours of our becoming aware of it (see Data Security and Breaches).
- **Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency** — a suspected side effect of a medicine, or a medical device incident, through the Yellow Card scheme.
- **UK Health Security Agency health protection team** — a suspected outbreak of infection in the home, as set out in Outbreak Management.
- **Our insurers** — incidents that might lead to a claim, as our policy requires.

## 9. How we make sure it happens

- The shift leader passes anything that may be reportable to the registered manager, or to the on-call manager at nights and weekends, the same day, or straight away if it is serious.
- The registered manager records which notifications are needed against the incident through the site's concerns process, even when the answer is none.
- Notifications are checked in the monthly review of incidents, and a summary goes to {{nominated_individual}}.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — decides what must be reported and makes the notifications, or makes sure the deputy does.
- **Nominated individual** — checks that notifications are being made, and makes those that fall to them.
- **Deputy manager and on-call managers** — make urgent reports and notifications in the registered manager's absence.
- **Shift leaders** — make residents safe, call the emergency services, and tell the registered manager or on-call manager at once.
- **Care staff** — report every incident to the shift leader straight away.

## 11. Training

The registered manager, the deputy and on-call managers are trained in CQC notifications, RIDDOR and safeguarding referrals before they take on the role, and update their knowledge whenever the rules or guidance change. Shift leaders are trained in whom to call when a resident dies or a serious incident happens on their shift.

## 12. Records

Copies of every notification, the reference given and any reply are kept with the incident through the concerns process, for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
