## 1. Purpose

Help with medicines is one of the most important things we do, and one of the easiest to get wrong. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} agrees the help each client needs with their medicines, how care workers give it safely, and what happens when something goes wrong. The medicines belong to the client and stay in their home.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who supports clients with medicines, and to every client whose care plan includes medicines support.

Healthcare tasks beyond routine medicines — such as injections, insulin, medicines through a PEG tube, and rescue medicines for epilepsy — need training from a health professional for the named client, and are covered by our Delegated Healthcare Tasks policy. Giving medicines disguised in food or drink is covered by our Covert Medication policy.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, including the proper and safe management of medicines; Regulation 11: Need for consent; Regulation 17: Good governance, including accurate records.
- NICE guideline NG67, Managing medicines for adults receiving social care in the community — assessing and recording the support each person needs, medicines records, training and competency, and working with prescribers and pharmacists.
- Human Medicines Regulations 2012 — a prescribed medicine is given only to the person it was prescribed for, as the prescriber directs.
- Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 and the Misuse of Drugs Regulations 2001 — controlled drugs, such as oxycodone, fentanyl and some forms of morphine, are tightly controlled because they can be misused.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — consent to medicines support, and best interests decisions for a client who lacks capacity.

## 4. Our policy

- Clients manage their own medicines wherever they can. The support we give with each medicine is assessed, agreed with the client and written in the care plan, and care workers give no more and no less.
- Nobody gives medicines until they have been trained and assessed as competent, with their competency recorded on their file.
- Every dose we prompt, assist with or give, and every dose refused or missed, is recorded at the time.
- Errors and near misses are reported straight away, without fear of blame, so that we can put things right and learn.

## 5. How we work

### 5.1 Assessing the support a client needs

Before care starts, and at every care plan review, the assessor records each medicine, who orders and collects it, where it is kept, and the level of support the client needs:

- Self-managing — the client manages it themselves. Care workers report any sign that they are struggling.
- Prompting — reminding the client to take a medicine they have chosen to take. The client stays in control.
- Assisting — doing something the client asks and directs, such as opening a bottle or popping a tablet from a blister pack.
- Administering — the care worker selects, prepares and gives the medicine, including creams, eye drops and patches.

The client's consent is recorded. If they may lack capacity to decide about their medicines support, a capacity assessment is carried out and, if they lack it, a best interests decision is made and recorded.

### 5.2 Ordering, collecting and storing

The client or their family usually order and collect medicines. Where we do, the care worker records what was ordered and collected, takes medicines straight to the client's home, and never leaves them in a car.

Medicines stay in the client's home, in their original pharmacy containers, stored as the label says and out of reach of anyone who might take them by mistake. Where a client might take too many, or someone in the home might misuse them, the assessor agrees with the client or their family a lockable box or other safe storage.

### 5.3 The medicines administration record

The medicines administration record (MAR) is kept in the client's home. It is printed by the pharmacy where possible. If a MAR has to be written by hand, a trained senior writes it from the pharmacy label or prescription, and a second trained person checks it.

Record each dose straight after it is taken, never in advance, and record why if a dose is refused, missed or not available. Never sign for a medicine you did not see the client take.

### 5.4 Giving medicines

- Read the MAR and the care plan, wash your hands, and ask the client's consent.
- Check the medicine, dose, time, route and expiry date against the MAR and the pharmacy label.
- Give medicines only from their original pharmacy containers or a pack filled by the pharmacy — never from a container filled by a relative or anyone else.
- Do not leave medicines out for the client to take later, unless the care plan says so after a risk assessment.
- If anything does not match — a new box, a different tablet, a changed dose — do not give it. Phone the office, who will check with the pharmacist or prescriber.

### 5.5 "When required" medicines

Medicines prescribed "when required", such as painkillers or laxatives, each have a protocol in the care plan: what it is for, the dose, the minimum time between doses, the most that can be taken in 24 hours, and when to seek advice. Ask the client whether they need it, record the time, dose and reason, and note whether it helped.

### 5.6 Over-the-counter medicines

Care workers support medicines a client has bought for themselves only where they are in the care plan, after the office has checked with the pharmacist that they are safe with the client's other medicines.

### 5.7 Refusals and time-sensitive medicines

A client with capacity may refuse a medicine. Do not pressure them. Record it and tell the office the same day; the office tells the prescriber if refusals continue. Some medicines, such as those for Parkinson's disease, must be given at set times, and the office plans visits so that they can be (see Missed and Late Visits).

### 5.8 Controlled drugs

There is no legal requirement for a controlled drugs cupboard or register in a client's own home. We agree with the client, or their family, safe storage out of sight of visitors. For each controlled drug we administer, the care worker records the dose on the MAR and the amount left, and reports any difference straight away. A difference that cannot be explained is treated as a possible safeguarding concern.

## 6. When something goes wrong

### 6.1 Errors and near misses

If you make or find an error — a wrong medicine, dose, time or person, a missed dose, or a recording mistake:

- check the client is safe, and call 999 if they are unwell;
- phone the office or on-call straight away, who will get advice from the pharmacist, the GP or NHS 111;
- record it through the site's concerns process the same day, as set out in Accidents, Incidents and Near Misses.

The registered manager decides whether it is a safeguarding concern, whether CQC must be notified (see Reportable Incidents), and whether the duty of candour applies. The care worker's competency is reassessed before they give medicines alone again.

### 6.2 Side effects

If you think a medicine is causing a side effect, tell the office the same day. The office tells the prescriber or pharmacist, and the registered manager makes sure the suspected reaction is reported to the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) through the Yellow Card scheme.

## 7. Disposal

Unwanted, stopped or out-of-date medicines are returned to the pharmacy by the client or their family, or by a care worker where the care plan says so, with the return recorded. Never put medicines in the bin, the sink or the toilet, and never take them home. When a client dies, their medicines stay in the home for the family to return, unless a nurse or the police ask otherwise.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — leads on medicines, makes sure assessments and MARs are right, arranges training and competency checks, and reviews every error.
- **Seniors and assessors** — assess medicines support, write and check MARs, and carry out medicines spot checks.
- **Office and on-call staff** — get advice when care workers raise a problem.
- **Care workers** — give only the support in the care plan, record every dose, and report concerns.

## 9. Training

Care workers who support clients with medicines complete medicines training and are assessed as competent, by observation in a client's home, before they give medicines alone. Competency is recorded on their file, refreshed every year, and reassessed after an error or when a spot check shows a need. Training is recorded on the training matrix.

## 10. Records

MARs come back to the office every month and are checked by a senior, who follows up gaps and errors. Spot checks include medicines practice and are recorded on the care worker's file. Assessments, care plans and MARs are kept on the client's record for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
