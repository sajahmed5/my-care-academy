## 1. Purpose

Some incidents must, by law or by contract, be reported to people outside {{organisation}}: the Care Quality Commission, the Health and Safety Executive, the local authority, the commissioners who fund our clients' care, and sometimes the police. This policy sets out what must be reported, by whom, to whom and how quickly, so that nothing is missed and nothing is delayed.

## 2. Scope

It applies to the registered manager, the nominated individual, anyone deputising for them, and the office and on-call staff who take reports out of hours. Care workers report every incident to the office as set out in our Accidents, Incidents and Near Misses policy; this policy covers what happens next.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009 — Regulation 16 (notification of death of a service user), Regulation 18 (notification of other incidents) and Regulation 14 (absence of the registered manager).
- Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR) — reporting certain work-related injuries, diseases and dangerous occurrences to the Health and Safety Executive.
- Care Act 2014, section 42 — the local authority's duty to make enquiries when an adult may be at risk of abuse or neglect.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 20: Duty of candour; Regulation 17: Good governance.
- Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006 — the duty to refer to the Disclosure and Barring Service.
- UK GDPR — reporting serious personal data breaches to the Information Commissioner's Office.
- Our contracts with the local authorities and NHS bodies that commission care from us.

## 4. Our policy

- Notifications are made without delay. When in doubt, we notify.
- The registered manager is responsible for notifications. When they are away, a named deputy makes them; a notification is never held back until the manager returns.
- We make the person safe, and refer to safeguarding, before anything else.
- We keep a copy of every notification and the reply, linked to the incident.

## 5. Notifying CQC

Notifications are sent using CQC's notification forms, by the registered manager or the person deputising for them. Each one gives the facts, the action taken and the action planned, and identifies people by initials or reference rather than by name where CQC's forms ask for that.

### 5.1 Deaths (Regulation 16)

We notify CQC without delay of the death of a client that happened while we were providing care to them, or that was, or may have been, a result of the care we provided — including during a visit, on the way to hospital after an incident on a visit, or after a fall or other event linked to our care. If we are unsure whether a death falls within this, we notify. Deaths that are not linked to our care in this way, such as an expected death in hospital, do not need a notification, but we record what we know.

### 5.2 Other incidents (Regulation 18)

We notify CQC without delay of:

- a serious injury to a client, as the regulation defines it — for example a fracture, a serious burn, a head injury needing hospital care, or an injury likely to cause lasting pain, harm or impairment — where it happened while we were providing care or may be linked to our care;
- abuse, or an allegation of abuse, involving a client — whoever is alleged to be responsible, including a member of our staff, a family member or a stranger;
- any incident reported to, or investigated by, the police that involves a client or our service;
- any event that stops, or may stop, us running the service safely or properly — for example a serious staff shortage that means visits cannot be covered, the loss of our office or phone and IT systems, or a fire or flood at our premises.

The DoLS notifications in Regulation 18 apply to care homes and hospitals, not to home care. Where a client's care involves a deprivation of liberty, see our Deprivation of Liberty policy.

### 5.3 The registered manager's absence (Regulation 14)

If the registered manager will be, or has been, absent for 28 days or more, {{nominated_individual}} notifies CQC — in advance where the absence is planned, and as soon as it is known where it is not — and says who is managing the service meanwhile. See Registered Manager Absence and Deputising.

## 6. Reporting to the Health and Safety Executive (RIDDOR)

As the employer, we report to the Health and Safety Executive the RIDDOR events that arise from our work, within the time limits the regulations set. In home care these are most likely to be:

- the death of, or a specified injury to, a member of staff (for example most fractures, or an injury from physical violence) caused by an accident at work;
- an injury to a member of staff that keeps them off work, or unable to do their normal duties, for more than seven days;
- an injury to a client, or another person not at work, that arises out of or in connection with our work and results in them being taken directly from the scene to hospital for treatment, or in their death — for example a client who falls because a sling we were using failed. A fall that is not linked to how we provided care is not reportable under RIDDOR, though it may still be notifiable to CQC;
- a reportable occupational disease diagnosed in a member of staff, such as occupational dermatitis, or an infection caught through work;
- a dangerous occurrence, such as the failure of a load-bearing part of a hoist.

Deaths and specified injuries are reported without delay. Injuries that keep staff off work for more than seven days are reported within 15 days of the accident. Injuries that keep staff off work for more than three days are recorded, though not reported. The registered manager checks HSE's current RIDDOR guidance for health and social care before deciding, and records the reason when they decide an event is not reportable.

## 7. Reporting to the local authority and commissioners

### 7.1 Safeguarding

Any concern that a client is experiencing, or is at risk of, abuse or neglect is referred to the local authority's adult safeguarding team on the same day, as set out in our Safeguarding policy. A safeguarding referral and a CQC notification are separate: most abuse concerns need both.

### 7.2 Commissioners

Our contracts with the local authorities and NHS bodies that fund our clients' care require us to tell them about certain events, often within a set time. These usually include serious incidents, safeguarding concerns, the death or hospital admission of a client, missed visits, complaints, and anything that affects our ability to deliver the care they have bought.

## 8. Other bodies

- **Police** — any crime, or suspected crime, against a client or a member of staff; and immediately where anyone is in danger. A client who dies suddenly and unexpectedly is reported through 999 or as the ambulance service directs.
- **Disclosure and Barring Service** — anyone we dismiss, or who leaves, because they harmed a child or adult or put them at risk of harm, as the law requires.
- **Information Commissioner's Office** — a personal data breach that is likely to put people's rights at risk, within 72 hours of our becoming aware of it (see Data Security and Breaches).
- **Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency** — a suspected side effect of a medicine, or an incident involving a medical device, through the Yellow Card scheme, usually with the prescriber or pharmacist.
- **UK Health Security Agency health protection team** — an outbreak of infection, as set out in Outbreak Management.
- **Our insurers** — incidents that might lead to a claim, as our policy requires.

## 9. How we make sure it happens

- The office or on-call passes anything that may be reportable to the registered manager the same day, or straight away if it is serious.
- The registered manager records which notifications are needed against the incident through the site's concerns process, even when the answer is none.
- Notifications are checked in the monthly review of incidents.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — decides what must be reported and makes the notifications, or makes sure the deputy does.
- **Nominated individual** — notifies CQC of the registered manager's absence, and checks that notifications are being made.
- **Deputy or senior on duty** — makes notifications in the registered manager's absence.
- **Office and on-call staff** — pass on anything that may be reportable without delay.
- **All staff** — report every incident to the office the same day.

## 11. Training

The registered manager and anyone who deputises for them are trained in CQC notifications, RIDDOR and safeguarding referrals before they take on the role, and update their knowledge whenever the rules or guidance change.

## 12. Records

Copies of every notification, the reference given and any reply are kept with the incident through the concerns process, for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
