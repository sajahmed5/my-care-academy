## 1. Purpose

Clients depend on us to arrive when we said we would. A missed visit can leave someone without food, medicines or help to get out of bed; a late one can mean a missed dose, a fall, or a long and frightening wait. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} plans visits so that they can be kept, monitors them as they happen, acts quickly when a visit is late or at risk of being missed, and learns from every one that goes wrong.

## 2. Scope

It applies to the registered manager, the office and on-call staff who plan and monitor visits, and every care worker. It covers every planned visit, single-handed or double-up.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, Regulation 13: Safeguarding service users from abuse and improper treatment (which includes neglect), Regulation 17: Good governance, Regulation 18: Staffing, and Regulation 20: Duty of candour.
- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, Regulation 18 — notifying CQC of serious injuries, abuse or allegations of abuse, and incidents reported to the police.
- Care Act 2014 — neglect and acts of omission are forms of abuse the local authority must make enquiries about.
- NICE guideline NG21: Home care: delivering personal care and practical support to older people living in their own homes — including continuity of care, visits long enough to do the job without rushing, and planning travel time.
- National Minimum Wage Regulations 2015 — time spent travelling between visits is working time and must be paid.

## 4. Definitions

- A missed visit is a planned visit that did not take place, and was not cancelled by the client or their representative in advance.
- A late visit is one that starts later than the time window agreed with the client or set in the commissioner's contract.
- A time-critical visit is one where a delay could cause harm: for example, medicines that must be given at set times (such as for Parkinson's disease or diabetes), a client who cannot get out of bed or to the toilet without help, or a client who cannot eat or drink without help.

> Record the time window you use for "late" (for example, a set number of minutes either side of the planned time), and any different window your commissioners require.

## 5. Our policy

- We do not accept a package unless we have the staff to deliver it reliably.
- Rotas give care workers realistic, paid travel time between visits.
- Time-critical visits are identified in the care plan and protected on the rota.
- Visits are monitored as they happen, and the office acts before a late visit becomes a missed one.
- Clients are told if we are running late, and are never left wondering whether we will come.
- Every missed visit, and every late visit that causes or risks harm, is investigated and learned from.

## 6. Scheduling

The office plans visits with the client's agreed times, the length of each visit, the number of care workers needed, and realistic travel time between visits, using the route and traffic the care worker will actually face. Continuity matters: clients see a small number of familiar care workers where we can manage it. Time-critical visits are marked on the rota and are not moved without the client's agreement. When rotas change, the office checks that every visit is still covered and tells care workers and clients about changes in good time.

The registered manager reviews the rota regularly for visits that are routinely late, too short, or overlapping, and does not let call times be squeezed to fit more visits in.

## 7. Call monitoring

Care workers log the start and end of every visit electronically as they have been shown. The office, or on-call out of hours, watches visits as they happen and is alerted when a visit has not started within its window. Logging must be honest and made at the time: logging a visit that did not happen, or false times, is gross misconduct.

If you cannot log a visit — no signal, phone not working — phone the office as soon as you can with the times.

## 8. What to do

### 8.1 If you are running late

- Phone the office as soon as you know you will be late, and say which visits are affected and by how much.
- Never skip a visit, cut one short, or change the order of visits without the office's agreement.
- Do not rush or cut corners to catch up. Give the client the care they need.

### 8.2 What the office does

- Phone the client, or their family if agreed, to explain and give a new time.
- For a time-critical visit, send another care worker, or rearrange the round, so that the time-critical care is given on time. If medicines cannot be given on time, follow the advice in the client's medicines plan or ask the pharmacist or NHS 111 for advice.
- If no care worker can attend, tell the on-call manager, who decides what to do, including asking family to help with the client's agreement or attending themselves.
- Record what happened and the actions taken.

### 8.3 If a visit has been missed

When the office or on-call discovers a missed visit, they act straight away:

- Contact the client, or attend, to make sure they are safe and to give the care that was missed.
- If the client cannot be reached, follow our No Reply and Unable to Gain Entry policy.
- Tell the registered manager.
- Apologise to the client and explain what happened.

## 9. Telling people and reporting

- The client and, with their agreement, their family are told what happened and what we are doing about it.
- The commissioner is told of missed visits, and of late visits where the contract requires it, within the time the contract sets.
- If the client has been harmed or put at risk of harm, the registered manager raises a safeguarding concern with the local authority.
- If the client has suffered harm that meets the threshold for the duty of candour, we follow our Duty of Candour policy.
- If the harm is a serious injury, or the matter involves abuse or the police, the registered manager notifies the Care Quality Commission under Regulation 18.

## 10. Investigating and learning

Every missed visit, and every late visit that caused or risked harm, is recorded through the site's concerns process and investigated by the registered manager or a senior they name. The investigation finds out what happened and why — rota error, sickness cover, travel time, a care worker's conduct, or a system problem — and what will stop it happening again. The care worker involved is supported to give their account. Disciplinary action follows only where someone has been careless, dishonest or deliberately failed to attend, and is handled under our Disciplinary policy.

The registered manager reviews missed and late visits every month for patterns — by area, time of day, care worker or client — and reports them through our governance checks.

## 11. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure we only take on care we can staff, rotas are realistic, visits are monitored, and every missed visit is reported, investigated and learned from.
- **Office and on-call staff** — plan rotas with travel time, watch visits as they happen, act on late visits, inform clients and commissioners, and record what happened.
- **Care workers** — log every visit honestly, phone the office as soon as they will be late, and never skip or shorten a visit without agreement.

## 12. Training

Care workers are shown how to log visits during induction, and what to do if they are late. Office and on-call staff are trained in scheduling, monitoring and this policy before they work alone, and refresh it every year.

## 13. Records

Visit logs, rotas, and the record of every missed or late visit and its investigation are kept for as long as our record retention schedule requires. Concerns and notifications are kept through the concerns process.
