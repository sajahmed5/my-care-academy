## 1. Purpose

Care work is demanding, and nobody should do it without support. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} supports, guides and checks the work of its staff through regular supervision, a yearly appraisal, and, for care staff, spot checks and competency assessments — so that people feel supported and the people we support receive good care.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all employees. Supervision and appraisal are for everyone, including office staff and managers. Spot checks and competency assessments are for care staff.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 18: Staffing — staff receive the appropriate support, training, professional development, supervision and appraisal they need to carry out their role.
- Regulation 17: Good governance — we monitor and improve the quality of care, including the practice of our staff.
- Skills for Care guidance on effective supervision in adult social care.
- Code of Conduct for Healthcare Support Workers and Adult Social Care Workers in England — the standard staff are supervised against.

## 4. Our policy

- Supervision is a two-way conversation. It is support first; it is also where we check practice and agree what needs to change.
- Every member of staff has a named supervisor.
- Supervisions, appraisals, spot checks and competency assessments are held at least as often as this policy sets, and more often when someone needs it.
- What is agreed is written down, signed and followed up.
- Supervision is not the place to deal with a disciplinary matter. If a serious concern arises, the supervisor stops and follows our Disciplinary or Safeguarding policy.

## 5. How often

Unless the company sets different periods, these are the minimum:

- Supervision — every three months, and more often during probation, after a difficult incident, or when someone is new to a role or needs extra support.
- Appraisal — every year. It may take the place of one supervision.
- Spot check — every 12 weeks, for care staff.
- Competency assessment — every year for each skill a care worker uses, such as medication, hoisting, moving and handling, and infection control, and before they first use that skill alone.

The periods the company uses are set on the site, which shows each person's next due date, lists who is due and overdue, and includes them in the manager's weekly email. Each record is kept on the person's file.

## 6. What to do

### 6.1 Supervision

The supervisor books the supervision in advance, and the employee is told the date and who it is with. It is held in private, in paid time, and lasts long enough for a proper conversation. It covers:

- how the employee is — their wellbeing and workload;
- the people they support: what is going well, any changes or concerns;
- their practice, including feedback from spot checks, compliments and complaints;
- training completed, due and wanted;
- any concerns they want to raise, including about safeguarding;
- the actions agreed last time, and whether they were done.

New actions are agreed with dates, and go onto the employee's task list on the site. Both sign the record at the end of the meeting. If the employee does not agree with something in the record, their view is added; if they cannot or will not sign, the supervisor records why.

Group supervision may be used for team learning, for example after an incident, but does not replace one-to-one supervision.

### 6.2 Appraisal

The appraisal looks back over the year and forward to the next. Before it, the employee completes a short self-assessment from their own login. Together they review performance against the role and the Code of Conduct, recognise what has gone well, agree goals for the year ahead, and agree a training and development plan, including any qualification. Both sign the appraisal.

### 6.3 Spot checks

Spot checks are unannounced observations of a care worker at work — on a visit in the client's home, or on shift in a care home. The checker looks at whether they:

- arrive on time and are wearing their uniform and ID badge;
- use personal protective equipment and hand hygiene correctly;
- treat the person with dignity and respect, and offer choice;
- give medicines safely, where they do so;
- follow the care plan and keep good records.

The checker asks the person supported, if they are willing, how they find their care. The care worker is given feedback straight away and signs the record. In homecare, the checker introduces themselves to the client, and leaves if the client does not want them there.

### 6.4 Competency assessments

A competency assessment signs off one skill against its own criteria, by someone who is competent in that skill and trained to assess it. If the care worker meets the criteria, they are signed off. If not, they do not use that skill alone until they have had further training or support and been assessed again.

### 6.5 When things are not met

If a spot check or competency assessment is not met, the manager discusses it with the employee and decides on training, further support or a follow-up check. Where poor practice continues, the manager may agree a performance plan with clear goals and dates. A performance plan that does not succeed may lead to the capability part of our Disciplinary policy. Anything that suggests a person has been harmed or put at risk is reported straight away under our Safeguarding policy.

## 7. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure everyone has a supervisor, that supervisions, appraisals, spot checks and competency assessments happen on time, and arranges their own supervision with the nominated individual or another suitable person.
- **Supervisors and seniors** — prepare, hold meetings in private, record them accurately and follow up actions. A senior care worker who supervises others is given an office login to do so.
- **Employees** — attend, prepare, speak openly, and complete agreed actions.

## 8. Training

Supervisors are trained in supervision before they supervise others. Staff who carry out spot checks and competency assessments are competent in the skills they observe and trained to assess them.

## 9. Records

Supervisions, appraisals, spot checks, competency assessments and performance plans are recorded and signed on the person's file on the site. They are confidential to the employee and the managers who need them. Employees can ask to see their own records at any time. Records are kept for six years after employment ends.
