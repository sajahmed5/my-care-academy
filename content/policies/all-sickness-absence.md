## 1. Purpose

When you are ill, the people we support still need their care. This policy sets out how to tell us you are unwell, what we need from you, how we support you back to work, and how we manage absence that is long or frequent — fairly and in a way that keeps the people we support safe.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all employees of {{organisation}}.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Social Security Contributions and Benefits Act 1992 and the Statutory Sick Pay regulations — who is entitled to Statutory Sick Pay and for how long.
- Equality Act 2010 — reasonable adjustments for disabled employees, and absence related to a disability or pregnancy treated fairly.
- Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR — health information is special category data, kept confidential.
- UK Health Security Agency guidance on infections in care settings — when staff with an infection should stay away from work.

## 4. Reporting sickness

### 4.1 Tell us as early as you can

If you cannot work, phone the office on {{office_phone}}, or the on-call line out of hours, as soon as you know — and at least [number] hours before your first visit or shift, so that your work can be covered. Speak to a person: a text, email or message through a colleague is not enough unless you have been told otherwise.

> Set out your reporting arrangements here: the minimum notice you need, the on-call number, and whether staff may also report sickness from their own login on the site.

Tell us why you are unwell, how long you expect to be off, and anything about your work that needs passing on. Keep in touch on each day of absence, or as agreed with your manager.

### 4.2 Infections

Do not come to work with diarrhoea or vomiting until 48 hours after your last symptoms. Tell us if you have, or have been in contact with, an infectious illness such as a respiratory infection, chickenpox or shingles, so that we can follow current guidance for the people we support.

### 4.3 Evidence

For absence of up to seven calendar days, you complete a self-certification when you return. For longer absence, you need a fit note from a doctor or other authorised health professional, sent to us as soon as you get it and renewed before it runs out. If a fit note says you "may be fit for work", we will talk to you about the changes it suggests.

## 5. Sick pay

Statutory Sick Pay is paid to those entitled to it, at the rate and under the rules in force at the time.

> State any company sick pay you offer on top of Statutory Sick Pay, and its conditions — or say that you pay Statutory Sick Pay only.

Sick pay may be withheld if you do not follow this reporting procedure without good reason.

## 6. Returning to work

Every absence is followed by a return-to-work interview with your manager, within three working days of coming back. It is short and supportive. It confirms you are well enough to work, checks infection rules have been met, asks whether anything at work contributed to your absence, and agrees any support you need — for example a phased return, lighter duties or changes to your visits for a while. It is recorded in the HR pack.

## 7. Long-term absence

Absence of more than four weeks is long-term. We keep in regular, reasonable contact, and hold welfare meetings to understand how you are and when you might return. With your consent, we may ask your GP or an occupational health service for advice on what support or adjustments would help. If you are unable to return to your role in a reasonable time, even with adjustments, we will consider other suitable work and, as a last resort, whether your employment can continue — through the capability part of our Disciplinary policy, with your right to be accompanied and to appeal.

## 8. Frequent short absences

Frequent short absences are hard to cover and can mean the people we support see unfamiliar faces. We monitor absence using trigger points, which may include a Bradford Factor score set by the company. Reaching a trigger point is a prompt for a conversation, not an automatic sanction. Your manager meets you to understand the reasons and agree how to improve, and sets a review period. If absence remains at a level we cannot sustain, it may be dealt with under the capability part of our Disciplinary policy. Absence related to a disability, to pregnancy, or to an injury at work is considered with that in mind.

## 9. Sickness during annual leave

If you are ill during annual leave, tell us on the first day of illness and provide evidence as above. The days you were ill may then be treated as sickness and your leave taken another time.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — oversees absence, makes sure return-to-work interviews and welfare meetings happen, and decides on adjustments and capability action.
- **Office and on-call staff** — record absences, arrange cover, and make sure the people we support are told of any changes.
- **Employees** — report sickness as set out above, keep in touch, provide evidence, and attend return-to-work and welfare meetings.

## 11. Records

Absences, reasons, fit notes and return-to-work interviews are recorded in the HR pack. Health information is kept confidentially and seen only by those who need it. Sickness records are kept for the period our record retention schedule requires for pay and employment records.
