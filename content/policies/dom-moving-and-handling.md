## 1. Purpose

Helping a client to move — out of bed, onto a commode, into a chair — is part of most visits, and done badly it hurts clients and care workers alike. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} assesses moving and handling in clients' own homes, decides how many care workers a task needs, makes sure the equipment we use is safe, and keeps clients as independent as they can be.

## 2. Scope

It applies to every care worker, senior and manager who helps a client to move, and to every piece of moving and handling equipment we use in a client's home, whoever owns it.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 — our duty to protect the health and safety of our staff and of the people affected by our work.
- Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992 — avoid hazardous manual handling where we can, assess what cannot be avoided, and reduce the risk of injury.
- Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — the duty to assess risks and to act on what the assessment finds.
- Lifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations 1998 (LOLER) — lifting equipment must be suitable, safe and thoroughly examined by a competent person at the intervals the regulations set; for equipment and accessories used to lift people, such as hoists and slings, that is at least every six months.
- Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998 — work equipment must be suitable, maintained and used only by people trained to use it.
- Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR) — certain injuries to staff and others, and dangerous occurrences, must be reported to the Health and Safety Executive.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, and Regulation 15: Premises and equipment.

## 4. Our policy

- No care worker lifts a client's whole weight by hand, except in a life-threatening emergency.
- Every client who needs help to move has a moving and handling risk assessment before care starts, and the care plan says exactly how each move is done, with what equipment and by how many care workers.
- We encourage clients to do as much as they safely can for themselves.
- A task assessed as needing two care workers is never done by one.
- We do not use equipment that is damaged, unfamiliar to the care worker, or out of date for its thorough examination.
- The number of care workers on a visit is changed only after a reassessment, never because a rota is short.

## 5. Assessing moving and handling

A trained assessor from the office carries out the moving and handling risk assessment in the client's home at the start of the package, using the community occupational therapist's or physiotherapist's assessment where there is one. It looks at the client (their weight, mobility, pain, skin, understanding, and how they vary through the day), the task, the space and the furniture, the equipment, and the care workers who will do it.

The assessment is reviewed whenever the client's needs change — after a fall, a hospital stay, a new diagnosis or a change in how they manage — and at every care plan review.

### 5.1 Single-handed and double-up visits

Whether a visit needs one care worker or two is part of the assessment. Some clients who once needed two care workers can be moved safely by one with the right equipment and technique. That decision is made only by a community occupational therapist or a trained assessor, with the client, in writing, and after care workers have been shown the new method. We do not reduce a double-up to a single-handed visit on our own initiative.

If the assessor thinks a client needs two care workers but the package has been commissioned for one, the registered manager raises it with the commissioner in writing straight away. Until it is resolved, we do not carry out the task single-handed.

### 5.2 The community occupational therapist

Most equipment in clients' homes is assessed for and supplied by the community occupational therapist or the community equipment service. Ask the office to refer to them when a client's mobility changes, when equipment no longer fits the client or the space, or when a task cannot be done safely as it is planned.

## 6. Equipment in clients' homes

Equipment in a client's home may belong to the community equipment service, to the client, or occasionally to us. Whoever owns it, you must be satisfied it is safe before you use it.

- The owner of lifting equipment is responsible for its thorough examination and servicing. For loan equipment this is the community equipment service; it usually puts a label on the equipment showing the date of the last examination.
- Where a client has bought their own hoist or sling, the registered manager agrees with the client or their family how it will be thoroughly examined and serviced, and records that agreement. We do not use privately owned lifting equipment that has no record of a thorough examination.
- Every sling is for one client only, and must be the type and size their assessment specifies.

### 6.1 Before each use

- Check the hoist, sling, stand aid, slide sheet, bed or other equipment for damage: frayed or torn fabric, worn loops or stitching, cracks, missing parts, a battery that is not charged.
- Check the examination label is in date. If the label is missing or out of date, do not use the equipment.
- Check the sling is the right one for the client and is clean.
- Make sure the brakes, bed height and space are right before you start.

### 6.2 If something is wrong

If the equipment is faulty, missing, out of date for examination, or not the equipment in the care plan, do not use it. Make the client safe and comfortable, phone the office or on-call, and record what happened. The office reports the fault to the owner, chases the repair or replacement, and agrees with the client how care will be given in the meantime.

## 7. What to do on a visit

- Read the care plan and moving and handling plan before you help the client to move. If it is not clear, phone the office before you start.
- Explain what you are going to do and ask the client's consent. Let them do what they can.
- On a double-up visit, wait for the second care worker. Do not start a two-person task alone. If your colleague has not arrived, phone the office, do the parts of the visit that are safe for one, and wait or return as the office directs.
- Agree who leads the move and use clear, agreed commands.
- Stop if the client is in pain, frightened, or unwell, or if anything changes.

### 7.1 If a client falls

Do not lift a client from the floor by hand. Check them for injury, keep them warm and reassured, and phone 999 if they are hurt, unwell or you are in any doubt. If they are not hurt and their care plan includes equipment for getting up from the floor that you have been trained to use, follow it. Otherwise phone the office or on-call for advice. See our Falls Prevention and Management policy.

### 7.2 Injuries and near misses

Report any injury or near miss to the office the same day. It is recorded through the site's concerns process as an incident, the risk assessment is reviewed, and the registered manager decides whether it must be reported under RIDDOR or to the Care Quality Commission.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure every client has an up-to-date moving and handling assessment, that assessors are trained, that equipment is safe and examined, and that commissioners are told when a package does not meet assessed needs.
- **Moving and handling assessors** — complete and review assessments, write clear plans, refer to occupational therapists, and show care workers how each move is done.
- **Office and on-call staff** — roster double-up visits so both care workers arrive together, and act on equipment faults the same day.
- **Care workers** — follow the moving and handling plan, check equipment before each use, never attempt a two-person task alone, and report changes, faults and incidents.

## 9. Training

All care workers complete practical moving and handling training, including hoists and slide sheets, during induction and before they help any client to move, and refresh it every year. Competency is observed in clients' homes and recorded on the person's file; spot checks include moving and handling. Care workers are shown any new or unusual equipment before using it. Assessors complete further training before they assess.

## 10. Records

Moving and handling risk assessments and plans are kept on the client's care record, with a note of the equipment, who owns it, and its examination date. Equipment faults and incidents are kept through the concerns process. Training is on the training matrix.
