## 1. Purpose

Some residents, particularly those living with dementia, may leave the home without anyone knowing and be unable to find their way back or keep themselves safe. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} reduces the risk of a resident going missing without restricting residents more than we need to, and exactly what staff do when a resident cannot be found, so that every minute is used well.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all staff on every shift, including nights, weekends and agency staff, and to every resident. It covers residents who leave the home unnoticed and residents who do not return when expected from going out.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, and Regulation 13: Safeguarding service users from abuse and improper treatment (including that residents must not be deprived of their liberty without lawful authority).
- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, Regulation 18 — notifying CQC of incidents reported to or investigated by the police, and of serious injuries.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 and the Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards — residents with capacity can come and go as they choose; restrictions on residents who lack capacity must be in their best interests, the least restrictive option, and, where they amount to a deprivation of liberty, authorised.
- The Herbert Protocol — a national scheme run by police forces, in which information about a person at risk of going missing is gathered in advance and handed to the police if they go missing.

## 4. Our policy

- Residents with capacity are free to come and go. We ask them, not tell them, to let us know when they go out and when they expect to be back.
- Every resident's risk of going missing is assessed on admission and reviewed, and the care plan says how it is managed in the least restrictive way.
- Every resident at risk has a completed Herbert Protocol form, kept up to date and ready to give the police.
- When a resident at risk cannot be found, we search at once, and call the police without delay. We would rather call the police and find the resident a few minutes later than wait.

## 5. Reducing the risk

### 5.1 Assessment

On admission, and at every care plan review, the registered manager or a senior assesses whether the resident might leave without staff knowing and be at risk if they did. The assessment considers their memory and orientation, their history of walking or leaving places, their routines and past life (such as going to work or collecting children), their health and medicines, and what has unsettled them before. Walking about with purpose is often a sign of a need — pain, boredom, the toilet, looking for someone — and the care plan says how staff meet it.

### 5.2 The Herbert Protocol form

For every resident assessed as at risk, staff complete a Herbert Protocol form with the resident and their family. It includes a recent photograph, a physical description, medical needs and medicines, how they communicate, places they have lived, worked or like to go, and anything they may say or do. It is kept with the care plan, in a place every shift leader can reach in a hurry, and updated when anything changes, including a new photograph when their appearance changes.

### 5.3 The building

- Doors and gates are secured in a way that keeps residents at risk safe without locking in residents who can come and go. Keypads and locked doors are a restriction; for residents who cannot leave freely and lack capacity to decide where to live, the registered manager considers whether a Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards authorisation is needed. See our Deprivation of Liberty policy.
- Door alarms, sensors and other monitoring are used only where they are in the resident's care plan, are the least restrictive option, and have been agreed with the resident or in their best interests.
- Visitors are asked to sign in and out and to make sure doors close behind them.

### 5.4 Knowing where residents are

The shift leader checks at every handover that all residents are accounted for. Residents who go out sign out, or tell staff, where they are willing to.

## 6. What to do when a resident cannot be found

### 6.1 Raise the alarm

As soon as you realise a resident cannot be found, tell the shift leader straight away. Do not assume they are with a visitor or in another room.

### 6.2 Immediate search

The shift leader takes charge, notes the time, and organises a quick, systematic search, while keeping other residents safe:

- every room, including bathrooms, toilets, cupboards, laundry, kitchen, store rooms, plant rooms and empty bedrooms, and under beds;
- the garden, grounds, outbuildings, car park and the area just outside the home;
- the signing-out book, CCTV where the home has it, and whether any door alarm has sounded;
- whether the resident has gone out with family, to an appointment, or been taken to hospital.

### 6.3 Call the police

If the resident is not found by the search, the shift leader phones 999, says that a vulnerable adult is missing from a care home, and gives the Herbert Protocol form to the police. The shift leader does not wait for the search to finish before calling 999 if the resident is at particular risk — for example, they are frail, need regular medicines, it is dark or the weather is cold or very hot, or the home is near a main road or water.

> Note any local features near the home that staff should check first or tell the police about, such as a bus stop, a former home, or a river.

### 6.4 Tell people

The shift leader then:

- tells the registered manager or on-call manager, who comes to the home if needed;
- tells the resident's next of kin, attorney or deputy;
- keeps searching nearby only if there are enough staff to keep other residents safe, and with phones, in pairs, and following the police's advice;
- records the time and every action taken.

### 6.5 When a resident with capacity is late back

A resident with capacity who is late back from going out is not necessarily missing. Try their mobile and anyone they were with. If you are worried about their safety, because of their health, the time or the weather, or it is out of character, treat it as a missing resident and phone the police.

## 7. When the resident is found

- Welcome them back calmly and without blame.
- Check them for injury, cold, dehydration or distress, and call the GP or NHS 111, or 999 if they are hurt or unwell. Give any medicine that was missed if it is safe to, following advice.
- Tell the police, the family, and anyone else who was told.
- When the resident is settled, talk with them, if they are able, about where they went and why.

## 8. After the event

The registered manager:

- notifies the Care Quality Commission under Regulation 18 where the police were involved or the resident was seriously injured;
- tells the local authority or NHS commissioner, and the Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards supervisory body if the resident has an authorisation;
- raises a safeguarding concern if the resident was harmed or put at risk, or if there may have been neglect;
- records the incident through the site's concerns process and reviews it with the staff involved: how the resident left, how long before it was noticed, and what will reduce the risk without adding unnecessary restrictions;
- reviews the resident's risk assessment, care plan and Herbert Protocol form.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure every at-risk resident is assessed and has a Herbert Protocol form, that the building is safe without being unduly restrictive, and that every missing episode is notified and learned from.
- **Shift leaders** — account for every resident at handover, take charge when a resident is missing, call the police, and keep records.
- **Care staff** — know which residents are at risk, raise the alarm straight away, search as directed, and support the resident on their return.

## 10. Training

All staff learn this policy, including the search plan, during induction, and refresh it every year. Shift leaders practise the procedure, including at night, and know where Herbert Protocol forms are kept.

## 11. Records

Risk assessments and Herbert Protocol forms are kept with the resident's care plan. The record of every missing episode, notifications and reviews are kept through the concerns process for as long as our record retention schedule requires.
