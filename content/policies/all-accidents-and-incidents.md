## 1. Purpose

Things go wrong in care, however careful everyone is. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} makes people safe when an accident or incident happens, how we record and look into it, who we tell, and how we learn from it so that it is less likely to happen again. Near misses matter as much as accidents: they show us a risk before anyone is hurt.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us, and to every accident, incident and near miss that involves the people we support, our staff, visitors or anyone else affected by our work — in a person's own home, in a care home, in our office, or while travelling for work.

- An accident is an unplanned event that causes injury, illness or damage — a fall, a burn, a cut, a vehicle collision.
- An incident is any other event that causes, or could cause, harm or disruption — a medicines error, a missed visit, a fire, a threat or assault, a lost key, a data breach, a person going missing.
- A near miss is an event that could have caused harm but did not, by luck or because someone acted in time — a sling found to be frayed before use, the wrong tablets noticed before they were given.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 — our duty to protect staff and anyone affected by our work.
- Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — the duty to assess risks and review them when something shows they are not controlled.
- Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR) — some injuries, diseases and dangerous occurrences must be reported to the Health and Safety Executive.
- Social Security (Claims and Payments) Regulations 1979 — the record of accidents at work to employees that employers above a certain size must keep, and which we keep whatever our size.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment; Regulation 17: Good governance; Regulation 20: Duty of candour.
- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, Regulations 16 and 18 — notifying CQC of deaths and other incidents.

## 4. Our policy

- The safety of the person comes first; the paperwork comes second, but it always comes.
- Every accident, incident and near miss is reported the same day, however minor it seems.
- We do not look for someone to blame. We look for what happened and why, so we can put it right. Staff who report honestly are supported. Deliberate harm, recklessness and cover-ups are a different matter, dealt with under our Disciplinary policy.
- Where someone has been harmed, we are open with them and their family, in line with our Duty of Candour policy.
- Every incident is reviewed by a manager, and patterns are looked at across all incidents.

## 5. What to do

### 5.1 Straight away

- Make the situation safe for the person, yourself and anyone else. Do not put yourself in danger.
- Give first aid if you are trained to and it is needed. Call 999 for an emergency, or 111 for urgent advice. See First Aid, Medical Emergencies and Deterioration.
- If a person has fallen, do not lift them or help them up until you have checked for injury, and follow our Falls Prevention and Management policy. Anyone who has hit their head, or who takes blood-thinning medicines, needs medical advice even if they seem well.
- Tell the office or on-call (in home care) or the shift leader (in a care home) as soon as the person is safe.
- If the incident is serious — a death, a serious injury, a suspected assault or crime — leave things as they are, as far as you safely can, so that nothing is lost that the police or an investigation may need.

### 5.2 Recording it

Record the accident, incident or near miss through the site's concerns process the same day, before you finish your shift. Write:

- what happened, where and when, in the order it happened;
- who was involved and who saw it;
- any injury, using a body map where it helps, and what first aid or treatment was given;
- who you told, and what they advised;
- what you did to make things safe.

Write what you saw and heard, not what you think happened. If you did not see it — for example, you found a person on the floor — say so. Also record it in the person's daily notes, so the next worker knows.

If you are hurt at work yourself, report it in the same way, however small the injury. It is also recorded as an accident at work to an employee.

### 5.3 What the manager does

{{registered_manager}}, or the manager on duty, reviews every report within one working day and decides:

- whether the person needs further medical help, and whether their family or representative should be told;
- whether it is a safeguarding concern, and if so refers it to the local authority (see Safeguarding);
- whether it must be reported to CQC, the Health and Safety Executive under RIDDOR, the commissioner, the police or anyone else (see Reportable Incidents);
- whether the duty of candour applies;
- how far it needs to be looked into.

### 5.4 Looking into it

The depth of the review matches the seriousness of what happened. A near miss with an obvious cause may need only a note of the action taken. A serious incident is investigated by a manager who was not involved: they gather statements, records, visit times, equipment checks and training records, and they talk to the person affected and their family where appropriate.

Every review asks the same questions: what happened, why it happened, whether it could have been prevented, and what will stop it happening again. The answer is rarely one person's mistake alone. Look for the things around it — staffing, rotas, training, equipment, information in the care plan, communication between staff.

The findings and the actions agreed are recorded with the incident, with a named person and a date for each action. The manager checks that each action is done before the incident is closed.

### 5.5 After the incident

- The person's care plan and risk assessments are updated where the incident shows they need to change.
- The person affected, and their family where they wish, are told what we found and what we are doing about it.
- Staff involved are told the outcome and offered support. A frightening or distressing incident can affect people for some time.
- Equipment involved in an incident is taken out of use and checked before it is used again.

## 6. Learning from incidents

{{registered_manager}} looks at all accidents, incidents and near misses together at least every month, to spot patterns: the same person falling at the same time of day, medicines errors on one run or one shift, injuries linked to one piece of equipment. Trends and the actions taken are reported to {{nominated_individual}} as part of our governance checks.

Lessons are shared with staff without naming anyone — in team meetings, in supervision, and through updates to policies, care plans and training. Where an incident shows a gap in someone's knowledge, it is put right through training or a competency check recorded on their file.

## 7. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure every incident is reported, reviewed and acted on, makes the notifications that are needed, and leads the monthly review of trends.
- **Nominated individual** — checks that incidents are being managed and that learning leads to change.
- **Office, on-call staff and shift leaders** — give advice at the time, make sure the person is safe, and make sure the incident is recorded.
- **All staff** — make things safe, report every accident, incident and near miss the same day, and take part honestly in any review.

## 8. Training

Reporting accidents, incidents and near misses is covered at induction, with first aid and health and safety, and refreshed every year. Managers who investigate serious incidents are trained to do so.

## 9. Records

Accidents, incidents, near misses, reviews and actions are kept through the concerns process, linked to the person's record. Accidents to employees are kept in a form that meets the legal requirement for an accident record. Records are kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention, and for longer where a claim or investigation is possible.
