## 1. Purpose

Food and drink are among the things residents care about most, and getting them right is central to their health. Malnutrition and dehydration are common in care homes and lead to falls, infections, pressure ulcers, confusion and hospital admissions. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} makes sure every resident eats and drinks well and as they choose, and how the home runs its kitchen safely, including how it manages allergens.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works in the home — care staff, shift leaders, the cook and kitchen staff, and anyone who serves or helps residents with food and drink — and to every resident.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 14: Meeting nutritional and hydration needs — enough to eat and drink, to meet residents' needs and preferences, with support to eat and drink where needed.
- Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment — including following the advice of speech and language therapists and dietitians.
- Food Safety Act 1990 and the Food Safety and Hygiene (England) Regulations 2013 — the home is a food business: it must be registered with the local authority, have food safety procedures based on hazard analysis (HACCP) principles, and make sure food handlers are supervised and trained in food hygiene in proportion to their work.
- Food Information Regulations 2014 and the assimilated food information law — we must be able to tell residents and visitors whether food we serve contains any of the 14 allergens the law lists.
- Malnutrition Universal Screening Tool (MUST), published by the British Association for Parenteral and Enteral Nutrition.
- International Dysphagia Diet Standardisation Initiative (IDDSI) framework — the standard descriptions of food textures and drink thicknesses.
- NICE guideline CG32, Nutrition support for adults, and NICE quality standard QS24, Nutrition support in adults.
- Food Standards Agency guidance, including Safer Food, Better Business for caterers.

## 4. Our policy

- Residents choose what, when and where they eat, from a varied, nutritious menu that reflects their tastes, culture and faith. Food and drink are available at any time of day or night.
- Every resident's nutrition and hydration needs are assessed on admission, recorded, and reviewed.
- Mealtimes are unhurried and sociable, with help given discreetly to those who need it.
- Texture-modified diets, thickened drinks and special diets are prepared and served exactly as prescribed.
- The kitchen meets food safety law, and allergen information is accurate for every dish.

## 5. Assessment and planning

On admission, the shift leader or a senior records each resident's likes and dislikes, allergies and intolerances, cultural and religious needs, special diets, any difficulty with chewing, swallowing or using cutlery, and any texture-modified diet or thickened drinks with the IDDSI level. They complete a MUST screen, including weight and height or an alternative measure. The information the kitchen needs is passed to the cook in writing the same day.

Residents are weighed and screened again at least monthly, or more often if their care plan or the MUST result says so. A medium or high score leads to the actions in the care plan: food fortification, snacks between meals, a food and fluid record, and referral to the GP and dietitian where indicated.

## 6. Mealtimes and drinks

### 6.1 Menus and choice

The cook plans menus with residents and changes them through the year. Menus are displayed in a format residents can read, with pictures where they help. Residents choose close to the meal; for people living with dementia, show them the plates so they can choose by sight and smell. An alternative is always available.

### 6.2 Protected mealtimes

Mealtimes are protected from non-urgent tasks such as medicines rounds where possible, cleaning and visits from professionals, unless the resident wants otherwise. Visitors are welcome to join residents for meals.

### 6.3 Helping residents eat

Make sure residents are sitting upright, with their glasses, teeth and hearing aids, and offer handwashing. Sit beside a resident you are helping, at their eye level, and go at their pace. Do not stand over them or help two people at once. Offer adapted cutlery and crockery as their plan says.

### 6.4 Drinks and snacks

Offer drinks regularly throughout the day and night, and leave a fresh drink within reach of every resident, including those in their rooms. Snacks are available at all times, including overnight, for anyone who is awake and hungry. Report signs of dehydration: dark urine, a dry mouth, headaches, new confusion, dizziness and constipation.

### 6.5 Food and fluid records

Where the care plan asks, record what the resident actually ate and drank, specifically — "half a portion of shepherd's pie, 150 ml of tea" — straight after the meal or drink. The shift leader checks the records at the end of each shift and acts on poor intake, and handover includes any resident who has eaten or drunk too little.

## 7. Swallowing difficulties and choking

Residents with swallowing difficulties are referred to the speech and language therapist through the GP. Their plan sets out the IDDSI level for food and drinks, their position for eating, and how to help them. Kitchen and care staff check every texture-modified meal and thickened drink against the plan before it is served. Thickening powder is stored securely, out of reach of residents, and never left on tables or trolleys.

If a resident is choking, follow your first aid training: encourage them to cough; if they cannot, give back blows, then abdominal thrusts if back blows do not work. Call for the shift leader and phone 999 if the blockage does not clear, or if the resident becomes unresponsive, and start CPR if they are not breathing normally. Anyone who has had abdominal thrusts must be seen by a doctor. Record the incident through the site's concerns process and ask for a swallowing review.

A resident who can make the decision may choose to eat food that has been advised against; this is recorded as a choice with the risks discussed (see Choice and Positive Risk-Taking).

## 8. The kitchen

### 8.1 Food safety management

The home is registered as a food business with the local authority. The cook keeps a written food safety management system based on HACCP principles — we use the Food Standards Agency's Safer Food, Better Business pack or an equivalent — and keeps its daily records: fridge and freezer temperatures, cooking and reheating temperatures, cooling, deliveries, cleaning and opening and closing checks. The registered manager checks the records at least monthly, and acts on the local authority's food hygiene inspections.

### 8.2 Day-to-day safety

- Only staff trained in food hygiene prepare food. Anyone with diarrhoea or vomiting does not handle food until they have been free of symptoms for the time our Infection Prevention and Control policy sets.
- Food is stored, labelled, dated and rotated correctly, and anything past its use-by date is thrown away.
- Food brought in by visitors for a resident is labelled with the resident's name and date and stored separately; staff check it is safe for that resident's diet.
- Kitchen cleaning schedules are followed and signed. Pest control is in place.
- Residents who like to help in the kitchen or make their own snacks are supported to do so safely.

### 8.3 Allergens

The cook keeps an up-to-date record of the allergens in every dish and every product used, from recipes and supplier labels, and updates it whenever a recipe or supplier changes. Each resident's allergies are recorded and known to the kitchen and to care staff. Food for a resident with an allergy is prepared and served to avoid cross-contact, and checked against their allergy before it is served. Staff can tell any resident or visitor what allergens a dish contains. If you are not sure, do not guess — ask the cook or check the record.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure nutrition needs are assessed and met, the kitchen meets food law, and referrals are made.
- **Cook** — plans menus with residents, runs the kitchen safely, keeps food safety and allergen records, and prepares special diets correctly.
- **Shift leaders** — complete MUST screens and weights, check food and fluid records, and act on concerns.
- **Care staff** — help residents eat and drink, record intake, and report changes.

## 10. Training

Care and kitchen staff complete food hygiene, allergen awareness, nutrition and hydration, and dysphagia training including the IDDSI framework during induction, and refresh it at the interval on the training matrix. The cook holds a food hygiene qualification suitable for running a care home kitchen. Choking is covered in first aid training. Shift leaders are trained to use the MUST tool before they screen.

## 11. Records

Nutrition assessments, weights, MUST screens, professional advice and food and fluid records are kept on the resident's record. Food safety records, allergen information and menus are kept in the kitchen for as long as the local authority and our retention schedule require.
