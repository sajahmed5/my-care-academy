## 1. Purpose

Infections such as norovirus, flu and COVID-19 can spread quickly among the people we support, many of whom are frail, and among the staff who care for them. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} recognises an outbreak early, who we tell, and how we limit its spread while making sure people still get the care they need.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us. It covers outbreaks of respiratory infections (such as flu, COVID-19 and RSV) and gastrointestinal infections (such as norovirus), and other infections where public health advice is given. Our everyday precautions are in Infection Prevention and Control; this policy covers what we do in addition when an outbreak is suspected.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008: Code of Practice on the prevention and control of infections and related guidance — what registered providers must do to prevent and control infection.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, which includes assessing, preventing, detecting and controlling the spread of infection.
- UK Health Security Agency guidance on infection prevention and control in adult social care, and on managing outbreaks of acute respiratory and gastrointestinal infections in care settings.
- Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 — protecting staff from exposure to harmful biological agents at work.
- Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 — some infections caught at work must be reported to the Health and Safety Executive.
- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, Regulation 18 — notifying CQC of events that stop, or may stop, the service running safely.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 9A: Visiting and accompanying in care homes — residents can still receive visitors during an outbreak.

## 4. Our policy

- We act on the first signs of an outbreak, not when it is established.
- We tell the UK Health Security Agency's local health protection team about a suspected outbreak, and follow its advice.
- Staff who are unwell with an infection stay away from work, and are not penalised for doing so.
- Care continues during an outbreak. We plan visits and shifts so that those who need care most receive it.
- We encourage staff and the people we support to be vaccinated, and make it easy for them.

## 5. Recognising an outbreak

An outbreak is usually two or more people with similar symptoms that are linked in time and place — for example, two residents on the same floor with diarrhoea and vomiting within a few days, or several clients and care workers on the same run with a respiratory illness. Look out for:

- diarrhoea or vomiting;
- a new cough, high temperature, sore throat, breathlessness, or a general decline in someone who is usually well;
- confusion, falls or loss of appetite in an older person, which may be the first sign of infection.

Report any of these to the office or on-call (in home care) or the shift leader (in a care home) on the same shift, and record it in the person's notes. Ask the person's GP for advice where they are unwell.

## 6. What the registered manager does

When an outbreak is suspected, {{registered_manager}} or the manager on duty:

- contacts the local UK Health Security Agency health protection team, or the local authority infection prevention and control team where that is the local arrangement, and follows their advice on testing, precautions and when the outbreak is over;
- keeps a list of everyone affected — people we support and staff — with the date their symptoms started, their symptoms, test results and outcome;
- tells the GPs of the people affected;
- tells commissioners as their contracts require, and notifies CQC if the outbreak stops, or may stop, us running the service safely (see Reportable Incidents);
- tells staff, the people we support and their families what is happening and what is being done;
- reviews staffing every day, using our Business Continuity and Emergency Planning arrangements if needed.

> Add the contact details of the UKHSA health protection team, and any local authority infection prevention and control team, for the areas you work in.

## 7. Staff who are unwell

- Do not come to work with diarrhoea or vomiting until 48 hours after your last symptoms, or longer if the health protection team advises.
- If you have symptoms of a respiratory infection and a high temperature, or do not feel well enough to work, stay at home until you are well, and follow current UK Health Security Agency guidance on testing and on when to return.
- Tell the office or the shift leader straight away, as set out in our Sickness Absence policy.

When looking at absence levels, the registered manager takes into account absence caused by an outbreak, so that no one is penalised for staying away as this policy asks.

## 8. Controlling the spread

### 8.1 In a person's own home

- The office plans visits so that, as far as possible, a small number of the same care workers visit clients with symptoms, and visits clients with symptoms at the end of a run rather than before visiting others.
- Care workers wear the personal protective equipment advised for the infection, put it on and take it off correctly, and dispose of it as the care plan says.
- Hand hygiene is carried out on arrival, before and after care, and on leaving.
- Care workers clean surfaces and equipment they use with the products advised, and keep laundry handling to a minimum.
- If visits cannot all be covered, the office follows the priority list in our Business Continuity and Emergency Planning policy, and tells clients and families of any change.

### 8.2 In a care home

- Residents with symptoms are cared for in their own rooms where possible, with their own equipment, until they are no longer infectious or as the health protection team advises.
- Where several residents are affected, care staff are allocated to care for them as a group, and do not move between affected and unaffected areas during a shift.
- Communal activities and dining may be paused in affected areas, but residents are not isolated for longer than advice requires.
- Cleaning of rooms, touch points and equipment is increased, using the products advised.
- Admissions, transfers and readmissions from hospital are decided case by case, with advice from the health protection team. We tell hospitals and other services about the outbreak before anyone is transferred.
- Visiting continues. Visitors are told about the outbreak, asked to follow the precautions in place and to stay away if they are unwell. If visiting in an affected area has to be limited on public health advice, it is limited for as short a time as possible, and each resident can still receive at least one visitor, in line with Regulation 9A and CQC's guidance on it.

## 9. Vaccination

Vaccination is one of the best protections against flu, COVID-19 and some other infections. We encourage all staff to be vaccinated when they are eligible, tell them where and when vaccines are available, and allow time to attend where we can. Vaccination is a personal choice, and no one is treated less favourably for declining. We support the people we support to have the vaccines offered to them, and record their decisions in their care record.

## 10. After an outbreak

The outbreak is declared over when the health protection team agrees. The registered manager then carries out a deep clean where needed, holds a short review of what went well and what could be better, and updates this policy, the business continuity plan and training as a result.

## 11. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — leads the response, contacts the health protection team, keeps the outbreak records and makes notifications.
- **Office, on-call staff and shift leaders** — report suspected cases straight away, organise visits and staffing, and keep information flowing.
- **All staff** — recognise symptoms, report them, stay away when unwell, and follow the precautions advised.

## 12. Training

All staff complete infection prevention and control training at induction and refresh it every year, including the use of personal protective equipment and hand hygiene. Managers are trained in managing an outbreak.

## 13. Records

The list of cases, contacts with the health protection team, notifications and the post-outbreak review are kept, and individual cases are recorded in each person's care record. Infections caught by staff that may be work-related are recorded through the site's concerns process. Records are kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
