## 1. Purpose

A good start makes a good care worker. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} welcomes new staff, teaches them what they need before they work alone, supports people new to care through the Care Certificate, and decides at the end of probation whether the job is right for them and for us.

## 2. Scope

It applies to every new employee: care staff, office staff and managers, whether new to care or experienced. It also applies, in a shortened form, to existing staff moving into a new role. Agency staff receive the shorter induction in Safe Staffing, Rotas and Agency Staff.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 18: Staffing — staff receive the induction, training and supervision they need to do their job.
- Regulation 19: Fit and proper persons employed — staff are competent for the work they do.
- Skills for Care, the Care Certificate — the agreed standards a care worker new to care must meet. CQC expects those new to care to complete it, normally within 12 weeks of starting.
- Health and Care Act 2022 — registered providers must make sure staff receive training in learning disability and autism appropriate to their role (see Training and Development).
- Employment Rights Act 1996 — written terms of employment from the first day.

## 4. Our policy

- Nobody starts work until the checks in our Recruitment and Right to Work and Sponsored Workers policies are complete.
- Nobody works alone, or with a person they have not been introduced to, before they have had the training and shadowing set out below and been judged ready.
- Everyone new to care completes the Care Certificate, normally within 12 weeks of starting.
- Probation is a two-way trial. We support new staff to succeed, review progress honestly, and tell people early if something is not going well.

## 5. Induction

### 5.1 The first day

Every new starter is welcomed by their manager and shown:

- who's who, how to reach the office and on-call, and what to do in an emergency;
- their login on the site, where they complete training, read and accept policies, and see their tasks;
- the policies set for their role, which the site gives them on the day they start with a date to accept them by — safeguarding, whistleblowing, confidentiality and the code of conduct first, then lone working in homecare or fire safety in a care home;
- their uniform, ID badge and personal protective equipment;
- their contract and the Employee Handbook.

### 5.2 Training before working with people

Before they give care, new care staff complete the training the company sets as required for their role, including practical moving and handling, infection prevention, safeguarding, basic life support and, where they will give medicines, medication training followed by a competency assessment. The training matrix on the site shows what is required and what is done.

### 5.3 Shadowing

New care staff shadow an experienced colleague — on visits in homecare, on shifts in a care home — before working alone. They meet the people they will support, learn their routines and preferences from them and from their care plans, and practise under supervision. The number of shadow shifts depends on the person's experience and confidence, and is extended for anyone who needs more. A senior or manager confirms they are ready to work alone, and records it.

In homecare, a new care worker's first visits to each client are, where possible, with someone the client already knows. In a care home, new care staff work alongside a named colleague on their first shifts and are not left in charge of a floor or unit.

## 6. The Care Certificate

Care staff who are new to care work towards the Care Certificate from the start. They complete the learning on the site, and a trained assessor observes them in their everyday work to confirm they meet each standard in practice. Knowledge is not enough: each standard is signed off only when it has been seen.

New staff who already hold the Care Certificate from a previous employer bring their certificate. We do not simply accept it: a manager checks their knowledge and observes their practice, and we fill any gaps.

If someone has not completed the Care Certificate within 12 weeks, their manager agrees a plan and a new date, and the reasons are recorded.

## 7. Probation

### 7.1 How long

Probation lasts six months unless the contract says otherwise. Reviews are held at one month, three months and six months. The site books each review, shows it to the manager when it is due, and tells the employee the date.

### 7.2 Reviews

At each review the manager and employee talk about conduct and performance: what is going well, what needs to improve, and what support is needed. The review uses what we already know — induction and Care Certificate progress, training completed and overdue, spot checks, competency assessments and supervisions held — and feedback from the people we support and colleagues. Agreed actions are recorded with dates.

Concerns are raised as soon as they arise, not saved for a review. An employee who is struggling is told plainly what needs to change, is given support, and has time to improve.

### 7.3 Outcomes

At the end of probation the outcome is one of three:

- Passed — confirmed in writing, and employment continues.
- Extended — when more time would help, the employee is told in writing how long the extension is, why, and what they need to show. Probation is extended only once, unless there is a good reason, such as a long absence.
- Not passed — when the employee has not reached the standard despite support. They are invited to a meeting, told the concerns in advance, and may bring a colleague or trade union representative. If employment ends, the decision and the notice are confirmed in writing, with the right to appeal.

Serious misconduct during probation is dealt with under the principles of our Disciplinary policy.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure every new starter has a full induction, that probation reviews happen on time, and decides probation outcomes.
- **Seniors and assessors** — lead shadowing, observe Care Certificate standards and competencies, and give honest feedback.
- **New employees** — complete their training and the Care Certificate, ask when they are unsure, and take part in reviews.

## 9. Training

Assessors who sign off the Care Certificate are experienced, competent in the standards they assess, and trained to assess. Managers who hold probation reviews are trained in giving feedback and in our Disciplinary policy.

## 10. Records

The induction checklist, shadow shifts, sign-off to work alone, Care Certificate evidence and probation reviews and letters are kept on the employee's file on the site, and form part of their training and employment record. They are kept for six years after employment ends.
