## 1. Purpose

This is the health and safety policy of {{organisation}}. It sets out our commitment to keeping our staff, our clients and anyone else affected by our work safe, and how we organise ourselves to do it. Most of our work happens in clients' own homes, which we do not own or control, so much of this policy is about how we manage risks in places where the client, not us, makes the rules.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us: care workers, office staff, managers, on-call staff and volunteers. It covers our office at {{office_address}}, the homes of every client we visit, and travel between them.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 — our duty to protect the health, safety and welfare of our employees and of anyone else affected by our work, and employees' duty to take care of themselves and others. Employers with five or more employees must have a written health and safety policy.
- Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — the duty to assess risks, put controls in place, appoint competent help, and give staff information and training; and to assess the risks to new and expectant mothers and young workers.
- Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992; Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002; Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998; Lifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations 1998; Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013.
- Workplace (Health, Safety and Welfare) Regulations 1992 and Health and Safety (Display Screen Equipment) Regulations 1992 — for our office.
- Health and Safety (Consultation with Employees) Regulations 1996 — we consult staff on health and safety.
- Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 — we hold employers' liability insurance and make the certificate available to staff.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment.

## 4. Our statement of intent

We will, so far as is reasonably practicable:

- assess the risks of our work, and put in place and keep under review the controls needed to manage them;
- give staff the information, training, equipment and supervision they need to work safely;
- consult staff about health and safety, and act on what they tell us;
- provide a safe office, and safe equipment and protective clothing for our work;
- report and investigate accidents and incidents, and learn from them;
- support staff's health and wellbeing at work.

This statement is signed by {{nominated_individual}} on behalf of the company, and is brought to every member of staff's attention.

## 5. Organisation

- **Nominated individual** — has overall responsibility for health and safety, makes sure the resources are available, and reviews our performance every year.
- **Registered manager** — is responsible day to day: makes sure risk assessments are done and reviewed, staff are trained, equipment is safe, incidents are investigated, and this policy is followed.
- **Office and on-call staff** — make sure care workers have the information they need before a visit, and respond when care workers raise concerns.
- **Care workers and all staff** — take reasonable care of themselves and others, follow their training and the care plan, use the equipment and protective clothing provided, and report hazards, accidents and near misses.

## 6. Working in clients' homes

### 6.1 The client's home is theirs

A client's home is their private space, and they decide how it is arranged and run. We cannot insist on changes, but we have a duty to protect our staff, so we agree with the client how the risks will be managed. If a risk cannot be made acceptable — a dangerous electrical fault, an aggressive dog that cannot be kept in another room, a floor that is unsafe — we explain the problem, suggest solutions, and may change how or when we provide care until it is resolved. In rare cases we may have to stop a part of the care, following our Ending a Service policy.

### 6.2 Before care starts

The environmental risk assessment done at the start of every package covers access, lighting, steps and stairs, floor coverings and trip hazards, heating, electrical and gas appliances the care worker will use, smoke alarms, smoking, pets, anyone else in the home, equipment, and the client's own risks. The findings, and what the care worker must do, are in the care plan. Equipment such as hoists and beds is checked for a current service or examination record (see Equipment and Maintenance). Where it would help, and the client agrees, we ask the local fire and rescue service for a home fire safety visit.

### 6.3 On every visit

- Look out for hazards, and put right what you safely can — a trailing lead, a spill.
- Use only equipment you have been trained to use, and check it before you use it.
- Use the client's cleaning products only as the label says, and never mix them (see COSHH).
- Wear the gloves, aprons and other protective equipment the care plan says.
- Ask the client, politely, not to smoke while you are there, and to keep pets in another room if they make your work unsafe.
- If something new worries you, tell the office before you leave, so the risk assessment can be reviewed.

### 6.4 Emergencies in the home

- If you smell gas, do not use light switches or anything that could cause a spark, open doors and windows, turn off the gas at the meter if it is safe to do so, help the client out, and call the national gas emergency number, 0800 111 999.
- If there is a fire, get yourself and the client out if you safely can, close doors behind you, and call 999. Do not go back in.
- For a water leak or power cut, make the client safe and warm, and tell the office, who will help the client contact the right service.

## 7. Our main risks and how we manage them

Each of these has its own policy:

- moving and handling people — individual risk assessments, equipment, and training (Moving and Handling);
- working alone, and violence or aggression — visit logging, on-call support, and the right to leave (Lone Working; Violence and Aggression Towards Staff);
- driving between visits (Driving at Work);
- infection — hand hygiene and protective equipment (Infection Prevention and Control);
- hazardous substances and equipment (COSHH; Equipment and Maintenance);
- stress and fatigue — reasonable rotas with travel time and breaks (Staff Wellbeing and Stress; Working Time and Rest Breaks).

## 8. Our office

The registered manager makes sure the office meets the workplace regulations: a safe building, adequate heating, lighting, ventilation, toilets and drinking water; display screen assessments for staff who use computers for much of their day; a fire risk assessment, fire alarm and extinguishers, and an evacuation plan; first-aid arrangements; and the health and safety law poster displayed or given to every member of staff.

## 9. New and expectant mothers, and young workers

When a member of staff tells us in writing that she is pregnant, has given birth in the last six months or is breastfeeding, the registered manager carries out an individual risk assessment and adjusts her work — moving and handling, infection risks, long hours, lone working — to remove any risk. A risk assessment is also done for any worker under 18 before they start.

## 10. Consulting staff

We consult staff on health and safety through supervisions, team meetings and spot checks, and ask what they think whenever we change how we work. Anyone can raise a concern with the registered manager at any time. No one is treated unfairly for raising a health and safety concern.

## 11. Training

All staff complete health and safety training during induction, including moving and handling, infection prevention, fire safety, first aid and COSHH, and refresh it every year. Training is recorded on the training matrix. Managers who carry out risk assessments are trained to do so.

## 12. Monitoring

The registered manager checks health and safety through spot checks on care workers, reviews of risk assessments, and the monthly review of accidents and incidents. Spot checks are recorded on the care worker's file. The nominated individual reviews health and safety performance every year.

## 13. Records

Risk assessments are kept on the client's record and reviewed at least every year and whenever anything changes. Accidents and incidents are kept through the site's concerns process. Training, spot checks and individual risk assessments for staff are kept on their file. All are kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
