## 1. Purpose

Every employer must check that the people it employs are allowed to work in the UK. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} carries out right to work checks, repeats them when someone's permission is time-limited, and, if we hold a sponsor licence, meets the duties that come with it. Doing this properly protects the people who work for us as well as the company.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone we employ, whatever their nationality, before they start work: care staff, office staff, managers and bank staff. Agency workers are checked by their agency, and we ask the agency to confirm it in writing (see Safe Staffing, Rotas and Agency Staff). The sponsor licence section applies only if we hold one.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Immigration, Asylum and Nationality Act 2006 — an employer who employs someone without the right to work faces a civil penalty, unless it holds a "statutory excuse": proof that it carried out the prescribed checks before employment began.
- Immigration Act 2016 — knowingly employing someone without the right to work, or having reasonable cause to believe they do not have it, is a criminal offence.
- Home Office Code of practice on preventing illegal working: civil penalty scheme for employers, and the Employer's guide to right to work checks — how checks must be done.
- Home Office Code of practice for employers on avoiding unlawful discrimination while preventing illegal working — we check everyone the same way.
- Equality Act 2010 — no one is treated less favourably because of their race, nationality or ethnic origin.
- Home Office Workers and Temporary Workers guidance for sponsors — the duties of a sponsor licence holder.
- Department of Health and Social Care Code of practice for the international recruitment of health and social care personnel in England — recruiting from overseas ethically.

## 4. Our policy

- No one starts work, including shadowing or paid training, until their right to work has been checked and the check recorded.
- We check every new employee, including British citizens. We never assume someone has the right to work from their name, accent or appearance, and we never check only some people.
- We follow the Home Office guidance in force at the time. The rules change often, so the manager doing the check uses the current guidance, not memory.
- Where someone's permission is time-limited, we repeat the check before it runs out.
- We treat people fairly while checks are done. A delay or a problem with a check is not a reason to treat someone with suspicion.

## 5. How we check

There are three ways to carry out a check. Each gives a statutory excuse only if it is done in the way the Home Office guidance describes.

### 5.1 Online check

People with an eVisa or other digital immigration status prove their right to work online. They give us a share code and their date of birth, and we check their status on the Home Office online service. We check that the photograph matches the person, in person or on a live video call, and keep the result page. Physical documents such as an expired biometric residence permit cannot be accepted in place of the online check.

### 5.2 Manual document check

For people who cannot use the online service — for example British or Irish citizens using a passport — we check original documents from the Home Office lists of acceptable documents. The check is done with the person present, in person or on a live video call with the originals in front of us. We make sure the documents are genuine and unaltered, belong to the person, and are in date where they need to be; that photographs and dates of birth match; and that any difference in name is explained by a further document, such as a marriage certificate. We take a clear copy of each document, record the date of the check, and keep it so it cannot be altered.

### 5.3 Digital identity check

For British and Irish citizens with a valid passport or Irish passport card, we may use a certified identity service provider to check their identity digitally. We remain responsible for the check, and we confirm that the person in front of us is the person the provider checked.

### 5.4 Pending applications and appeals

If someone cannot show their right to work because they have an application or appeal outstanding with the Home Office, we ask the Home Office Employer Checking Service to confirm their status, and follow its answer.

## 6. Repeat checks

When a check shows that someone's permission to work is time-limited, we record the date it ends and repeat the check before then. The site holds the documents on the person's file with their expiry date and reminds the manager as it nears. If a repeat check shows that someone no longer has the right to work, or the Home Office does not confirm it, we must stop employing them. The registered manager takes advice before acting, meets the person, explains why, and confirms it in writing.

Where someone has restrictions on their work — a limit on hours, as for some students, or a condition to work only in a named job — we record them and make sure the rota keeps to them.

## 7. Sponsored workers

This section applies if we hold a sponsor licence. Holding a licence is a privilege the Home Office can suspend or revoke, which would affect every worker we sponsor.

- We follow the Home Office sponsor guidance in force at the time, including the rules for the route a worker is on. We do not rely on what the rules used to be.
- We keep the records the guidance requires for each sponsored worker, and keep their contact details up to date.
- We report changes through the Sponsor Management System within the time the guidance sets — for example, when a worker does not start, is absent without permission, changes job or pay in a way the guidance covers, or leaves.
- We pay each sponsored worker at least the salary on their certificate of sponsorship, and give them the hours and the job it describes.
- We do not charge a sponsored worker, directly or through deductions or a repayment agreement, for the costs the guidance says the sponsor must bear, such as the sponsorship and licence fees and the Immigration Skills Charge.
- We do not use recruitment agencies that charge workers fees, and we recruit from overseas only in line with the Code of practice for international recruitment.
- The people named on our licence — the authorising officer and the users of the Sponsor Management System — are named here and kept up to date with the Home Office.

> If you hold a sponsor licence, name your authorising officer, key contact and level 1 user. If you do not, write "We do not hold a sponsor licence" and the rest of this section will not apply.

Sponsored workers have the same rights as everyone else who works for us. If a sponsored worker raises a concern about how they are treated — pay, hours, accommodation or pressure to stay — it is dealt with under our Grievance or Whistleblowing policy, and anyone who thinks a worker is being exploited follows our Modern Slavery policy.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure every check is done before employment begins and repeated in time, and acts on any failed check.
- **Authorising officer** (sponsors only) — is responsible for the licence and makes sure the sponsor duties are met.
- **Office staff who carry out checks** — are trained to do them, follow the current Home Office guidance, and record each check fully.
- **Employees** — provide their documents or share code, and tell us at once if their immigration status changes.

## 9. Training

Staff who carry out right to work checks are trained before they do so, and read the Home Office guidance again whenever it changes. Users of the Sponsor Management System are trained in the sponsor duties.

## 10. Records

Copies of documents, online check results and the date of each check are kept on the person's file on the site, securely and seen only by those who need them. They are kept for the whole of the person's employment and for two years after it ends, as the Home Office requires, and then destroyed securely. Sponsor records are kept as the sponsor guidance requires.
