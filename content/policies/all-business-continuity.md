## 1. Purpose

The people we support depend on us every day, whatever happens — snow, a heatwave, a flu outbreak, a power cut or a computer failure. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} plans for emergencies and disruptions, so that we can keep providing the care people cannot do without, and get back to normal as quickly as possible.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us. It covers any event that disrupts, or could disrupt, our ability to provide safe care, including:

- staff shortages, from sickness, an outbreak, severe weather or fuel shortages;
- severe weather — snow and ice, heatwaves, flooding and storms;
- failure of our phones, IT systems or the systems we use to plan care, including a cyber attack;
- loss of power, water, gas or heating;
- loss of our office or, in a care home, part or all of the building;
- the sudden loss of a key person, or of a key supplier.

Infectious outbreaks are covered in more detail in Outbreak Management, and fire in a care home in Fire Safety.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, and Regulation 17: Good governance — assessing and planning for risks to the service.
- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, Regulation 18 — notifying CQC of events that stop, or may stop, the service running safely.
- Civil Contingencies Act 2004 — the duties of local authorities and NHS bodies to plan for emergencies. It does not apply to us directly, but our commissioners expect us to plan with them, and to take part in local arrangements.
- UK Health Security Agency, Adverse Weather and Health Plan — heat-health and cold-health alerts, and the actions care providers should take.
- Our contracts with commissioners, which usually require a business continuity plan.

## 4. Our policy

- We keep a written business continuity plan, separate from this policy, that sets out how we will respond to each kind of disruption.
- We know which of the people we support would be at greatest risk if their care were disrupted, and we protect their care first.
- The plan can be used when our systems are down: key information is available offline as well.
- We test the plan, learn from every disruption, and keep it up to date.

## 5. The business continuity plan

{{registered_manager}} keeps the plan, and {{nominated_individual}} approves it. It contains:

- who takes charge in an emergency, and who deputises;
- contact details for staff, the on-call manager, commissioners, GPs and community nurses, utility companies, suppliers and insurers;
- the priority list of the people we support (see section 6);
- what we do for each kind of disruption, as set out in section 7;
- where copies of the plan and key information are kept, including offline;
- in a care home, the arrangements for moving residents to another place if the building cannot be used.

> Say where the plan and the offline copies of key information are kept, and who holds them.

## 6. The priority list

Every person we support is given a priority rating, reviewed with their care plan and whenever their needs change:

- **High** — cannot go without care without serious risk: for example, a person who lives alone with no one to help, needs help to eat, drink or take time-critical medicines, needs two staff to move, or is at the end of life.
- **Medium** — needs care but has some support, such as a family member who could help for a short time.
- **Low** — could manage safely for a short time, or with a shorter or later visit, or with family support.

In home care, the priority list tells the office which visits to protect first. In a care home, all residents are cared for, but the list shows who needs the most staff time and who would be most at risk if they had to be moved. The list is kept up to date on the site, with an offline copy kept securely and replaced whenever it changes.

## 7. What we do

### 7.1 Staff shortages

- The office or the shift leader asks available staff to cover, including part-time staff, office staff and managers who are trained to provide care, and uses agency staff where needed (see Safe Staffing, Rotas and Agency Staff).
- In home care, visits are rearranged by priority. With the agreement of the person and their family, some visits may be shortened, moved or covered by family for a short time. High-priority visits are never dropped.
- In a care home, non-essential tasks are put off, and staff are moved to where they are most needed.
- We tell commissioners early, and ask for help where we cannot cover care safely.

### 7.2 Severe weather

We act on heat-health and cold-health alerts from the UK Health Security Agency.

- In hot weather — make sure people drink enough, keep rooms and homes cool, check for signs of heat illness and dehydration, and check medicines that may be affected by heat or make people more at risk.
- In cold weather — make sure people are warm, eating and drinking well, and have heating; tell the office if a person's home is too cold.
- In snow, ice and flooding — plan routes, allow extra time, share cars where it is safe, walk to nearby visits, and ask for help from local volunteer drivers where that is arranged. No one is asked to make an unsafe journey.

### 7.3 Phones and IT systems

- Phones are diverted to a mobile or another line.
- Staff use the offline copy of the rota, the priority list and key contacts until the systems are back.
- Care workers and care staff record care on paper, if they cannot record it as usual, and the records are entered on the system when it returns.
- A cyber attack, or anything that may have put personal information at risk, is treated as a data breach (see Data Security and Breaches).

### 7.4 Power, water and heating

- In home care, the office helps clients contact their supplier, and checks on clients who rely on electrical equipment, such as oxygen concentrators or pressure-relieving mattresses. We encourage these clients to join their energy network's Priority Services Register.
- In a care home, the shift leader makes residents safe and warm, uses emergency lighting and torches, contacts the supplier and the on-call manager, and follows the plan for alternative heating, water and food.

### 7.5 Loss of the office or the building

- If our office cannot be used, the office team works from home or another location, using the offline information until systems are restored.
- If a care home building cannot be used, residents are moved to a safe area of the building or, if necessary, to the place agreed in the plan, with their medicines, records and the things they need. The local authority and families are told straight away.

## 8. Telling people

During any disruption, the person in charge makes sure that the people we support and their families know of any change to their care; that staff know what is happening and what is expected of them; that commissioners are told as their contracts require; and that CQC is notified if the event stops, or may stop, us running the service safely (see Reportable Incidents).

## 9. After a disruption

The registered manager reviews what happened, what worked and what did not, and updates the plan. Staff who have worked under pressure are thanked and supported.

## 10. Testing the plan

The plan is reviewed every year and after every disruption. It is tested at least every year — for example by talking through a scenario with managers and senior staff, or by running the service from the offline copies for a short time — and the result is recorded.

## 11. Roles and responsibilities

- **Nominated individual** — approves the plan and makes sure the resources it needs are available.
- **Registered manager** — keeps the plan up to date, leads the response, and reviews it afterwards.
- **Office, on-call staff and shift leaders** — follow the plan, keep the priority list current, and keep people informed.
- **All staff** — know what to do in an emergency, keep their contact details up to date, and help where they safely can.

## 12. Training

All staff are told about the business continuity plan, and their part in it, at induction. Managers, on-call staff and shift leaders are trained in using the plan, and take part in testing it.

## 13. Records

The plan, the priority list, test results and reviews of disruptions are kept by the registered manager. Incidents during a disruption are recorded through the site's concerns process. All are kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
