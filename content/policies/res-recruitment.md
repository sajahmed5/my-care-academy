## 1. Purpose

Residents depend on everyone who works in the home, from care staff to the night staff, cooks, domestics and volunteers. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} recruits safely and fairly, the checks we make before anyone starts, and how we keep them up to date.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone we employ or engage directly to work in the home, in every role and on days or nights, including bank staff and self-employed contractors, and to volunteers. Directors, the nominated individual and the registered manager are also checked under Fit and Proper Persons. Agency workers are checked by their agency (see Safe Staffing, Rotas and Agency Staff).

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 19: Fit and proper persons employed — everyone we employ is of good character, competent, and able after reasonable adjustments to do the work, and our recruitment procedures must make sure of it.
- Schedule 3 of the same Regulations — what we must hold about each person: proof of identity with a recent photograph; a criminal record check at the level the role needs, with barred list information where it applies; evidence of conduct in previous care work and of why it ended; relevant qualifications; a full employment history with a written explanation of any gaps; and information about any health condition relevant to the work. Section 6 follows it.
- Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006 — a barred person must not work in regulated activity with adults, and knowingly allowing it is an offence.
- Police Act 1997 and the Disclosure and Barring Service Code of Practice — checks only where the role is eligible, and the information used fairly, kept securely and not kept longer than needed.
- Rehabilitation of Offenders Act 1974 (Exceptions) Order 1975 — work in a care home is excepted, so applicants must declare spent convictions and cautions, except protected ones that are filtered from DBS certificates.
- Equality Act 2010 — no discrimination, and reasonable adjustments for disabled applicants. Under section 60 we do not ask about health before offering the job, except for the narrow reasons the Act allows, such as adjustments for the interview.

## 4. Our policy

- Nobody in any role starts work, including shadowing and paid training, until every check below is complete, satisfactory and recorded.
- Every post is filled the same way, and nobody is waved through because a shift is short.
- We recruit for values as well as experience: kindness, honesty and reliability.
- A criminal record does not automatically rule someone out. We consider each one fairly, against the role.

## 5. How we recruit

### 5.1 The job

Each post has a job description and a person specification setting out the duties, shifts and values we look for. Adverts say the post is subject to a DBS check.

### 5.2 Applications and shortlisting

Every applicant completes our application form, with a full employment history since leaving school, dated by month and year, and referees; a CV alone is not enough. Equality monitoring information is kept apart from shortlisting. We shortlist against the person specification and ask each shortlisted applicant whether they need adjustments for the interview.

### 5.3 Interview

Interviews are held in the home by at least two people, at least one trained in safer recruitment. Every candidate for a role is asked the same core questions, including:

- why they want to work in a care home, and scenarios that test their values;
- each gap in their employment history, until it is explained;
- why they left any previous care work;
- whether there is any reason they should not work with adults at risk.

For night posts we ask how they would deal with a fall, a distressed resident or a fire alarm with few colleagues on duty and the manager on call.

Candidates are shown round so residents can meet them, and residents or relatives who wish may join an informal part of the interview. Their views count; the decision is the manager's.

Interviewers check photo ID and record notes and scores. Candidates declare spent and unspent convictions, as the Exceptions Order allows, on a separate form seen only by the decision-maker.

### 5.4 Conditional offer

The successful candidate is offered the job in writing, conditional on every check listed in the letter being satisfactory. Only then do we ask about their health.

## 6. The checks

### 6.1 Identity and right to work

We check photo ID and proof of address, and check the right to work exactly as Right to Work and Sponsored Workers sets out. Since 1 October 2026 that includes self-employed contractors and other workers we engage. Care worker and senior care worker jobs can no longer be sponsored for people recruited from overseas; we sponsor someone for these jobs only as that policy allows.

### 6.2 DBS checks

Care staff, and anyone else whose work includes regulated activity with adults, have an enhanced DBS check with a check of the adults' barred list. Kitchen, domestic, laundry, maintenance and administrative staff who work in the home are eligible for an enhanced check; whether the adults' barred list can also be checked depends on the role — a kitchen assistant who helps residents to eat is in regulated activity. The registered manager checks each role against the DBS eligibility guidance and records the level chosen. Where someone has lived abroad as an adult, we also ask for a criminal record certificate or certificate of good conduct from that country where one can be obtained.

If a certificate shows a conviction, caution or other information, the registered manager discusses it with the applicant before deciding, weighing its relevance and seriousness, how long ago it was and what has changed since, and records the decision and reasons. Nobody on the adults' barred list is employed in regulated activity.

### 6.3 DBS Update Service

An applicant on the DBS Update Service may let us check their existing certificate online instead of applying for a new one, if it is enhanced, for the adult workforce, with the barred list check the role needs. We ask all staff to join, and recheck their status, with consent, as often as Fit and Proper Persons sets.

### 6.4 References, conduct and employment history

We ask for at least two references: from the most recent employer, and from the most recent work in health or social care or with children or vulnerable adults, if different. We ask care employers about the person's conduct and why they left, and confirm by phone any reference that is unclear or from a personal email address. Open "to whom it may concern" letters are not accepted. Every gap in the employment history has a written explanation on file; an unexplained gap stops the process.

### 6.5 Health

After the offer, the applicant completes a health questionnaire, so we can check they can do the essential parts of the job, such as moving and handling, and plan reasonable adjustments, asking occupational health with their consent where needed. Night staff are also offered the free health assessment night workers are entitled to (see Working Time and Rest Breaks).

### 6.6 Qualifications

We see original certificates for qualifications the person relies on, such as a Diploma in Adult Care or food hygiene, and check any professional register.

## 7. Volunteers

Volunteers have a written role description and are recruited in proportion to what they will do. A volunteer who helps residents to eat or move, or spends time alone with them, has the same checks as care staff; the DBS check is free for those who meet the DBS definition of a volunteer. Volunteers are never counted in a shift's staffing, and never give personal care or medicines.

## 8. Starting work

Once the registered manager has signed off the file, the new starter begins induction and probation (see Induction, Probation and the Care Certificate). Night staff shadow an experienced night colleague and are trained in the night-time fire evacuation plan before they work a night shift. Staff must tell us straight away if they are arrested, charged, cautioned or convicted, and confirm at each yearly appraisal that nothing has changed.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — is responsible for safe recruitment, decides the DBS level for each role and on any DBS information, and signs off each file against Schedule 3 before a start date.
- **Nominated individual** — checks a sample of staff files as part of our governance.
- **Interviewers** — interview fairly and consistently, and record their reasons.
- **Administrators who carry out checks** — complete and record each check, and raise anything unclear with the registered manager.

## 10. Training

At least one person on every interview panel has completed safer recruitment training and refreshes it every three years. Staff who carry out right to work and DBS checks are trained before they do so.

## 11. Records

The application, interview notes, offer, Schedule 3 evidence and sign-off are kept on the person's file on the site. For a DBS certificate we record its number, date, level, the barred list checked and our decision, and do not keep a copy longer than the DBS Code of Practice allows. Files are kept for six years after employment ends. Papers about applicants we do not employ are kept for six months after the decision and then destroyed securely.
