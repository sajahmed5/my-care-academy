## 1. Purpose

Most of our care workers work alone, in other people's homes, often early in the morning or late at night. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} keeps lone workers safe: how we assess the risks, what we expect you to do, and how we respond if something goes wrong.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works alone for us — care workers on visits, staff travelling between visits, and office staff working out of hours or on-call.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 — our duty to protect the health, safety and welfare of employees.
- Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — the duty to assess risks, including the risks of working alone.
- Health and Safety Executive guidance on protecting lone workers.

## 4. Our policy

- No visit is so important that you must put yourself in danger. If you feel unsafe, leave and tell us.
- We assess the risks of every client's home before care starts, and again when anything changes.
- Someone always knows where you are expected to be, and someone is always available to help.
- We act on every report of an incident or a near miss.

## 5. Before care starts

The environmental risk assessment done at the start of each package looks at the home and its surroundings: access and lighting, parking, pets, smoking, the condition of the property, anyone else living there or visiting, and any history of aggression. Where a risk cannot be controlled for one worker, visits are made by two workers, at a different time, or not at all until it is resolved. What you need to know is in the client's care plan before your first visit.

## 6. What to do

### 6.1 Before you set out

- Make sure your phone is charged and you have the on-call number saved.
- Check your rota and any notes about the visit.
- Tell the office if your car or travel plans change.

### 6.2 Logging in and out

Log the start and end of every visit as you have been shown. Visit logging lets us see that you have arrived and left safely; if a visit is not logged as expected, the office or on-call will contact you. If you cannot log a visit, phone the office.

### 6.3 Arriving

- Park in a well-lit place, facing the way out, where you can.
- Have your ID badge visible.
- If something does not feel right — a raised voice, someone you do not expect, a strong smell of gas — do not go in. Phone the office or on-call from a safe place.
- If there is no answer, follow our No Reply and Unable to Gain Entry policy.

### 6.4 During the visit

- Keep your phone and keys with you.
- Know your way out.
- If anyone becomes aggressive, stay calm, do not argue, and leave as soon as you can. Your safety comes before finishing the visit.
- Do not accept lifts or hospitality that would put you at risk.

### 6.5 If you need help

Phone 999 if you or anyone else is in immediate danger. Then phone on-call.

### 6.6 Late and missed log-ins

If a care worker has not logged in to a visit within [number] minutes of the planned time and cannot be reached, the office or on-call contacts the client, then the next client on the rota. If the worker still cannot be found, the on-call manager decides whether to go to the area, contact the worker's emergency contact, or call the police.

## 7. Travelling

Plan your route, keep your car in good condition, and keep valuables out of sight. Do not use your phone while driving. If you walk or use public transport, keep to well-lit routes where you can, and tell the office if a visit means walking somewhere you do not feel safe. See also our Driving at Work policy.

## 8. Reporting

Report every incident, near miss, threat or abusive behaviour, however small, to the office the same day. They are recorded through the site's concerns process as an incident, looked into, and the client's risk assessment is updated. We support staff after any frightening incident, including time off where needed.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure risk assessments are done and kept up to date, that on-call is always covered, and that incidents are acted on.
- **Office and on-call staff** — monitor visit logging, follow up late log-ins promptly, and give lone workers the help they ask for.
- **Care workers** — follow this policy, log visits, keep their phone charged, and report concerns and incidents.

## 10. Training

Lone working and personal safety are covered in induction, with a refresher every year. Staff who visit clients with a known history of aggression receive further training.

## 11. Records

Environmental risk assessments are kept on the client's record and reviewed at least every year. Incidents are kept through the concerns process. Visit logs are kept with the visit records.
