## 1. Purpose

Every client must get the care they need, at the time agreed, from someone who knows how to give it. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} makes sure we have enough suitably skilled care workers, plans rotas that are safe and realistic, keeps continuity of care, covers out of hours, and uses agency staff safely.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who plans rotas, takes on new packages of care, or works on-call, and to all care workers and agency staff who make visits for us.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 18: Staffing — enough suitably qualified, competent, skilled and experienced staff, deployed to meet people's needs.
- Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment — care is provided safely, including making sure staff have the competence and skills to give it.
- Regulation 19: Fit and proper persons employed — everyone who works for us, including through an agency, is checked and fit to do the work.
- National Institute for Health and Care Excellence, NG21: Home care: delivering personal care and practical support to older people living in their own homes — continuity of care, enough time for visits, and short visits only in limited circumstances.
- Working Time Regulations 1998 and National Minimum Wage Regulations 2015 — rest, breaks and paid travel time.

## 4. Our policy

- We take on a new package of care only when we can staff it safely, every visit, with suitably trained care workers.
- Visits are planned to be long enough to give the care in the care plan without rushing. Travel time between visits is planned separately and is never taken from visit time.
- We aim for each client to be visited by a small team of care workers they know.
- Someone with access to rotas and care records is always on-call outside office hours.
- Agency staff are used only when we cannot cover a visit with our own staff, and only after we have checked them.

## 5. Taking on new packages

Before agreeing to a new package, the registered manager or the person they delegate to checks:

- that we have care workers with the right skills, training and availability for every visit, including weekends, evenings and two-person calls;
- that visit times can be met, with realistic travel from the visits before and after;
- that the client's needs, and any risks, can be met with our current team.

If we cannot staff it safely, we say no, or agree a later start date. We never accept a package on the understanding that cover will be found later.

## 6. Planning rotas

### 6.1 Visit times and length

Visits are planned at the times agreed with the client and in their care plan. Visits that are time-critical — for example for time-specific medicines, insulin, or meals for someone with diabetes — are marked as such and given priority. Visit length is set by what the care plan needs; short visits are used only for simple tasks, such as a medicine prompt or a safety check, with someone who knows the client.

### 6.2 Travel time

Rotas include realistic travel time between visits, based on distance, traffic and how the care worker travels. If travel time is regularly too short, care workers tell the office and the rota is changed.

### 6.3 Continuity

Each client is given a regular team of care workers where possible, and is told who is coming, particularly if it is someone new. Changes are kept to a minimum, and a new care worker is introduced by someone the client knows wherever possible.

### 6.4 Skills and needs

The rota planner matches care workers to clients: two-person calls have two trained workers; visits needing a hoist, medicines or a delegated healthcare task go only to workers who are trained and assessed as competent in that task; preferences about the gender of the care worker and other wishes in the care plan are followed.

### 6.5 Rest and fairness

Rotas give care workers their breaks and rest, do not plan excessive hours, and are shared fairly. Rotas are published in advance, and changes are made with as much notice as possible.

## 7. When things change

### 7.1 Sickness and gaps

When a care worker cannot work, the office or on-call finds cover from within the team first. If a visit cannot be covered, the manager decides which visits to prioritise, based on risk — those who live alone, need medicines or have no one else are first — and tells the clients affected and their families. Our Missed and Late Visits policy is followed.

### 7.2 Monitoring visits

The office monitors visits as they happen. If a visit has not started within the expected time, the office or on-call contacts the care worker and, if needed, the client. Late and missed visits are recorded and looked into.

### 7.3 On-call

On-call is covered at all times outside office hours by someone who can see rotas, contact details and care plans, make decisions about cover, and reach a manager. On-call staff know the escalation routes for emergencies and safeguarding.

## 8. Agency staff

We use an agency only when we cannot cover visits ourselves. Before an agency worker works for us:

- the agency confirms in writing that it has completed the checks we require — identity, right to work, an enhanced DBS check with the adult barred list, references and health — and that the worker is trained in the subjects our care workers must complete;
- we check the worker's identity when they arrive;
- they are introduced to the client where possible, given access to the care plan and risk assessments, and told how to log visits and reach on-call;
- they are not given visits that need a skill they have not been shown to be competent in.

We keep a record of every agency worker who works for us, with the agency's confirmation of their checks, and raise any concern about them with the agency.

## 9. Reviewing staffing

The registered manager reviews staffing at least every month: hours delivered against commissioned, late and missed visits, continuity of care, use of agency staff, overtime, sickness, and staff turnover. Where staffing is not safe, the manager acts — recruiting, pausing new packages, or telling commissioners and, where needed, CQC.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — decides whether new packages can be taken on, makes sure staffing is safe, and reviews it.
- **Rota planners** — plan rotas that are safe, realistic and continuous, and keep them up to date.
- **On-call staff** — cover gaps, monitor visits, and escalate when needed.
- **Care workers** — keep to their rota, tell us early if they cannot work, and report when travel or visit times are not enough.

## 11. Training

Rota planners and on-call staff are trained in this policy, in prioritising visits by risk, and in our Missed and Late Visits and Safeguarding policies.

## 12. Records

Rotas, visit times, missed and late visits, and agency workers' checks are kept for the period set in Record Keeping and Retention. Staffing reviews are recorded and kept for inspection.
