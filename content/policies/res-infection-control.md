## 1. Purpose

Residents live close together, share rooms and staff, and are often frail, so an infection can spread quickly through a care home and be serious for those who catch it. This policy sets out the everyday precautions {{organisation}} takes to keep the home clean and stop infection spreading.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works in the home, on every shift, and to agency staff, contractors and visitors. What we do in addition when an outbreak is suspected is in our Outbreak Management policy.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008: Code of Practice on the prevention and control of infections and related guidance — what registered providers must do to prevent and control infection, which CQC takes into account when it inspects.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, which includes assessing the risk of, and preventing, detecting and controlling the spread of, infection; and Regulation 15: Premises and equipment, which must be clean.
- UK Health Security Agency, Infection prevention and control: resource for adult social care — the standard precautions, isolation, and when unwell staff should stay away.
- Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regulations 2002 (COSHH) and the Personal Protective Equipment at Work Regulations 1992 — protecting staff from germs and harmful products, with protective equipment provided free.
- Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR) — some infections caught at work, and some sharps injuries, must be reported to the Health and Safety Executive.
- Environmental Protection Act 1990, section 34, the waste duty of care, and the Hazardous Waste (England and Wales) Regulations 2005 — waste is separated, stored securely and collected only by an authorised carrier, following the good practice in Health Technical Memoranda HTM 07-01 (waste) and HTM 01-04 (linen).

## 4. Our policy

- Every member of staff uses the standard precautions — hand hygiene, protective equipment, safe handling of sharps, spills, laundry and waste — with every resident, whether or not they are known to have an infection.
- {{registered_manager}} is the home's infection prevention and control lead, or names a trained senior to be the lead.
- Staff who are unwell with an infection stay away from work, and are not penalised for doing so.
- Residents with an infection are never isolated more, or for longer, than is needed.

## 5. Standard precautions

### 5.1 Hand hygiene

Clean your hands before and after contact with each resident, after taking off gloves, before handling food or medicines, and after handling waste, laundry or bodily fluids. Use soap and water when your hands are visibly dirty, after contact with diarrhoea or vomit, and when a resident has Clostridioides difficile (C. diff), which hand rub does not kill. Otherwise use alcohol hand rub. Be bare below the elbows during care, keep nails short, and cover cuts with a waterproof dressing (see Code of Conduct, Professional Boundaries and Dress). Help residents to clean their hands before meals and after using the toilet.

### 5.2 Protective equipment

- Wear disposable gloves and an apron for personal care, continence care, and any contact with blood, bodily fluids, broken skin or soiled laundry.
- Wear a fluid-resistant mask and eye protection when there is a risk of splashing, or when the care plan or current guidance says so.
- Change gloves and apron between residents and between tasks. Take off gloves first, then the apron, then the mask, and clean your hands.
- Never wear protective equipment in corridors, communal areas or the kitchen.

### 5.3 Sharps

Never re-sheathe a needle; put it straight into a sharps bin. Bins are labelled and dated, kept out of residents' reach, and locked when filled to the line. If you are injured by a used needle or splashed with blood in the eyes or mouth, wash the area with running water and soap, encourage a puncture to bleed, and get medical advice straight away from the nearest emergency department, as treatment may need to start quickly. Then tell the shift leader and record it through the site's concerns process.

### 5.4 Spills of blood and bodily fluids

Use a spill kit, following its instructions and the COSHH assessment: gloves and apron on, soak up the spill, clean with detergent and water, then disinfect with the product provided.

### 5.5 Waste

Waste goes in the right bag or bin where it is produced, as the home's waste procedure and our contractor's arrangements say: used pads and protective equipment from residents without a known infection as offensive waste, waste from a resident with a known infection as infectious waste in orange bags, and sharps in sharps bins. Full bags are tied and kept in the locked waste area until collected. The registered manager keeps the waste transfer and consignment notes the law requires.

## 6. A clean environment

### 6.1 Cleaning schedules

Rooms, bathrooms, toilets and communal areas are cleaned every day to a written schedule saying what is cleaned, how and how often, signed when done. Handrails, door handles, call bells and other frequently touched surfaces are cleaned more than once a day. Each room is deep cleaned on a rota and before a new resident moves in. Cloths and mops follow the national colour code: red for bathrooms and toilets, blue for general areas, green for kitchens, yellow for isolation rooms.

### 6.2 Equipment and the environment

- Shared equipment — hoists, stand aids, commodes, wheelchairs, bath seats — is cleaned after every use. Slings are for one resident only.
- Commodes, bedpans and urinals are emptied and cleaned in the sluice, never in a resident's bathroom.
- Mattresses and cushions are checked inside the cover every month, and replaced if contaminated.

## 7. The laundry

- Residents' clothing is labelled and washed separately for each resident.
- Soiled or infected linen is handled with gloves and an apron, never shaken, and put straight into a water-soluble bag inside the coloured outer bag the laundry procedure sets out.
- It is washed on the disinfection cycle set for the machine, or the hottest the fabric allows.
- Dirty and clean items are kept apart, and clean linen is stored covered and off the floor.

## 8. The kitchen

The kitchen is run to food safety law, as our Nutrition and Hydration policy sets out. Care staff do not enter the kitchen in protective equipment or carrying anything soiled, and wash their hands and change their apron before serving food. Dining tables are cleaned before and after meals. Kitchen staff, and anyone who handles food, do not work with food until 48 hours after their last diarrhoea or vomiting.

## 9. A resident with an infection

When a resident may have an infection, the shift leader asks the GP for advice, and the care plan sets out the precautions. Where needed, the resident is cared for in their own room with their own equipment, with the door closed if they agree and it is safe, for as long as the GP or health protection team advises.

A resident who lacks capacity, or does not understand why they should stay in their room, is never locked in. Staff use the least restrictive approach that works, and any restriction is recorded in their care plan (see Restraint and Restrictive Practice).

## 10. If you suspect an outbreak

If two or more residents or staff have similar symptoms within a few days, tell the shift leader on the same shift. The registered manager contacts the UK Health Security Agency health protection team, and our Outbreak Management policy is followed from then on.

## 11. Staff health and vaccination

Do not come to work with diarrhoea or vomiting until 48 hours after your last symptoms. With symptoms of a respiratory infection and a high temperature, or if you do not feel well enough to work, stay at home and follow current UK Health Security Agency guidance on when to return. Report absence as our Sickness Absence policy sets out; absence we asked you to take is not held against you.

We offer and encourage the vaccinations staff are eligible for under the national programmes, such as flu, and where the COSHH assessment shows a risk of contact with blood, we offer hepatitis B vaccination free. Vaccination is a personal choice, and no one is treated less favourably for declining it. We support residents to have the vaccines offered to them.

## 12. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — leads infection prevention and control, or names the lead; makes sure supplies, schedules and training are in place; and acts on audits.
- **Shift leaders** — make sure precautions are followed on their shift, including at night, and report signs of infection.
- **All staff** — follow the standard precautions, schedules and procedures for their work, and report signs of infection.

## 13. Training

All staff complete infection prevention and control training at induction and refresh it every year, and housekeeping, laundry and kitchen staff are trained in their own procedures. Hand hygiene and protective equipment use are observed and recorded on each person's file. Training is recorded on the training matrix.

## 14. Records and audit

The lead audits infection prevention and control across the home every three months, and observes hand hygiene every month. Cleaning schedules, audits, mattress checks and waste notes are kept by the registered manager; sharps injuries and work-related infections are recorded through the concerns process. All are kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
