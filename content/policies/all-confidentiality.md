## 1. Purpose

The people we support tell us, and let us see, things they would not share with most people. They must be able to trust us to keep them private. They also need us to share the right information with the right people, at the right time, to keep them safe and well. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} keeps information confidential and when, and how, it may be shared.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us, during and after their employment, and to all information about the people we support, their families, our staff and the company, however it is held or passed on — on the site, on paper, by phone, by message or in conversation.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Common law duty of confidentiality — information given in confidence must not be shared without the person's consent, unless the law requires or allows it, or there is an overriding public interest.
- UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 — the rules for using and sharing personal information, especially health information.
- Human Rights Act 1998, Article 8 — the right to respect for private and family life.
- Health and Social Care Act 2012, section 251B — the duty to share information with other health and adult social care providers where it is needed for a person's direct care and they do not object.
- Care Act 2014 — the expectation that information is shared to protect adults at risk of abuse or neglect.
- The Caldicott Principles, set by the National Data Guardian for health and adult social care.

## 4. The Caldicott Principles

We follow the eight Caldicott Principles:

- Justify the purpose for using confidential information.
- Use confidential information only when it is necessary.
- Use the minimum confidential information necessary.
- Access to confidential information is on a strict need-to-know basis.
- Everyone with access to confidential information understands their responsibilities.
- Comply with the law.
- The duty to share information for individual care is as important as the duty to protect confidentiality.
- Inform the people we support about how their confidential information is used.

## 5. What to do

### 5.1 Every day

- Look only at information you need for your work. Do not look up someone because you are curious, even if you know them.
- Do not talk about the people we support where you can be overheard — on the bus, in a shop, or with friends and family.
- Do not tell one person we support about another.
- In homecare, do not tell a client where else you are going or who else you visit. In a care home, do not discuss one resident with another resident or their visitors.
- Keep paper records and screens out of sight of others, and log out when you have finished.
- When you leave our employment, you must still keep confidential everything you learned.

### 5.2 Talking to families and friends

Families are often closely involved, but they do not have an automatic right to information. Share information with a family member or friend only when the person has agreed to it, or the care plan says so, or someone has legal authority — such as a lasting power of attorney for health and welfare, or a deputy appointed by the Court of Protection — for the decision in question. Where a person lacks capacity to decide, share what is in their best interests. If you are not sure, say you will ask the office or the person in charge, and do so.

### 5.3 Phone calls and messages

Before giving information over the phone, make sure you know who you are talking to. If in doubt, take their number and call back through a number you know. Never leave confidential details in a voicemail.

### 5.4 Sharing to give care

Information may be shared with others involved in a person's care — their GP, district nurse, pharmacist, social worker, or a hospital — when it is needed for their care. The person is told this when their care starts. If a person objects to their information being shared for their care, respect it, tell the registered manager, and record it, unless the law or a risk of serious harm means it must be shared.

### 5.5 Sharing to protect

Confidentiality never stops you reporting abuse or neglect. If you think someone is at risk of harm, report it under our Safeguarding or Safeguarding Children policy straight away. Where possible, the person is told and asked for consent before information is shared with the local authority, but information can be shared without consent when:

- the person lacks capacity to consent and sharing is in their best interests;
- others are at risk, including children;
- a crime has been or may be committed;
- the person is being coerced or controlled;
- there is a serious risk to their life or health, or to someone else's;
- the law requires it, for example a court order.

The registered manager, or the senior on duty, decides whether to share without consent, and records the decision and the reasons.

### 5.6 Requests from others

Requests from the police, solicitors, insurers, the media or anyone else are passed to the registered manager. Do not confirm or deny that someone is supported by us. Never speak to the media about the people we support.

## 6. After death

The duty of confidentiality continues after a person dies. Requests for the records of a person who has died are passed to the registered manager.

## 7. Staff information

Information about staff — their health, address, pay, disciplinary or grievance matters — is confidential too, and is seen only by those who need it.

## 8. Breaches

Sharing confidential information without a lawful reason, or looking at it without a reason, is a serious matter, dealt with under our Disciplinary policy. If you think confidential information has been shared wrongly, tell the registered manager straight away (see Data Security and Breaches).

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — is the lead for confidentiality, decides on sharing without consent, and handles requests for information.
- **All staff** — keep information confidential, share it only as this policy allows, and report concerns.

> If you have appointed a Caldicott Guardian or a named lead for confidentiality who is not the registered manager, name them here. Remove this if not.

## 10. Training

Confidentiality is covered at induction and in the data protection training all staff complete, refreshed every year.

## 11. Records

Consent and objections to sharing are recorded in the person's care record. Each decision to share without consent is recorded with the reasons, what was shared, with whom and when.
