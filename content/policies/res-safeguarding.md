## 1. Purpose

Every resident has the right to live in safety, free from abuse and neglect. Many residents cannot easily tell anyone when something is wrong, so care staff must notice, and act. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} prevents abuse and neglect in the home, how staff recognise and report it, and how we work with the local authority so that residents are protected in the way they want.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works in the home, on every shift, including agency staff, volunteers and contractors. It covers every resident aged 18 or over, and abuse or neglect by anyone: staff, other residents, visitors, family, professionals and strangers. A concern about a child, including a child who visits the home, follows our Safeguarding Children policy.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Care Act 2014, sections 42 to 46 — the local authority must make enquiries, or ask others to, when it suspects that an adult with care and support needs is experiencing, or at risk of, abuse or neglect and cannot protect themselves because of those needs (section 42). Each area's Safeguarding Adults Board sets local procedures, holds safeguarding adults reviews and can require information from us (sections 43 to 45). Section 46 abolished the old power to remove a person from their home.
- Care and support statutory guidance, chapter 14 — the types of abuse and neglect, the six safeguarding principles, and Making Safeguarding Personal.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 13: Safeguarding service users from abuse and improper treatment — systems to prevent abuse and investigate any allegation immediately; no care that is degrading or deprives a resident of their liberty without lawful authority.
- Care Quality Commission (Registration) Regulations 2009, Regulation 18 — we notify CQC of abuse, or an allegation of abuse, involving a resident.
- Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006 — the duty to refer to the Disclosure and Barring Service.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005, section 44, and Criminal Justice and Courts Act 2015, sections 20 and 21 — ill-treatment or wilful neglect by a care worker or a care provider is a crime.

## 4. Our policy

- Safeguarding is everyone's responsibility. Every concern is reported, however small it seems.
- Staff report; they do not investigate. Proving abuse is not your job.
- We follow the six principles: empowerment, prevention, proportionality, protection, partnership and accountability.
- We make safeguarding personal: we ask the resident what has happened and what they want to happen, and their wishes shape what we do unless others are at risk or the law requires otherwise.
- Every concern that a resident is experiencing, or is at risk of, abuse or neglect is referred to the local authority on the same day, unless local procedures say otherwise (see section 7).
- We prevent abuse through safer recruitment with enhanced DBS checks, enough staff on every shift, supervision every three months, and a culture in which staff challenge poor care.

## 5. Recognising abuse and neglect

The statutory guidance lists ten types:

- Physical abuse — hitting, pushing, rough handling, misuse of medicines, restraint.
- Domestic abuse — by a partner, ex-partner or relative, including coercive control, which can carry on after a resident moves in.
- Sexual abuse — any sexual act or contact the resident has not or could not consent to.
- Psychological abuse — threats, humiliation, mocking, or stopping a resident seeing people.
- Financial or material abuse — theft of money or belongings, scams, misuse of a power of attorney or benefits.
- Modern slavery — forced labour or trafficking (see Modern Slavery).
- Discriminatory abuse — harassment or unequal treatment because of a protected characteristic such as age, disability, race or religion.
- Organisational abuse — poor care caused by how the home is run: rigid routines, residents got up or put to bed to suit the rota, call bells left unanswered.
- Neglect and acts of omission — residents left wet, hungry, thirsty, without medicines, or not helped to see a doctor.
- Self-neglect — a resident refusing all help with their health or hygiene to the point of danger.

## 6. What to do

### 6.1 If someone is in immediate danger

Make the resident safe, separating them from the person causing harm if you safely can. Call 999 if anyone is hurt or in danger. Then tell the shift leader straight away. If you suspect a physical or sexual assault, give the care the resident needs, but where you can, leave the room, bedding and clothing as they are and do not wash the resident until the police advise.

### 6.2 If a resident tells you something

- Stay calm and listen. Do not ask leading questions or press for detail.
- Tell them they were right to tell you, and that you must pass it on. Never promise to keep it secret.
- Ask what they would like to happen, and pass that on too.
- Do not confront the person who may be responsible.

### 6.3 Report and record

Tell the shift leader at once, or the registered manager if it is about the shift leader. At night, the shift leader phones the on-call manager. Record it through the site's concerns process as a safeguarding concern before you go off shift: what you saw and heard, in the resident's own words where you can, the date and time, and who was there. Use a body map for any injury. Write facts, not opinions.

If the concern is about the registered manager, tell {{nominated_individual}}. You can always report directly to the local authority or the police yourself.

### 6.4 What the registered manager does

On the same day, {{registered_manager}}, or the on-call manager:

- makes sure the resident and other residents are safe;
- asks for the resident's consent to a referral, unless asking would increase the risk;
- refers to the local authority's adult safeguarding team by phone, followed in writing;
- reports to the police where a crime may have been committed;
- notifies CQC (see Reportable Incidents), and tells the commissioner and family where appropriate;
- records each decision and the reasons through the concerns process.

A resident with capacity can decline a referral, and we respect that wherever we can. We refer without consent in the cases our Confidentiality policy sets out — for example where other residents are at risk, a crime has been committed, or one of our staff is involved — and tell the resident why, unless that would increase the risk.

> Add the adult safeguarding number and the out-of-hours (emergency duty team) number for each local authority that places residents with you.

### 6.5 After a referral

The local authority decides whether to make enquiries under section 42, and may ask us to share records, attend meetings or look into part of the concern. We co-operate and follow the safeguarding plan. If the response leaves the resident unprotected, the registered manager escalates it under the Board's procedures.

## 7. In the home

- Harm between residents — one resident hitting, frightening, touching or taking from another is a safeguarding concern, even when it comes from illness such as dementia. Separate them safely, check both, and report it. The registered manager reviews both residents' care plans and the seating and staffing arrangements. Some local authorities ask for low-level incidents between residents to be logged and sent in regularly rather than referred one by one: we follow local procedures, and if in doubt, we refer.
- Visitors — visits matter to residents and are protected (see Visiting). If a visitor harms or exploits a resident, the registered manager decides what will protect them. Any restriction on a visitor is the least restrictive that will work, takes account of the resident's wishes, and is recorded and reviewed.
- Nights — fewer staff are on duty and residents are more vulnerable. Night staff report concerns to the shift leader or the on-call manager at once, not in the morning. Managers visit the home at night, unannounced, from time to time.
- The building — keypads, sensor mats and CCTV can protect residents or restrict them, and are used only as the care plan and our CCTV and Safety and Security of Premises policies allow. A resident must not be deprived of their liberty without a Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards authorisation (see Deprivation of Liberty).
- Closed cultures — abuse grows where staff stop noticing poor practice, residents are not listened to, or staff are afraid to speak up. Managers spend time on every shift, listen to residents and families, and act on what staff tell them.

## 8. Concerns about our own staff

An allegation that someone who works here has harmed a resident, or may be a risk to residents, goes to the registered manager straight away, or to {{nominated_individual}} if it is about the registered manager. It is referred to the local authority the same day, under its procedures for concerns about people in positions of trust, and to the police where a crime may have been committed. The worker may be moved to other duties, or suspended on full pay, as a precaution (see Disciplinary). Nothing we do may hinder a police or local authority enquiry. We refer to the Disclosure and Barring Service anyone we dismiss, or who leaves, because they harmed or posed a risk of harm to a child or adult, as the law requires. Agency workers are reported to their agency as well.

If you see a colleague mistreat a resident, report it. Our Whistleblowing policy protects you.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — leads on safeguarding, makes referrals and notifications, manages allegations against staff, and keeps local contact numbers up to date.
- **Nominated individual** — receives concerns about the registered manager, and checks that safeguarding is working.
- **Shift leaders** — keep residents safe on their shift, contact the on-call manager, and refer directly if no manager can be reached.
- **Care staff** — recognise, report and record concerns on the same shift, and never investigate or confront.

## 10. Training

All staff complete safeguarding adults training during induction, before they give care, and refresh it every year. Shift leaders are also trained in the first steps on their shift. The registered manager and anyone who deputises for them complete higher-level training in managing concerns and allegations against staff. Training is recorded on the training matrix.

## 11. Records

Concerns, referrals, decisions and their reasons, and outcomes are kept through the concerns process, linked to the resident's record, shared only with those who need to know, and kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention. The registered manager reviews them with all incidents every month to spot patterns, such as concerns on one shift or between the same residents.
