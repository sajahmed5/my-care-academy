## 1. Purpose

The home is a resident's home, and asking someone to leave it is one of the most serious decisions we can make. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} supports residents who choose to move on, the limited circumstances in which we may give a resident notice, and how we make sure every move is planned, fair and safe.

## 2. Scope

It applies to every resident, however their place is funded, including residents on a trial stay or a short-term or respite stay, and to the registered manager and staff who manage a move.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Care Act 2014 — the local authority's duty to meet the eligible needs of people in its area, which continues if a resident must leave, and its temporary duty when a provider fails.
- Consumer Rights Act 2015 — the terms of a resident's contract must be fair and transparent, including the terms about notice, ending the contract and fees after death.
- The Competition and Markets Authority's consumer law advice for care home providers — including that a home should only ask a resident to leave for a good reason, after consulting them, and with reasonable notice.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — decisions about where a resident lives, when they lack capacity, are made in their best interests, involving their attorney or deputy and, where needed, an independent advocate.
- Human Rights Act 1998 — the right to respect for home, private and family life.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 9: Person-centred care, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment (including working with other providers on a transfer), and Regulation 17: Good governance.

## 4. Our policy

- A resident can choose to leave the home, on the notice their contract sets out.
- We give a resident notice only as a last resort, for a reason the contract allows, after reviewing their care with them, their representatives and their funder, and trying every reasonable way to resolve the problem.
- We never ask a resident to leave because they or their family have complained.
- No resident is moved until a safe place to go has been arranged, except in a genuine emergency.
- A move is planned with the resident, and the information they need goes with them.

## 5. When a resident chooses to move on

A resident may want to move nearer family, to a home that better suits them, or back to their own home. The registered manager:

- talks with the resident about their reasons, and records any concern or complaint through the site's concerns process;
- confirms the notice period under the contract, and does not insist on notice where that would be unfair, such as an urgent move to nursing care;
- where the resident lacks capacity to decide, makes sure the decision is made in their best interests, involving their attorney or deputy, family, the funding authority and, where there is nobody else to consult, an independent mental capacity advocate;
- tells the local authority or NHS body if they fund the place, and helps the resident with the move as below.

## 6. When we may give notice

We may give notice only when, after a review, one of these applies:

- the resident's needs have changed so that the home can no longer meet them safely, for example they now need nursing care that we are not registered to provide;
- the resident's behaviour puts other residents or staff at serious risk, and it cannot be managed safely here despite specialist advice and support;
- fees have not been paid, after reminders, a chance to agree a plan, and help to seek funding or benefits;
- the home is closing, or can no longer provide the service.

Before deciding, the registered manager holds a review with the resident, their representatives, the GP and any specialists involved, and the funding authority, and records what has been tried. The decision is made by the registered manager and agreed by the nominated individual. If the resident has a Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards authorisation, the supervisory body is told.

### 6.1 Giving notice

Notice is given in writing to the resident and their representatives, and to the funding authority, setting out the reasons, what has been tried, the date, and how they can raise a complaint. The notice period is that set in the contract. It is extended where the resident needs more time to find somewhere suitable, and we do not move anyone before a safe placement is found.

### 6.2 An emergency

If a resident needs to leave urgently — for example, they need hospital or nursing care that cannot wait, or a serious incident means others are at immediate risk — the registered manager arranges the move with the GP, ambulance service, hospital or local authority as needed, tells the resident's representatives at once, and follows up in writing. An emergency hospital admission does not end the contract; the room is kept on the terms of the contract. See our Hospital Admissions and Discharges policy.

## 7. A safe transfer

For every planned move, the registered manager or a senior:

- agrees the date and time with the resident, their family, and the new home or service, and arranges transport;
- with the resident's agreement, shares a transfer summary: the care plan and risk assessments, how they communicate, their routine and preferences, the hospital passport, any DNACPR decision, ReSPECT form or advance decision, and anything that will help them settle;
- sends the resident's medicines with a copy of the current medicine administration record, signed for by the person receiving them, so that there is no gap in treatment;
- checks the resident's belongings against their property inventory, packs them with care, and has them signed for;
- returns any money held for the resident, against their money record, to them or their representative, and settles any deposit or refund as the contract sets out;
- tells the GP practice, the funding authority, and the pharmacy of the move.

Where possible, a member of staff the resident knows goes with them, or a member of the new home's staff visits beforehand. We ask the new provider whether the resident has settled, and pass on anything they need to know.

## 8. When a resident dies

The contract ends on a resident's death in the way the contract sets out, including any fees charged after death, which must be fair and clearly explained when the resident moves in. Belongings and money are released only to the executor or the person dealing with the estate. The family are given reasonable time to collect belongings, and are not hurried. See our End of Life Care, Death and Advance Decisions policy and Residents' Money and Property policy.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — reviews care before any notice is given, gives notice, works with funders and the new provider, and makes sure every move is safe.
- **Nominated individual** — agrees any decision to give a resident notice, and makes sure contract terms are fair.
- **Shift leaders and seniors** — prepare the transfer summary, medicines, and belongings, and hand over on the day.
- **Care staff** — support the resident through the move, check belongings, and pass on what will help them settle.

## 10. Training

The registered manager and seniors understand the resident contract, this policy and the Competition and Markets Authority's advice, and refresh their knowledge whenever the contract changes. Care staff learn about safe transfers during induction.

## 11. Records

Reviews, notice letters, the transfer summary, the medicines handover, and the return of property and money are kept on the resident's record for as long as our record retention schedule requires (see Record Keeping and Retention).
