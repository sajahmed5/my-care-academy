## 1. Purpose

Prevent is the part of the government's counter-terrorism strategy that aims to stop people being drawn into terrorism. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} recognises when someone may be vulnerable to radicalisation, and what our staff should do about it. We treat it as part of safeguarding: radicalisation is a form of exploitation, and the people at risk need protection and support, not suspicion.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who works for us. It covers concerns about the people we support, members of their households, visitors, and our own staff and volunteers.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Counter-Terrorism and Security Act 2015, section 26 — the Prevent duty on specified authorities, such as local authorities and NHS bodies, to have due regard to the need to prevent people from being drawn into terrorism. Independent care providers are not named in the Act, so the duty does not apply to us directly. We follow it because the local authorities and NHS bodies we work with expect it of the services they commission, and because it protects people.
- Counter-Terrorism and Security Act 2015, Part 5 — Channel, the multi-agency programme that supports people at risk of radicalisation.
- Prevent duty guidance for England and Wales — how the duty works in practice, including in health and social care.
- Care Act 2014 — the local authority's adult safeguarding duties, which cover exploitation of adults at risk.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 13: Safeguarding service users from abuse and improper treatment.
- Equality Act 2010 and Human Rights Act 1998 — no one is treated differently because of their religion, belief, race or political views, and people keep their rights to freedom of thought, belief and expression.

## 4. Our policy

- Holding views that others disagree with, or practising a religion, is not a concern. A concern arises when there are signs that someone is being groomed, pressured or encouraged towards supporting or committing violence, or towards terrorism.
- We act on concerns about all kinds of extremism, whatever the ideology.
- Staff do not investigate, challenge or debate. They notice, check and share — with the registered manager, who decides what happens next.
- A Prevent referral is about getting support for someone who may be at risk. It is not a criminal record and does not mean that someone has done anything wrong.
- We never use Prevent to target anyone because of their religion, ethnicity or background.

## 5. Who may be at risk

Anyone can be drawn into extremism, but some of the people we support may be particularly vulnerable to grooming: people who are lonely or isolated, people with a learning disability or autism, people with mental health needs, people who spend long hours online, and people going through a crisis such as a bereavement. Younger adults living in a household we visit may also be at risk. Grooming often happens online, and often through someone who presents themselves as a friend.

## 6. Signs to notice

No single sign means that someone is being radicalised, and many have innocent explanations. Look for a change in the person, and for several signs together:

- expressing support for violence, or for a terrorist group, or saying violence against a particular group is justified;
- a sudden change in behaviour, appearance, friendships or routines, or becoming withdrawn and secretive;
- talking about "us and them", or describing a group of people as less than human;
- accessing, sharing or storing extremist material, online or on paper, or new contacts who seem to be influencing them;
- talking about harming others, or showing interest in weapons or explosives;
- being controlled or pressured by someone else, or being asked to keep secrets.

## 7. What to do

### 7.1 If someone is in immediate danger

If you believe someone is about to cause harm, or an attack may be planned, call 999 at once. Then tell the office or on-call (in home care) or the shift leader (in a care home).

### 7.2 Notice, check, share

- Notice — write down what you saw or heard, in the person's own words where you can, with the date and time. Stick to facts.
- Check — tell {{registered_manager}} on the same day, on {{office_phone}} or through the out-of-hours on-call line. Do not discuss your concern with the person, their family or colleagues, and do not search their belongings or devices.
- Share — record it through the site's concerns process as a safeguarding concern. The registered manager takes it from there.

If the concern is about the registered manager, report it to {{nominated_individual}}, or directly to the local authority's Prevent contact.

### 7.3 What the registered manager does

The registered manager:

- looks at what has been reported, and finds out anything else they reasonably can without alerting anyone;
- asks for advice from the local authority's Prevent lead or the police Prevent team where they are unsure;
- makes a Prevent referral, through the local authority's referral route, where there is a genuine concern that someone may be being drawn into terrorism;
- considers whether a safeguarding referral is also needed, for example where the person is being exploited, or where a child is involved (see Safeguarding Children);
- records the decision and the reasons, whether or not a referral is made.

> Add your local authority's Prevent referral route and contact, and the police Prevent team's number for the areas you work in.

### 7.4 After a referral

The police and local authority assess the referral. Where they agree there is a risk, the person may be offered support through Channel. Taking part in Channel is voluntary. We share information with the Channel panel when asked and when the law allows (see Confidentiality), and we co-operate with any support plan. We do not tell the person about a referral if the police or the local authority ask us not to.

## 8. Concerns about staff

If a member of staff expresses support for terrorism, shares extremist material, or behaves in a way that suggests they may be drawn into extremism, report it in the same way. The registered manager follows the steps above and also considers our Disciplinary policy, and whether the person can safely continue to work with the people we support.

## 9. Information sharing

Data protection law does not stop us sharing information to protect someone or to prevent crime. Information is shared on a need-to-know basis, and the registered manager records what was shared, with whom and why.

## 10. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — is our Prevent lead, decides on and makes referrals, keeps local contacts up to date, and makes sure staff are trained.
- **Nominated individual** — receives concerns about the registered manager and checks that this policy is followed.
- **Office, on-call staff and shift leaders** — pass concerns to the registered manager without delay.
- **All staff** — notice, check and share; do not investigate or confront.

## 11. Training

All staff complete Prevent awareness training during induction, and refresh it as part of their safeguarding training. The registered manager and anyone who deputises for them complete the government's Prevent referral training.

## 12. Records

Concerns, decisions and referrals are recorded through the concerns process, kept securely and seen only by those who need to know. They are kept for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
