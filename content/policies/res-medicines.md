## 1. Purpose

Most residents take several medicines every day, and the home orders, stores, gives and disposes of them. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} does each safely, so that every resident gets the right medicine at the right time, and residents who can manage their own medicines do so.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who handles medicines in the home, and to every resident. The home does not provide nursing, so medicines are given by care staff who have been trained and assessed as competent.

Healthcare tasks beyond routine medicines, such as injections, insulin, medicines through a PEG tube and rescue medicines for epilepsy, are covered by our Delegated Healthcare Tasks policy. Giving medicines disguised in food or drink is covered by our Covert Medication policy.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, including the proper and safe management of medicines; Regulation 11: Need for consent; Regulation 17: Good governance, including accurate records.
- NICE guideline SC1, Managing medicines in care homes — ordering, receiving, storing, giving and disposing of medicines, records, self-administration, homely remedies, controlled drugs, and training and competency.
- Human Medicines Regulations 2012 — a prescribed medicine is given only to the resident it was prescribed for, as the prescriber directs.
- Misuse of Drugs Act 1971, the Misuse of Drugs Regulations 2001 and the Misuse of Drugs (Safe Custody) Regulations 1973 — controlled drugs, such as oxycodone, fentanyl and some forms of morphine, are tightly controlled, and those the safe custody rules cover are kept in a cupboard that meets them.
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 — consent, and best interests decisions for a resident who lacks capacity.

## 4. Our policy

- Medicines are given only by staff trained and assessed as competent, with competency recorded on their file.
- Every dose given, refused or missed is recorded at the time on the resident's medicines administration record (MAR).
- Medicines are stored securely and at the right temperature, and only staff giving medicines hold the keys.
- Errors and near misses are reported straight away, without fear of blame, so that we can learn.

## 5. How we manage medicines

### 5.1 When a resident moves in

The shift leader checks the medicines that arrive against the discharge letter or GP's list, records them on a new MAR, and resolves any difference with the GP or pharmacist the same day. The resident's consent to the home managing their medicines is recorded; if they may lack capacity to decide, a capacity assessment and, where needed, a best interests decision are recorded.

### 5.2 Self-administration

Residents are supported to look after their own medicines where they want to and it is safe. The risk assessment is done with the resident, agreed by the registered manager and reviewed with the care plan. Their medicines are kept in a lockable cupboard or drawer in their room, and the resident has the key.

### 5.3 Ordering and receiving

Medicines are ordered on a regular cycle agreed with the GP practice and pharmacy, after a trained member of staff checks what is needed. Deliveries are checked against the order and the MAR, and discrepancies are raised with the pharmacy at once. Changes are added to the MAR and passed on at handover.

### 5.4 Storage

- Medicines are kept in a locked trolley secured to the wall, or a locked cupboard or room used only for medicines.
- Medicines needing refrigeration are kept in a lockable medicines fridge between 2°C and 8°C. Fridge and room temperatures are recorded daily, and readings out of range reported to the pharmacist.
- Each resident's medicines are kept separately in their original containers, and are dated when opened where the label gives a time limit.
- The member of staff giving medicines holds the keys, and hands them over in person at the end of the shift.

### 5.5 Giving medicines

- Check the resident's identity using their MAR photograph, and check the medicine, dose, time, route and expiry date against the MAR and the label.
- Ask for the resident's consent, and give the medicine in the way their care plan says.
- Stay until it has been taken, then sign the MAR. Never sign in advance, or for a dose you did not see taken.
- If anything does not match — a new box, a different tablet, a changed dose — do not give it. Tell the shift leader, who will check with the pharmacist or GP.

A handwritten MAR entry is made by a trained member of staff from the prescription or label, and checked and signed by a second.

### 5.6 "When required" medicines

Medicines prescribed "when required", such as painkillers or laxatives, each have a protocol with the MAR: what it is for, the dose, the minimum time between doses, the most that can be given in 24 hours, and when to seek advice. Record the time, dose and reason, and whether it helped. A "when required" medicine for agitation is given only after the other steps in the resident's plan have been tried (see Restraint and Restrictive Practice).

### 5.7 Homely remedies

Homely remedies are medicines the home buys for minor ailments, such as paracetamol. They are given only under a written protocol agreed with the GP or pharmacist, listing each remedy, its use, the dose, and how long it can be given before the GP is asked. Staff check it is safe with the resident's other medicines, and record each dose on the MAR.

### 5.8 Refusals and time-sensitive medicines

A resident with capacity may refuse a medicine. Do not pressure them. Record the refusal and tell the shift leader, who tells the GP if refusals continue. Medicines that must be given at set times, such as for Parkinson's disease, are given then, not on the medicines round.

### 5.9 Controlled drugs

- Controlled drugs the safe custody rules cover are kept in a controlled drugs cupboard that meets them.
- Every receipt, dose and return is recorded in a controlled drugs register, as NICE SC1 recommends, with the balance remaining.
- Two members of staff, at least one of them trained and assessed as competent, check each dose, watch it being given, and sign the register.
- Two members of staff check the stock against the register every week, and report any difference to the registered manager at once. One that cannot be explained is reported to the NHS controlled drugs accountable officer for the area, and to the police where theft is suspected, and is treated as a safeguarding concern.

## 6. When something goes wrong

### 6.1 Errors and near misses

If you make or find an error — a wrong medicine, dose, time or resident, a missed dose, or a recording mistake:

- check the resident is safe, and call 999 if they are unwell;
- tell the shift leader, who gets advice from the GP, pharmacist or NHS 111;
- record it through the site's concerns process the same day, as set out in Accidents, Incidents and Near Misses.

The registered manager decides whether it is a safeguarding concern, whether CQC must be notified (see Reportable Incidents), and whether the duty of candour applies. The member of staff's competency is reassessed before they give medicines again.

### 6.2 Side effects

If you think a medicine is causing a side effect, tell the shift leader, who tells the GP or pharmacist. The registered manager makes sure it is reported to the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) through the Yellow Card scheme.

## 7. Disposal

Unwanted, stopped or out-of-date medicines are recorded in a returns book and returned to the pharmacy, or collected by a licensed waste contractor, as the home has arranged. Controlled drugs are returned as the pharmacy directs and recorded in the register. When a resident goes out or stays with family, their medicines go in their original containers with written instructions, and what was sent and returned is recorded. When a resident dies, their medicines are kept separately until the registered manager is satisfied the coroner will not need them.

## 8. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — leads on medicines, makes sure staff are trained and assessed, carries out a medicines audit every month, and reviews every error.
- **Shift leaders** — make sure medicines are given on their shift, and get advice when something is wrong.
- **Care staff who give medicines** — follow this policy and the MAR, record every dose, and report concerns.
- **GPs and pharmacists** — prescribe, dispense, advise and review. We ask for each resident's medicines to be reviewed at least every year.

## 9. Training

Care staff complete medicines training and are observed until assessed as competent before they give medicines alone. Competency is recorded on their file, refreshed every year, and reassessed after an error or when an audit shows a need. Agency staff do not give medicines unless they have been assessed as competent in this home. Training is recorded on the training matrix.

## 10. Records

MARs, the controlled drugs register, the returns book, temperature records, self-administration assessments and homely remedy protocols are kept securely in the home, and audits by the registered manager, for the periods set in Record Keeping and Retention.
