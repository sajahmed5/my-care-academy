## 1. Purpose

Residents need help to move many times a day and night: getting up, washing, using the toilet, sitting in the lounge, going to bed. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} assesses each resident's moving and handling, makes sure the home's equipment is safe and examined, staffs every shift so that two-person moves can always be done safely, and helps residents keep as much independence as they can.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all care staff, senior carers, shift leaders and the registered manager, to activities and domestic staff who help residents move, and to every piece of moving and handling equipment in the home.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 — our duty to protect the health and safety of staff, residents and visitors.
- Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992 — avoid hazardous manual handling where we can, assess what cannot be avoided, and reduce the risk of injury.
- Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 — the duty to assess risks and act on what we find.
- Lifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations 1998 (LOLER) — the home's lifting equipment must be suitable, safe and thoroughly examined by a competent person at the intervals the regulations set; for equipment and accessories used to lift people, such as hoists, bath hoists and slings, that is at least every six months.
- Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998 — equipment must be suitable, maintained, and used only by people trained to use it.
- Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR) — certain injuries and dangerous occurrences must be reported to the Health and Safety Executive.
- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment, Regulation 15: Premises and equipment, and Regulation 18: Staffing.

## 4. Our policy

- No member of staff lifts a resident's whole weight by hand, except in a life-threatening emergency.
- Every resident has a moving and handling assessment on admission, and their care plan says how each move is done, with what equipment and how many staff, by day and by night.
- We help residents to do as much as they safely can for themselves.
- A move assessed as needing two staff is never done by one. Every shift, including nights, has enough trained staff on duty to do it.
- The home owns, examines and maintains its equipment, and we do not use anything damaged, unfamiliar or out of date for its examination.

## 5. Assessing moving and handling

A trained assessor completes the resident's moving and handling assessment on the day they arrive, using any assessment from hospital, the community therapy team or their previous home. Until it is done, staff follow the pre-admission information and ask the shift leader before helping the resident to move.

The assessment looks at the resident (weight, mobility, pain, skin, understanding, sight and hearing, and how they change through the day and night), each task, the room and the bathroom, and the equipment. It is reviewed every month as part of the care plan review, and straight away after a fall, an illness, a hospital stay, or whenever staff notice a change.

Where a resident's needs are complex or changing, the registered manager asks the GP to refer them to the community physiotherapist or occupational therapist for advice on technique and equipment.

## 6. Staffing for moving and handling

The registered manager sets staffing for each shift with the residents' moving and handling needs in mind, including the number of residents who need two staff at night. When the dependency of residents rises, staffing is reviewed. The shift leader allocates staff so that two trained people are free together when a two-person move is due, and does not leave one member of staff to manage a two-person move because the other is busy elsewhere. If the shift is short and safe moves cannot be done, the shift leader phones the registered manager or on-call manager straight away.

## 7. The home's equipment

The home keeps an equipment register listing every hoist, bath hoist, stand aid, sling, profiling bed, bed rail, slide sheet and other moving aid, with its location, owner and the dates of its last and next thorough examination and service.

- The registered manager arranges thorough examination of all lifting equipment and slings by a competent person, and servicing in line with the maker's instructions, and acts on any defect the examiner reports.
- Each resident who uses a hoist has their own slings, of the type and size their assessment specifies, labelled with their name and not used for anyone else.
- Slings are washed as the maker directs and checked after laundering.
- Bed rails are used only after a risk assessment of the resident, the bed and the rails together.
- Hoist batteries are charged as the maker directs, and spare equipment is available if one breaks.

### 7.1 Before each use

- Check the equipment for damage: frayed or torn fabric, worn loops or stitching, cracks, loose parts, a flat battery.
- Check the examination label is in date. If it is not, do not use the equipment and tell the shift leader.
- Check the sling is the resident's own, the right size, and clean.
- Check brakes, bed height and space before you start.

### 7.2 Faulty equipment

Take faulty or out-of-date equipment out of use straight away, label it, and tell the shift leader, who records it and makes sure each resident affected can still be moved safely. The registered manager arranges the repair or replacement without delay.

## 8. What to do

- Read the resident's moving and handling plan before you help them. If you are not sure, ask the shift leader.
- Explain what you are doing and ask the resident's consent. Let them do what they can, at their own pace.
- For a two-person move, wait for a colleague. Agree who leads and use clear, agreed commands.
- Stop if the resident is in pain, frightened or unwell, or if anything changes.

### 8.1 If a resident falls

Do not lift a resident from the floor by hand. Call for the shift leader, who checks the resident for injury before anyone moves them. If they are hurt, unwell, have hit their head or you are in any doubt, call 999 or NHS 111 as the shift leader decides. If they are not injured, use the home's equipment for getting people up from the floor, operated by staff trained to use it. See our Falls Prevention and Management policy.

### 8.2 Injuries and near misses

Report any injury to a resident or member of staff, and any near miss, to the shift leader before the end of the shift. It is recorded through the site's concerns process as an incident, the resident's assessment is reviewed, and the registered manager decides whether it must be reported under RIDDOR or to the Care Quality Commission.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — makes sure every resident is assessed, sets safe staffing, keeps the equipment register, arranges thorough examinations and servicing, and acts on incidents.
- **Moving and handling assessors** — complete and review assessments, write clear plans, and show staff how each resident is moved.
- **Shift leaders** — allocate staff so that two-person moves can be done safely, check equipment problems are acted on, and escalate when a shift is short.
- **Care staff** — follow each resident's plan, check equipment before use, never attempt a two-person move alone, and report changes, faults and incidents.

## 10. Training

All care staff complete practical moving and handling training, including the home's hoists, stand aids, bath hoist and equipment for getting people up from the floor, during induction and before they help any resident to move, and refresh it every year. Competency is observed on shift and recorded on the person's file. Staff are shown any new equipment before using it. Assessors complete further training before they assess.

## 11. Records

Moving and handling assessments and plans are kept on the resident's care record. The equipment register, thorough examination reports and service records are kept for the life of the equipment and as long as the regulations require after. Incidents are kept through the concerns process, and training on the training matrix.
