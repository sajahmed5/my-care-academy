## 1. Purpose

Welcome to {{organisation}}. The work you do matters: you help people live the way they want, safely and with dignity. This handbook is a short guide to working here — what we expect of you, what you can expect of us, and where to find the full policy on each subject.

## 2. Scope

It is for everyone who works for us, in homecare and in our care homes. Where the two differ, it says so.

## 3. The handbook and your contract

Your contract sets out your terms of employment: pay, hours, notice and holiday. This handbook is not part of your contract and does not change it; where they differ, your contract applies. Each subject is covered in full by the policy named, on the site. We may update the handbook and policies as the law or our practice changes.

The law behind it includes the Employment Rights Act 1996, the National Minimum Wage Act 1998, the Working Time Regulations 1998, the Pensions Act 2008 and the Equality Act 2010.

## 4. Starting with us

### 4.1 Induction

On your first day you are shown who's who, how to reach the office and on-call, and what to do in an emergency. On the site you complete training, read and accept the policies set for your role by the date given, and see your tasks. You complete required training, and shadow experienced colleagues, before you work alone. If you are new to care, you complete the Care Certificate, normally within 12 weeks. See Induction, Probation and the Care Certificate, and Training and Development.

### 4.2 Probation

Probation lasts six months unless your contract says otherwise, with reviews at one, three and six months. You will be told early if anything needs to improve.

## 5. Pay, pension and hours

### 5.1 Pay

You are paid at least the National Minimum Wage or National Living Wage for every hour of working time, at the rate in force at the time. Working time includes required training, meetings and supervision. In homecare it includes travel and waiting time between visits, but not the journey from home to your first visit or from your last visit home. Where you sleep in, any time you are woken to work is paid. You are paid [how often and on what date], by bank transfer, with a payslip showing your hours and any deductions. See Working Time and Rest Breaks.

### 5.2 Pension

If you meet the age and earnings conditions the law sets, we enrol you automatically in our workplace pension, [name of pension scheme], and both you and we contribute. Otherwise you can ask to join. You can opt out, but you would lose our contributions.

### 5.3 Hours and rest

You should not work more than an average of 48 hours a week unless you choose, in writing, to opt out. You are entitled to a break of at least 20 minutes when you work more than six hours, 11 hours' rest between working days, and 24 hours off each week or 48 hours each fortnight. Tell us about any other job, so we can keep your total hours safe.

## 6. Time off

### 6.1 Annual leave

Full-time employees have at least 5.6 weeks' paid leave a year, and part-time employees the same in proportion. Book leave from your own login, giving at least twice as much notice as the leave you want, and do not book travel until it is approved. See Annual Leave.

### 6.2 Sickness

If you cannot work, phone the office on {{office_phone}}, or on-call out of hours, as early as you can and within the notice Sickness Absence sets. Speak to a person. Do not come to work until 48 hours after your last bout of diarrhoea or vomiting. You self-certify for up to seven days and need a fit note after that. Every absence ends with a short return-to-work interview. Statutory Sick Pay is paid under the rules in force. See Sickness Absence.

### 6.3 Family and flexible working

Family leave includes maternity, paternity, adoption, shared parental, neonatal, bereavement and carer's leave, and time off for a dependant's emergency. Paternity leave, unpaid parental leave and the right to ask to work flexibly start on your first day. See Family Leave and Flexible Working.

## 7. Support and development

Everyone has a named supervisor, with supervision at least every three months and an appraisal every year. Care staff also have spot checks every 12 weeks and a competency assessment every year for each skill, such as medicines or hoisting. Use supervision to talk about how you are, not only about the work. See Supervision and Appraisal.

If you are struggling, at work or outside it, tell your manager or anyone you trust here. You can also call Samaritans free on 116 123 at any time. See Staff Wellbeing and Stress for the support we offer.

## 8. How we work

### 8.1 Conduct and boundaries

We follow the national Code of Conduct for Healthcare Support Workers and Adult Social Care Workers in England. Be on time, follow the care plan, ask before you act, and record honestly. Keep the relationship professional: do not give the people we support your personal number or connect with them on social media, lend or borrow money, or work privately for them without written agreement. See Code of Conduct, Professional Boundaries and Dress.

### 8.2 Dress

Wear a clean uniform each shift and your ID badge at all times; in homecare, show it at every visit. For personal care be bare below the elbows, with short nails and no varnish, long hair tied back, and only a plain band and small studs. Religious dress is welcome; we agree with you how it works safely.

### 8.3 Phones, social media and confidentiality

Keep your phone on silent during care, and make personal calls only between visits or on your break. Never photograph a person we support on your own phone, post anything that could identify them, or hold a phone while driving. Look only at the information you need, keep your login to yourself, and tell {{registered_manager}} straight away if information is lost or sent to the wrong person. See Social Media, Phones and Technology, Confidentiality, and Data Protection and Access to Records.

### 8.4 Gifts

You may accept only a small token gift, within the limit our policy sets, and must tell the office or the person in charge so it is recorded. Never accept money, vouchers, valuables or anything in a will. See Gifts, Gratuities and Legacies.

### 8.5 Alcohol and drugs

Never work, drive for work or be on call while affected by alcohol or drugs, and tell your manager in confidence if a medicine affects you. If you have a problem, tell us, and we will help. See Alcohol and Drugs.

## 9. Keeping people safe

### 9.1 Safeguarding

If someone is in immediate danger, call 999. If you think anyone is being abused or neglected, tell the office, on-call or the person in charge straight away, not at the end of your shift, and record it through the site's concerns process the same day. Never promise to keep it secret. See Safeguarding.

### 9.2 Whistleblowing

If you are worried about wrongdoing or poor practice, speak up. The law protects you, and you can also go to CQC, one of the bodies the law names. See Whistleblowing.

### 9.3 Health and safety

Follow risk assessments, use equipment and protective equipment as trained, and report every accident, incident and near miss through the site's concerns process the same day. In homecare, follow Lone Working and Driving at Work. In a care home, know the fire procedure for your shift. See Health and Safety and Accidents, Incidents and Near Misses.

## 10. Fairness at work

### 10.1 Equality and harassment

We treat everyone fairly, and do not tolerate bullying, harassment or discrimination from anyone, including the people we support and their families. We take all reasonable steps to prevent sexual harassment. Tell your manager, or {{registered_manager}}, if it happens to you. See Equality, Diversity and Human Rights, and Bullying and Harassment.

### 10.2 Grievance and disciplinary

If you have a problem at work, talk to your manager first; if that does not resolve it, raise a formal grievance, which we acknowledge within five working days. If there is a concern about your conduct or performance, we investigate first, tell you the concerns and hear your side. You can bring a colleague or trade union representative to formal meetings, and appeal any decision. See Grievance and Disciplinary.

## 11. Leaving

Give the notice your contract sets. Return your ID badge, uniform, keys and equipment, and never keep or share key safe or door codes. You are paid for leave you have built up but not taken, and you can ask for a copy of your training record.

## 12. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — gives every new starter this handbook and keeps it in line with our policies.
- **Managers and supervisors** — explain it at induction.
- **All staff** — read and accept it, and follow the policies it points to.
