## 1. Purpose

This policy sets out how {{organisation}} deals with concerns about an employee's conduct or capability fairly and consistently. Its aim is to help people improve where they can, to protect the people we support, and to make sure that any decision we take is reasonable and properly made.

## 2. Scope

It applies to all employees once they have finished their probation, and in its principles to employees on probation (see Induction, Probation and the Care Certificate). It does not apply to agency workers or self-employed contractors, whose arrangements are dealt with through their agency or contract.

It covers two kinds of case:

- Conduct — misconduct, such as breaking rules, poor timekeeping, or behaviour that falls short of our Code of Conduct.
- Capability — performance that is not good enough despite support, usually after a performance plan has not succeeded, or long-term or frequent absence (see Sickness Absence).

## 3. The law and guidance

- Employment Rights Act 1996 — the right not to be unfairly dismissed.
- Employment Relations Act 1999, section 10 — the right to be accompanied at a disciplinary hearing.
- Acas Code of Practice on Disciplinary and Grievance Procedures — the standard an employment tribunal judges us against. A tribunal can increase an award by up to 25% if an employer unreasonably fails to follow it.
- Equality Act 2010 — no one is treated less favourably because of a protected characteristic, and reasonable adjustments are made.
- Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006 — the duty to refer to the Disclosure and Barring Service.

## 4. Our policy

- Minor issues are dealt with informally first, through a conversation or a supervision, wherever that is appropriate.
- No action is taken until the facts have been investigated.
- The employee is told the allegations and given the evidence before any hearing, and has the chance to put their case.
- The employee may bring a companion — a work colleague or a trade union representative — to any hearing, and to an appeal.
- No one is dismissed for a first offence, except for gross misconduct.
- The employee may appeal against any formal outcome, to a manager not involved before.
- Timescales are kept, and any delay is explained.
- Cases are confidential, seen only by the managers who need to see them.

## 5. The procedure

The steps below are followed for every formal case. The HR pack on the site holds the case file, sends the letters, and shows the next deadline at each step.

### 5.1 The concern

A concern may come from a complaint, an incident, a safeguarding enquiry, a spot check, a supervision, or a manager's own observation. The manager decides whether it can be dealt with informally or needs formal action.

### 5.2 Suspension

Where the concern is serious — for example, a safeguarding allegation, or a risk to the people we support or to an investigation — the employee may be suspended on full pay. Suspension is a precaution, not a punishment and not a judgement. It is confirmed in writing with the reason, is kept as short as possible, and is reviewed at least every two weeks. The employee is given a named contact while suspended.

### 5.3 Investigation

A manager not otherwise involved investigates. They gather the facts: they speak to the employee and to witnesses, take signed statements, and collect records such as care notes, visit times and training records. The investigation ends with a short report recommending no case to answer, informal action, or a disciplinary hearing.

### 5.4 Invitation to a hearing

If there is a case to answer, the employee is invited in writing at least five working days before the hearing. The letter sets out the allegations, the possible outcomes, and the right to be accompanied, and includes the evidence. If the companion cannot attend, the employee may propose another time within five working days of the original date.

### 5.5 The hearing

The hearing is led by a manager, with someone to take notes. The allegations and evidence are explained; the employee responds, asks questions and may call witnesses. The manager adjourns to consider everything before deciding. The notes are shared with the employee, who may sign them as accurate.

### 5.6 Outcomes

The manager decides, and confirms in writing within five working days with the reasons and the right of appeal:

- No action.
- First written warning — stays active for 6 months.
- Final written warning — stays active for 12 months.
- Dismissal with notice — after a final written warning that is still active, or for capability where improvement has not been achieved.
- Summary dismissal, without notice — for gross misconduct only.

An expired warning stays on the employee's file but is no longer taken into account. A company may set longer periods for very serious cases, which the letter says.

### 5.7 Gross misconduct

Gross misconduct is conduct serious enough to destroy the trust between us and the employee. Examples are: abuse or neglect of a person we support; theft or fraud, including from a client or resident; falsifying care records or visit times; serious breach of confidentiality; working under the influence of alcohol or drugs; violence or harassment; serious breach of health and safety; and deliberately giving false information in an application. This list is not complete.

### 5.8 Appeal

The employee may appeal in writing within five working days of the outcome letter, saying why. The appeal is heard by a different, preferably more senior, manager, as soon as reasonably possible. The employee may be accompanied. The appeal decision is confirmed in writing and is final.

## 6. Referral to the Disclosure and Barring Service and others

If we dismiss someone, or would have dismissed them had they not left, because they harmed or posed a risk of harm to a child or an adult, we must refer them to the Disclosure and Barring Service. We also tell the relevant professional regulator where one applies, and the local authority where a safeguarding enquiry is involved.

## 7. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — decides how cases are handled, appoints investigators and hearing managers, and makes DBS referrals.
- **Managers** — investigate, hear cases and appeals impartially, and keep to the timescales.
- **Employees** — co-operate with investigations, attend meetings, and raise any reason for delay promptly.

## 8. Records

Case files, notes, letters and outcomes are kept confidentially in the HR pack. Warnings are recorded on the employee's file, with their expiry dates. Records are kept for six years after employment ends, unless a safeguarding concern requires longer.
