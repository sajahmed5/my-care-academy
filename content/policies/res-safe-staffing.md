## 1. Purpose

Residents need enough staff, with the right skills, on every shift, day and night. This policy sets out how {{organisation}} decides how many staff each shift needs, plans rotas, makes sure every shift has the right mix of skills and someone in charge, and uses agency staff safely.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who plans rotas, leads a shift or decides on admissions, and to all care staff and agency staff who work in the home.

## 3. The law and guidance

- Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, Regulation 18: Staffing — enough suitably qualified, competent, skilled and experienced staff, deployed to meet people's needs.
- Regulation 12: Safe care and treatment — care is provided safely by staff with the competence and skills to give it.
- Regulation 19: Fit and proper persons employed — everyone who works in the home, including through an agency, is checked and fit to do the work.
- Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 — the fire risk assessment and the evacuation plan must work with the staff on duty, including at night.
- Working Time Regulations 1998 — rest, breaks and night work.

CQC does not set staffing ratios. It expects us to work out the staffing our residents need, to be able to show how we did it, and to keep it under review.

## 4. Our policy

- Staffing is based on residents' assessed needs, not a fixed number.
- Every shift has a named shift leader, enough staff to meet residents' needs safely, and the skills the shift needs.
- Night staffing is enough to meet residents' needs at night and to carry out the evacuation plan.
- We admit a new resident only when we can meet their needs with the staff we have.
- Agency staff are used only when we cannot cover a shift ourselves, and are inducted before they work.

## 5. Working out staffing

### 5.1 Dependency

Each resident's needs are assessed on admission and reviewed every month, or sooner when they change, using a dependency tool. The assessment looks at help needed with moving, personal care, continence, eating and drinking, medicines, communication, behaviour, and care at night — including how many staff each task needs.

The registered manager uses the total to set the number of care staff needed on each shift, morning, afternoon and night, and records how the figures were reached. The tool is a guide, not the whole answer: the manager also considers the layout of the building, the number of floors, residents who need two staff or close observation, end of life care, and times of day when demand peaks, such as getting up and mealtimes.

### 5.2 Skill mix

Every shift has:

- a shift leader, competent to lead the shift and make decisions;
- at least one member of staff trained and assessed as competent to give medicines, on duty whenever medicines are due;
- at least one trained first aider;
- staff trained to use the hoists and equipment residents need;
- a balance of experienced and newer staff, so that no one new is left without support.

### 5.3 Night staffing

Night staffing is set by residents' needs at night and by the fire evacuation plan. There must be enough waking night staff to give the care residents need, to respond to call bells promptly, and to move residents to safety as the plan requires. Sleeping-in staff do not count towards the number needed for a night evacuation unless the fire risk assessment allows it. A manager is always on call overnight.

### 5.4 Other staff

Cooks, domestic, laundry and maintenance staff are planned separately. Care staff are not routinely taken off care to cover these roles. When they are, the shift's care staffing is checked first.

## 6. Planning rotas

Rotas are planned at least [number] weeks ahead, meet the staffing levels set, and give staff their breaks and rest. Staff are told of changes as early as possible. The shift leader checks at the start of each shift that it is staffed as planned and that breaks are covered, so that residents are never left without staff on a floor or unit.

## 7. When things change

### 7.1 Gaps in the rota

When a member of staff cannot work, the shift leader or on-call manager looks for cover from our own staff first, then bank staff, then an agency. If a shift is still short, the on-call manager decides what to do to keep residents safe — for example the manager or deputy working on the floor, or changing routines for the shift — and records it.

### 7.2 Admissions and changing needs

Before agreeing an admission, the registered manager checks that the new resident's needs, including at night, can be met by the staff on each shift. If a resident's needs increase — at the end of life, or during an illness or a period of distress — the manager reviews staffing straight away and adds staff where needed.

### 7.3 Monitoring

The registered manager reviews staffing at least every month, against call bell response times, falls and incidents at different times of day, residents' and families' feedback, staff feedback, use of agency staff, overtime and sickness. Where staffing is not safe, the manager acts, and tells the provider, commissioners and, where needed, CQC.

## 8. Agency staff

Before an agency worker works in the home:

- the agency confirms in writing that it has completed the checks we require — identity, right to work, an enhanced DBS check with the adult barred list, references and health — and that the worker is trained in the subjects our care staff must complete;
- the shift leader checks their identity on arrival.

At the start of their first shift they are given an induction, recorded and signed, covering:

- the fire procedure, the fire alarm, exits and the evacuation plan for their area;
- the residents they will support, their needs and any risks, and where care plans are kept;
- call bells, emergency equipment and first aid;
- how to raise a safeguarding concern and who is in charge;
- what they may and may not do — agency staff do not give medicines unless they have been assessed as competent in our home.

We ask for the same agency workers wherever possible, so that residents see familiar faces. We keep a record of every agency worker who works in the home, with the agency's confirmation of their checks and their induction, and raise any concern about them with the agency.

## 9. Roles and responsibilities

- **Registered manager** — sets staffing levels from residents' needs, records how they were reached, decides on admissions, and reviews staffing.
- **Shift leaders** — check staffing at the start of each shift, deploy staff, cover breaks, induct agency staff, and escalate gaps.
- **On-call manager** — decides how to cover gaps out of hours, and comes in when needed.
- **Care staff** — work the rota, tell us early if they cannot, and say when the shift is not safe.

## 10. Training

Shift leaders are trained in leading a shift, in the fire procedure and evacuation, and in this policy before they lead a shift.

## 11. Records

Dependency assessments, the staffing calculation, rotas as planned and as worked, agency confirmations and inductions, and staffing reviews are kept for inspection, for the period set in Record Keeping and Retention.
