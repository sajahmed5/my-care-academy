## The carer's documents page

Every hired carer has a documents page on their file: right to work, DBS,
references, car insurance and the rest, with expiry reminders. A Recruitment
sub-page inside it holds everything from their recruitment — application,
interview, test result, references, signed contract — when they were
recruited on the site. On hire, the checked documents move here with their
expiry dates, so reminders carry on.

## Disciplinary and grievance: the rules

- **ACAS.** Both follow the ACAS Code of Practice on Disciplinary and
  Grievance Procedures — what an employment tribunal judges against, and can
  raise an award by up to 25% for not following. A company can edit its
  letters and timescales, but can't remove the right to be accompanied or the
  right to appeal.
- **Who sees cases.** Managers only, by default. A manager can give named
  office workers access. A grievance about a manager is handled by someone
  else, and that manager can't see it.
- **The carer.** Raises a grievance from their own login, and can read the
  letters sent to them. Never the investigation notes or witness statements.
- **Letters.** Every letter is a template the company can edit, emailed to the
  carer and kept as a PDF, with the company's name and My Care Academy in the
  footer. AI can draft them (optional, 1.5 times cost); a manager always
  checks and sends.
- **Deadlines.** Each step has a timescale (notice before a hearing, the appeal
  window); the manager gets reminders, and a case shows its next deadline.
- **Minutes.** Hearings and meetings are typed in as they happen. The employee
  signs them as accurate, or the manager records that they refused.
- **Witness statements.** Typed in by the investigator and signed on the spot,
  or sent to the witness by link to write themselves — the investigator
  chooses.
- **One case file.** Everything in one place, downloadable as a single PDF if
  it goes to a tribunal.

## Disciplinary

One process, two types: **conduct** (misconduct) and **capability** (poor
performance — a performance plan that fails leads to a capability hearing).

1. **Concern raised** — from a complaint, incident, spot check or supervision,
   and linked to it; or on its own.
2. **Suspension**, if needed — recorded with its reason and a review date. On
   full pay; a precaution, not a punishment.
3. **Investigation** — an investigator named, witness statements, evidence
   uploaded, and a report ending in a recommendation: no case, informal, or a
   hearing.
4. **Hearing invite** — the allegations, the evidence, the date, and the right
   to bring a colleague or union rep.
5. **Hearing** — minutes typed in.
6. **Outcome** — no action, first written warning, final written warning, or
   dismissal; the letter gives the right to appeal.
7. **Appeal** — heard by a different manager; a final outcome letter.
8. **Afterwards** — warnings show on the carer's file while active: 6 months
   for a first written warning, 12 for a final, both editable. An expired
   warning stays on record but no longer shows as active.

## Grievance

1. Raised by the carer, in writing or from their own login.
2. Acknowledged by letter.
3. Meeting invite, with the right to bring someone; the meeting, with minutes.
4. Investigation, if needed.
5. Outcome letter — upheld, partly upheld or not upheld, with any actions.
6. Appeal.

## Probation reviews

- 6 months by default (the contract template's length), with reviews at 1, 3
  and 6 months. The company can change both.
- The review form asks about conduct and performance and fills in what the
  site already knows: induction and Care Certificate progress, training
  overdue, spot check and competency results, supervisions held.
- Outcome: **passed** (a confirmation letter), **extended** (a letter saying
  how long and why), or **not passed** (through the capability process, with
  an ending letter).
- Reviews show in Who's due, and the carer is told the date, as with
  supervisions.

*Built 31 Oct 2026: Employment on the carer's file (start date, job title,
reviews), Probation reviews under HR, Settings → HR → Probation and Letters,
and My HR for the carer (their letters and booked dates). What's agreed at a
review becomes actions on their file. Every HR letter goes through one
editor: the company's template, AI drafting if switched on, sent by a
person, kept as a PDF. Not passing gives the ending letter with the right
to appeal; the capability case itself comes with disciplinary.*

## Sickness absence and return to work

- The office records an absence: start and return dates, the reason as a
  category, self-certified up to 7 days or a fit note uploaded (with its end
  date) beyond that. Reporting sick from the carer's own login is a company
  switch, off by default — in homecare carers usually ring the on-call line.
- A **return-to-work interview** is due within 3 days of coming back, and
  shows in Who's due. It asks the care question: for sickness and diarrhoea,
  have 48 hours passed since the last symptoms?
- **Long-term sickness** (over 4 weeks): reminders for welfare meetings, and
  a record of any occupational health referral.
- **Triggers**: a Bradford Factor score, with trigger points the company sets.
  Reaching one flags it to the manager, who can open an absence capability
  case. Nothing happens automatically.

*Built 31 Oct 2026: HR → Absence (who's off, interviews and welfare
meetings due, triggers reached), each absence's page (fit note, the
interview, welfare meetings, occupational health), an Absence tab on the
file, Settings → HR → Absence, and reporting sick from My HR when switched
on (managers are emailed). Days are calendar days: home care works seven
days a week and there is no rota here. Pregnancy-related absence and
injuries at work never count towards a trigger; the office can leave out
others (a disability) with a reason. The capability case link comes with
disciplinary.*

## Holidays

Added 29 Oct 2026: not every company uses MyRoster to record holidays, so the
HR pack records them — for carers and office staff alike (everyone is a member
of staff; see "Staff logins" in PACKAGES.md).

- **A record of leave taken against an allowance.** The office sets each
  person's allowance in days or hours, and the hours by hand. No accrual
  sums: working out 12.07% of hours worked needs the hours, which MyRoster
  has. For a company that also uses MyRoster, how holidays pass between the
  two is part of the MyRoster review.
- The company sets when its leave year starts, and whether bank holidays come
  out of the allowance.
- **Requests** from the person's own login, or entered by the office on their
  behalf. Approved or declined with a reason, and the person is emailed.
- **Who approves.** Managers and office approve carers' leave; only managers
  approve office staff's leave, so nobody approves their own.
- **Clashes.** Approving shows who else is off that day. The company can set a
  "no more than X off at once" limit — a warning the manager can override,
  not a block.
- **Types.** Annual leave, unpaid, compassionate, maternity/paternity, and
  time off in lieu; a company can add its own. Sickness stays separate (above).
- Who's off today and this week on the Overview, and a holiday calendar.

*Built 31 Oct 2026: HR → Holidays (requests with who else is off, the
limit warning, today and this week, a month calendar, leave entered for
someone), a Holidays tab on the file (allowance per leave year, taken,
booked, left), My HR (balance, requests, taking one back), Who's off on
the Overview (leave and sickness), Settings → HR → Holidays. The amount is
suggested from weekdays and the bank holiday rule (England and Wales,
2026–2028) and can always be changed: only the rota knows.*

## Leavers

- The leaving reason (resigned, dismissed, end of probation, retired…), the
  notice date, the last working day, and whether they could be re-employed.
- A leaver checklist: ID badge, uniform, keys and equipment returned; **key
  safe codes changed** at the clients' homes they visited; final pay notified.
- Their login is switched off automatically on their last day.
- An optional exit interview, filled in by the carer on their login or by link.
- A PDF of their training record and certificates is emailed to them.
- **References given**: a new employer's request is recorded, and a factual
  reference letter (dates, role, reason for leaving) is produced from the file
  — AI can draft it at 1.5 times cost, and a manager checks it.

*Built 31 Oct 2026: HR → Leavers, each leaving record (the reason, dates,
re-employable, the checklist with whose key safes were changed, the exit
interview, the training record email, references given), a Leaving tab
on the file, the exit interview on My HR or by link (/exit), and the
daily job that switches logins off after the last day and emails the
training record with up to 25 certificates attached if it hasn't gone.
The site has no visits, so the key safe item asks the office to note the
homes. A manager sends references; the office can draft them.*

## Legal reminders

The site reminds the manager of a duty; the manager confirms it's done. The
site never files anything itself.

- **DBS barring referral** — when someone is dismissed (or would have been,
  had they not left) for harming or putting a vulnerable person at risk.
- **CQC and the local authority safeguarding team** — where a case needs
  them told.
- **When a safeguarding concern is raised** — the same reminders, in
  Complaints & safeguarding (Saj, 29 Oct 2026). To carry into that plan.
